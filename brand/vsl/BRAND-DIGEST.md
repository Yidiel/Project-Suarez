# VSL Brand Guide v6.0 R3: digest for the merch deck

Read 2026-10-26 from the unfinished brand guide (52 pages, several sections marked Work in Progress: Voice, Configurations, Accessibility, Color Combinations, Design System, Governance, Sub-Brands, Brand Kit). Yid's instruction: be loose on the merch design. The guide is the parent company, not the merch brand.

## Architecture
- **Visit Salt Lake** = organizational master brand (mark without the platform tag). Partner agreements, sponsorship credits, corporate.
- **America's Mountain City™** = the campaign platform lockup. Always title case, always with the ™, never shortened ("Mountain City"), never possessive, never ALL CAPS. "Never reconstruct the tag from type."
- The DMO uses "Salt Lake," not "Salt Lake City."
- Sub-brands section exists but is empty: the merch line can propose itself as the first sub-brand.

## Platform
- Opportunity: "Most can't tell you what Salt Lake is. That is also the opportunity." The only US city where a world-class urban core exists inside a mountain range.
- Positioning: "In Salt Lake, you don't have to choose between City or Mountains. You get both."
- Manifesto lines: "The skyline rises between the striking mountains. The grid leads into the canyons. The runway is ten minutes from downtown. Downtown is forty minutes from a chairlift." "We never accepted the trade-off, because we never had to."
- Three pillars, every piece must map to one:
  1. **Both at Once** (green). City and mountain, same place, no compromise. "Summit and a gourmet restaurant in the same afternoon." "Skiing at sunrise. Hockey at sundown." "A trail that ends two blocks from a sidewalk café."
  2. **Perpetual Momentum** (blue). Jazz, Mammoth, RSL, Royals; 2034 (only US city to host twice this century); Michelin on the horizon; festivals and art. "Already different than the last time you were here."
  3. **Frictionless** (orange). "Forty minutes from gate to gondola." "America's #1 rated airport, ten minutes from downtown." "Four world-class resorts within forty minutes." "Closer than you think."
- Brand guides: Show Don't Tell. Reach Our People (Families and Urban Explorers, but everyone).

## Voice
Four tones, one voice: **Confident** (declarative, "a refusal to qualify"), **Playful** (dry one-liner, the smile inside a contrast), **Friendly** (an experienced local), **Genuine** (no marketing polish, "no actors pretending to be locals"). Sample lines: "Forty minutes. Gate to slope. We timed it." "Trail at four. Rooftop at seven. Same adventure." "Trail at 4. Game at 7. Same day." "Mountain mornings. Downtown afternoons."

## Logo
- Salt Crystal Icon (an irregular crystal outline) + serif wordmark. Horizontal primary, stacked secondary. Outline is primary; filled crystal is a secondary mark "reserved for specific production contexts" (embroidery is exactly that context).
- Icon alone is a supporting asset, "not a standalone logo." For merch this is the rule to bend with intent: a crystal-only chenille patch is a sub-brand move, flag it as such.
- Misuse: no stretch, rotate, effects, busy backgrounds without overlay, no recreating from type.
- Co-branding: VSL mark leads, partner at ~80% visual weight, 1px line or × separator, never a three-logo lockup, always on brand colors.
- Minimums: horizontal 1.5 in wide print; stacked 0.5 in tall.

## Type
- **Saans** (modern grotesque; Light to Heavy) for body, subheads, eyebrows, CTAs. Eyebrow style: "40.7608° N 111.8910° W · ELEV, 4,219 FT" in Saans Medium, tracked caps.
- **Ivar** (Ivar Display web, Ivar Headline print; Regular, Italic, Bold) for titles, chapter heads, pull quotes in italic.
- System alternative: Arial. Fonts licensed to in-house and design partners only.

## Photography
- Natural light only. Lead with place. People living inside an experience, not posed. Natural, saturated, grounded color; no orange-and-teal, no moody desaturation. "Dress subjects as visitors, not locals... The subjects should look like they flew in for this."
- Test: "does this photo make someone want to book a flight?"
- Note for merch: the guide's casting is visitor-first. The merch deck will argue that the merch should dress the local, which is a deliberate departure to state out loud.

## Design system
- Layout = image / headline / colorblock / image / logo stacks. Topographic contour line pattern used as a texture on section dividers (that contour texture is a merch asset: all-over print, jacquard, laser-etch).
- Design accents: "general activities or Salt Lake statistics," not specific location names. Stats as graphics is on-brand (elevation, minutes, resort counts).

## Brand kit
Logos (SVG/PNG/EPS), Saans and Ivar families, 150 campaign photos (4096px), anthem film. Contact brand@visitsaltlake.com.

## What this means for the merch deck
1. The merch line is a **sub-brand** of VSL, not a campaign execution. Propose a name and a lockup that follows the co-branding rule (VSL leads) but lives one step away from the campaign.
2. Use "America's Mountain City™" sparingly and correctly, or not at all on garments. The city name, the coordinates, the elevation, the minutes, and the pillar lines are the better garment copy.
3. Steal the eyebrow system (coordinates, elevation, timings) as a label and hang-tag language.
4. Steal the contour texture and the color names.
5. The filled crystal is the embroidery mark. The outline crystal is the print mark.
6. Palette is strong and ownable. Salt Pink + Forest, Rodeo Red + Sego Lily, Mountain Navy + Aspen Gold are the garment colorways.
7. Photography for the merch lookbook should break the "flew in for this" rule on purpose: shoot locals, in the city, natural light, same color discipline.
