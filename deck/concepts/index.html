Streetwear

Salt Lake is for the weird

Lane thesis (CD approved): Salt Lake is for the weird. Salt Lake is for punk. Salt Lake is for the underground: punk rock shows, hardcore shows, counterculture. Merch logic is band-merch logic: back prints, tour-date lists, sleeve hits, xerox texture, one-line phrases, straight-edge X as hardware, SLC Punk! blue-black-white.

Palette used: Ink #212121, Cream #F6F1EC, Salt Pink #F58B88, Rodeo Red #A42F2C (alt), Mountain Navy #13375D (alt). One off-palette hit: Poster Blue #1A3BD4 on piece 03 (the SLC Punk! poster blue).

Fonts in the SVGs are stand-ins: Permanent Marker (hand lettering, to be redrawn by a letterer in Sharpie), Anton (condensed poster type), Special Elite (typewriter ticket text). All text is live vector text.

01 SALT LAKE ALL AGES (venue tour longsleeve)

File: 01-salt-lake-all-ages-longsleeve.svg

IN references: 29-flickr-flyer-a.jpg (marker-on-pink flyer, date column down the left, inverse-bar band names), 03-online-ceramics-blink182-dude-ranch-longsleeve.jpg (sleeve print down one arm plus a small chest hit), 04-online-ceramics-slaughterhouse-longsleeve.jpg (XXX stack on a sleeve), 30-flickr-flyer-b.jpg (one ink, alt colorway).

Idea: A band's tour-date longsleeve, except the band is the city and the dates are the rooms: eleven Salt Lake venues past and present listed down the back like a show flyer, year column on the left, venue name in hand-cut marker lettering, address underneath where a flyer would put "(New York)". The shirt body is the pink flyer paper; one black ink does everything.

Garment and fabric: 6.5 oz heavyweight longsleeve, garment pigment-dyed Salt Pink so the dye sits uneven like sun-faded paper. Longsleeve because the scene wears longsleeves to shows and because a sleeve hit needs an arm.

Technique: 1-color plastisol with a soft-hand additive, from hand-lettered art photocopied one generation before separation. No halftones; the copier does the texture. Placements: full back 12.5 x 17 in, left chest 4 x 2.5 in ("SALT LAKE PRESENTS:" in a hand-drawn box), wearer-left sleeve "SALT LAKE HARDCORE" 2.5 x 14 in, wearer-right cuff XXX stack 1.5 x 5 in.

What a local says: "Speedway, DV8, Lo-Fi... whoever made this actually went." Then they argue about which rooms are missing (that argument is the point; round 2 can add Burt's Tiki Lounge, Raunch Records, Diabolical Records, Quarters, Aces High).

Risk: Venue facts. Years and addresses are from background knowledge and all need a verify pass before print: The Speedway Cafe (late 80s, west side by the rail yards), DV8 (115 S West Temple, 90s), Bricks (579 W 200 S), Kilby Court (741 S Kilby Ct, since 1999), Club Vegas (445 S 400 W), Lo-Fi Cafe (165 S West Temple), The Urban Lounge (241 S 500 E; it is 21+, so either keep it and let the header lie, or swap it for The Depot/In the Venue), The Shred Shed (Exchange Place), Metro Music Hall (615 W 100 S, 2016), The Beehive (666 S State), The Underground (a DIY name; "ask a punk" is the address on purpose). Closed venues are fine to name; current venues may want a nod before VSL prints their names. "ALLE 10,-" is a wink at the German flyer reference; swap for "$5 AT THE DOOR" if it reads as a typo.

02 IT CAME FROM THE GREAT SALT LAKE (xerox monster tee)

File: 02-it-came-from-the-great-salt-lake-tee.svg

IN references: 05-brain-dead-sunn-o-tee.jpg (grainy one-color photocopy square on white, no cleanup), 30-flickr-flyer-b.jpg (cut-and-paste collage, one ink), 31-flickr-flyer-c.jpg (xerox photo under a heavy black type bar), 34-flickr-zine-a.jpg (zine black and white), plus the brine shrimp entry in the symbol bank.

Idea: A brine shrimp, the half-inch animal that is the lake's whole economy and is visible from space as cyst slicks, blown up on a copier until it is a 1950s creature-feature monster rising out of the lake toward a hardcore crowd with its hands up. Horror title bar "IT CAME FROM / THE GREAT SALT LAKE", ticket strip "ALL AGES * NO RE-ENTRY * ACTUAL SIZE 1/2 INCH", and a life-size shrimp on the upper back labeled ACTUAL SIZE so the joke lands when you walk away.

Garment and fabric: 7 oz heavyweight boxy tee in Cream, not optic white, so the print looks like it was copied onto paper that has been in a backpack. The Brain Dead Sunn O))) tee proves a single grainy square on a plain white body is enough.

Technique: 1-color water-based Ink through a coarse mesh, separation taken from a real photocopy of the collage so every speck survives. The SVG approximates the copier grain with a turbulence filter and draws the shrimp anatomically (segmented thorax, eleven pairs of feathery phyllopods, stalked eyes, male clasper antennae, forked furca) so it reads as the real animal, not a cartoon. Front 14 x 15.5 in, upper back 4 x 2 in.

What a local says: "Sea-Monkey from hell." Then they explain to the out-of-towner that the lake is pink and the shrimp are a $60-million-a-year industry, which is exactly the hangtag.

Risk: The title is a stock horror-film construction, not a scene phrase; if the CD wants it closer to straight edge, the alternate ticket strip is "DRUG FREE * ALL AGES * NO RE-ENTRY" with X's on the crowd's hands. Also the monster-shrimp joke is close to the Objects lane's scientific shrimp mascot; the two should not both ship in one round without a conversation.

03 NOTHING TO DO IN SALT LAKE (SLC Punk! palette hoodie)

File: 03-nothing-to-do-in-salt-lake-hoodie.svg

IN references: 36-slc-punk-poster.jpg (white condensed title on black bars over a flat blue field), 08-pleasures-freak-tee-glitter.jpg (phrase-first single hit, one line does all the work), 26-slc-punk-still-b.jpg (the blue hair, the color trio), 07-pleasures-suburbs-longsleeve-lookbook.jpg (small wordmark logic, heavy body).

Idea: A 14 oz black hoodie with one line of text in the SLC Punk! poster palette: "NOTHING TO DO" in white condensed type on black between thin white rules, then a flat blue field left completely empty, with "IN SALT LAKE" tucked at its bottom edge. The empty blue is the nothing. It is the sentence every kid who started a band here said first, and the city is in on the joke.

Garment and fabric: Cross-grain 14 oz cotton fleece hoodie, Ink body, hood lined in the same blue so the color trio shows with the hood down. A hoodie because the scene lives in hoodies from October to April and the inversion is cold.

Technique: 2-color plastisol, Cream and Poster Blue, blue as a flat flood with a slight dry-brush edge so it does not look like a corporate color block. Front panel 11.5 x 13 in above the pocket; upper back "ALL AGES" 4.5 x 1.5 in in one color.

What a local says: "Yeah, that's what we said at 16, and then we went to Kilby every night." They buy it for a friend who moved to LA.

Risk: A tourism office printing "nothing to do" is the whole point and also the whole risk; the client has to want the irony. If VSL blinks, the runner-up below ("SALT LAKE IS FOR THE WEIRD") drops into the same layout with no other change. Also check the film's title treatment is not reproduced: the SVG uses the palette and the bar structure, not the SLC Punk! logotype.

Phrase candidates (8), one-line, said by a Salt Lake punk about the city

1. NOTHING TO DO IN SALT LAKE (picked: the lie that started every band here) 2. SALT LAKE IS FOR THE WEIRD (the lane thesis; also a riff on the Alta "is for skiers" slogan locals know) 3. ALL AGES, ALWAYS (the scene's one rule, and the DMO's actual job) 4. SALT LAKE, NOT SALT LAKE CITY (how locals say it; the DMO agrees) 5. THE GRID ENDS AT THE MOUNTAINS (plain fact, and why you can walk out of town) 6. TOO WEIRD FOR UTAH, TOO UTAH FOR ANYWHERE ELSE (the local's self-diagnosis) 7. POSERS GO TO PARK CITY (true to the scene; a problem for a county DMO) 8. BORED IN A BEAUTIFUL PLACE (gentler, same idea as 1)

Performance

We run on Salt Lake

Lane thesis (CD-approved): "We run on Salt Lake. Salt Lake is performance." Performance as a belief system, material shown close, training alone more than race day. No race crowds, no bibs. Pillar: Both at Once (the city floor and the summit on the same garment).

Shared decisions across the three pieces

  • Type: Saans Medium, tracked caps, for every data line and eyebrow (shown in the SVGs as Inter with fallbacks; production uses the brand's Saans files). The one hard-copy line on the tee is set in a condensed grotesque in the 1990 Wieden+Kennedy register (shown as Oswald; production can use Saans Heavy condensed or Futura Extra Bold Condensed, which is what the 1990 ads used).
  • One number runs through the lane: 4,219 ft, the valley floor, lifted straight from the brand's eyebrow line "40.7608° N 111.8910° W · ELEV, 4,219 FT". It is the floor you start every run, ride and skin track from.
  • Reflective: a single 3M Scotchlite "SALT LAKE" at the back neck, 1.1 x 0.3 in, that only shows up in headlights. Never on the front.
  • Label mark: the salt-crystal line icon lives on the woven size tab at the hem or the sweatband, never in the artwork.
  • Palette: Mountain Navy, Cream, Rodeo Red, Sage, Ink. No off-palette hit was needed in this lane; reflective silver is a material, not a color.
  • Nothing can be bought at the airport: every blank is cut-and-sew for VSL, and the decoration on every piece is a construction technique (laser, bonded plate, knit-in) before it is a print.

01 Shoreline

File: 01-shoreline-perf-tee.svg

IN references: 24-satisfy-mothtech-logo-detail.jpg (laser perforation as the graphic), 20-bst-trail-runner-deseret-2025.jpg (the Bonneville bench above the grid), 17-nasa-lake-effect-slc-2001.jpg (data as image), 49-nike-1990-group-therapy-ad.jpg and 50-nike-1990-stress-management-ad.jpg (the hard copy line), 07-district-vision-lab-tights-film.jpg (how it should be shot: flat cold light, no gloss).

Idea: The three terraces of ice-age Lake Bonneville (Bonneville, Provo, Stansbury levels, the benches the city is built on and the Shoreline Trail runs along) are laser-perforated across the upper back as three dotted contour lines, dense at the terrace edge and thinning below it. There is no printed graphic on the back at all; the holes are the graphic, and they sit exactly where a vest or pack traps heat.

Why this garment and fabric: A training tee is the piece a Salt Lake runner wears 200 days a year, more than any jacket. 118 gsm recycled polyester micro-eyelet jersey because a CO2 laser heat-seals polyester edges as it cuts (cotton frays and needs a resin, which is why Satisfy's MothTech holes are small and the cotton reads "moth-eaten"; ours read "engineered"). Bonded hems and flatlock seams so nothing chafes under a vest.

Technique and why: Vector-driven laser perforation, back 11 x 7 in, chest 7 x 3 in, underarm gussets 2 x 3 in; 1.2 mm holes at 3.5 mm pitch at the terrace edge, opening to 8 mm pitch four rows down. One-color water-based screen print, Cream, left chest 2.4 x 1.3 in, for the hard copy line and its eyebrow. This is a decoration you cannot knock off on a heat press.

Hard copy line (chosen): 4,219 FEET / IS THE BOTTOM. with the eyebrow "BONNEVILLE SHORELINE · 4,900–5,200 FT". A plain statement about the place: the city sits at 4,219 ft and every run here starts by climbing out of it.

The other seven candidates, same W+K voice (declarative, talks to the runner, no slogan): 1. "The trail starts where the sidewalk stops. Two blocks." 2. "You can see your house from here. Keep going." 3. "Your office is 2,000 feet below you." 4. "903 inches of snow fell last winter. You ran in all of it." (Alta's 2022–23 record, verify before print) 5. "Nobody is watching at 5 a.m. That is the point." 6. "The lake left a bench at 5,200 feet. Run it." 7. "Lunch is a canyon."

What a local would say: "That's the BST. The holes are the benches." Then they would check whether the Provo level is in the right place relative to the Bonneville level (it is: lower and closer together, as on the hillside).

Risk: Laser perforation on a 118 gsm knit needs a tight tolerance on pitch or the back loses strength and ladders at the terrace edge; sample at three pitches before committing. Secondary risk: the pattern is subtle in flat light and the lookbook has to be shot with raking light or on a dark body for the holes to read.

02 Gain

File: 02-gain-alpha-pullover.svg

IN references: 21-polartec-alpha-direct-swatch.jpg (the fabric itself as the graphic), 36-nike-acg-lava-loft-official.jpg (one block of type over an honest, dirty technical garment), 28-nike-acg-fall-2026-trail.jpg (earth tones, trail-race register), 43-blackdiamond-lcc-home-crag.jpg and 54-wasatch-avalanche-danger-kutv.jpg (terrain as data, the vertical that is right there), 03-arcteryx-system-a-drop6-fieldmag.jpg (run-to-the-climb layering).

Idea: A raglan quarter-zip in Polartec Alpha Direct 90, the lofted grid shown exactly as it is, with nothing on it except an elevation data block on the left chest: the brand's coordinates, GRID 4,219 FT, SUMMIT 9,026 FT, GAIN +4,807 FT. The floor you live on, the peak in every office window (Mount Olympus), and the number between them, printed the way a race profile is printed on a bib.

Why this garment and fabric: Alpha Direct is the active-insulation fabric the serious Wasatch crowd already knows by name (Norrøna, Patagonia Nano-Air family, the skimo and dawn-patrol layer). It needs no liner, breathes while you climb, and is the honest "I know gear" fabric per the materials research. A pullover rather than a hoodie keeps it a mid-layer that goes under a shell on the skin track and over a tee on the Shoreline in March. 90 g/m² because that is the weight you can run uphill in.

Technique and why: Alpha Direct loft takes no ink and should not be needled (embroidery opens the grid), so the only decoration is a one-color Ink heat transfer onto a bonded Pertex Quantum Air 20D chest plate, 3.2 x 2.1 in, Cream. That plate is a real construction detail (it is where a bib pins, and it is the one wind-blocking patch on the garment) and it gives the soft garment its only hard edge. Lycra-bound hem and cuffs, YKK Vislon #3 zip with cord pull, 3M Scotchlite at the back neck.

What a local would say: "Olympus from the Pete's Rock side is 4,100, not 4,800" (they measure from the trailhead). The answer printed on the chest is deliberately from the grid, not the trailhead, because the point of this city is that you start downtown. Expect that argument; it is the right argument to have.

Risk: Alpha Direct snags and sheds, and Polartec restricts sale of some weights to vetted brands; VSL buys through a converter (Discovery Fabrics-type) at small MOQ, or partners with a Wasatch brand that already holds the mill relationship (Black Diamond, Kuhl). Also the Dawn red lot must be matched to Rodeo Red #A42F2C; approve a lab dip, do not accept the stock orange-red.

03 Valley Floor

File: 03-valley-floor-cap-sock.svg

IN references: 34-ciele-fstcap-elite.jpg (perforated panels, reflective trim, no hardware), 24-satisfy-mothtech-logo-detail.jpg (perforation as texture), 35-norda-blackdiamond-fkt-poles.jpg (the one-mark, all-function object), 40-tracksmith-amateur-spirit-cover.jpg and 42-tracksmith-falmouth-race-day.jpg (amateur devotion, the kit you wear every day rather than on race day), 52-porcupine-hill-climb-2016-mccutcheon.jpg (the canyon road climb starts from the valley floor).

Idea: A 5-panel running cap and a pair of merino crew socks that carry one quiet mark and nothing else: 4,219, the valley-floor elevation, tonal-embroidered low on the cap's side panel and knitted into the sock cuff. Perforated side panels on the cap and a reflective bound brim edge do the talking; the front panel is blank on purpose.

Why this garment and fabric: Hats are 80 percent of resort merch and Ciele built a brand on one cap; a running cap is the piece a visitor buys and a local actually wears up Millcreek. 85 gsm 4-way stretch recycled ripstop so it packs in a vest pocket and dries on the run; laser-perforated 1.2 mm / 4 mm pitch side panels for the same reason the Ciele FSTCap has them. Socks: 58 percent 18.5-micron merino, 39 nylon, 3 elastane, 200-needle, because merino is the mountain-town fiber, it does not stink after a two-hour skin, and a knit-in numeral is a real knit decision, not a print.

Technique and why: Cap: tonal flat embroidery, 0.55 in high, one color Sand on Cream, side panel only (left-handed is fine; it reads in the mirror and from the side at a trailhead). 3M Scotchlite bound brim edge as the only reflective; Coolmax sweatband; elastic back, no buckle, no eyelets. Sock: jacquard knit-in numeral at the cuff, 0.75 in, with a single 0.4 in Sage arch band; Y-heel gore and hand-linked toe. Salt-crystal line icon on the woven sweatband label only. Sold as a set in a printed glassine sleeve, gear tier.

What a local would say: "It's the elevation on the airport sign." Then: "Where's the front logo?" There isn't one. That absence is the status signal for the person who skis 20 days a year on a lunch break.

Risk: A number alone can read as arbitrary to a visitor who does not know the city; the hang tag has to carry the one-line explanation ("4,219 ft. The valley floor. Everything here is uphill from it."), and the deck should show the cap next to the Gain fleece so the number reads as a system. Secondary: tonal embroidery on perforated ripstop needs a stabilizer backing or the panel puckers; sample it.

01. North Arm / South Arm

File: 01-north-arm-south-arm-case.svg

IN references: 47-district-vision-annapurna-eyewear-pouch (technical-fabric eyewear case, zip and woven label as the only detail), 21-nasa-iss-great-salt-lake (the two-tone lake, the straight causeway dividing it), 27-le-labo-mazarine-label (lab label with blanks filled at the counter), 41-jmm-artemis-lighter-case (a small carried case treated as the hero).

Idea: A goggle-and-glasses box case colorblocked exactly like the Great Salt Lake from the ISS: Salt Pink north arm, Sky south arm, and a 1/4 in cream binding tape running corner to corner as the Union Pacific causeway. The label is a Le Labo-style spec sheet with three blanks, Canyon / Date / For, so the shop fills in "Little Cottonwood, Jan 14" with a pen at the register and the case becomes a record of one day.

Why this object and fabric: It lives in the glovebox and the lift-line pocket, and locals carry sunglasses and goggles in the same car all winter, usually loose. 210D PU-coated ripstop over an 8 mm EVA core is the District Vision move, light and crushproof, and the ripstop grid takes flat color without sheen. A flat divider keeps goggles and glasses from scratching each other, with a lens cloth sewn to it.

Technique and why: Cut-and-sew colorblock, not print, so the split is structural and the causeway is a real binding tape, bartacked at both ends and continued around the gusset. Woven label with a pen-friendly cream face. One heat-transfer eyebrow on the back (coordinates and elevation, straight from the brand guide's eyebrow system), in cream on Sky.

What a local would say: "That's the lake from the plane. The pink half is the north arm, the railroad splits it." Then: "Canyon and date? Mine says Brighton, every day."

Risk: The pink/teal split needs to read as the lake, not as a generic colorblock. The label copy ("north arm pink, south arm blue, one causeway between") and the aerial on the hang tag carry that story; without them it is a nice pouch. Also the label blanks only work if retail actually fills them in; train the counter.

02. Field Recordings, Great Salt Lake

File: 02-field-recordings-lp.svg

IN references: 54-a24-primetime-vinyl (a brand with a record label, sleeve as object, field-recording LP), 31-spiral-jetty-uibel-aerial (the spiral as the label shape, the jetty's arm running off the edge), 21-nasa-iss-great-salt-lake (split-color pour), 23-loewe-craft-prize-2025-winner-aoki (credit the maker: the recordist is named on every pressing), 03-a24-houseplant-ashtray-matchbook (small, bundled paper goods, the postcard download card).

Idea: A 12-inch LP of ten real Salt Lake sounds, nothing composed: wind on the north arm, the Wildcat lift's sheave wheels, snow on a windshield on I-80, a cold start and a scraper in Sugar House, TRAX door chimes, avalanche control in Little Cottonwood, brine flies lifting off Antelope Island, wind through the Saltair pavilion, gulls and a train on the causeway, salt crust underfoot at the jetty. The label is the Spiral Jetty, printed counter-clockwise as built, and the disc is poured in two colors at the causeway angle.

Why this object and format: The record is the one you put on when the snow is coming down. Vinyl makes a sound archive into a carried, shelved, gifted object, and the ASMR-leaning listener (the CD's "ASMR vinyl") is a real audience. 180 g, reverse-board jacket, uncoated label: everything matte and tactile.

Technique and why: Split-color pour (pink over blue) so every disc is a slightly different lake, like the real one. Two-color litho on the jacket, Salt Pink flood with an Ink spiral, the jetty's arm running off the top edge the way it does in the aerial. One-color Ink label; Side B is the same spiral at 60 percent, the jetty at low water. The recordist's name is set on the back in the credits line, on every pressing, not a liner note.

What a local would say: "Track A2 is the Wildcat lift. I'd know that hum anywhere." And "B5 is what the jetty sounds like when the water's down and you can walk out to it."

Risk: Rights and taste. The Spiral Jetty is a Holt/Smithson Foundation work; using its outline as a label needs a conversation with the Foundation, and the credit line on the back is there for that reason. Field recordings on resort property (Alta) and transit property (UTA) need permission to record and release. If the Foundation says no, the label becomes a plain salt-crystal outline and the piece survives.

03. Artemia

File: 03-artemia-pouch.svg

IN references: 36-ald-leather-keychain-pouch (keys and a transit card, carried daily, one small mark on good leather), 50-brine-shrimp-dwr (Artemia franciscana, Utah's state crustacean, the mascot nobody has used), 06-parksproject-brass-french-press (daily-use object in honest brass), 23-loewe-craft-prize-2025-winner-aoki (the maker is the point: a local leatherworker, credited inside).

Idea: A 4.5 × 3 in zip pouch in full-grain veg-tan leather with an exterior card slot, so the transit card taps through the leather without opening it, and a brass swivel hook for keys. The only mark is a single-line brine shrimp, blind debossed on the front: eleven pairs of leaf legs, stalked eyes, brood sac, forked tail, drawn from the DWR photo. No logo, no words outside.

Why this object and material: Keys and the transit card are the two things every Salt Lake day starts with, and nobody has made them a nice object here. Veg-tan in Sego Lily (the palette's natural-leather color) starts pale and goes honey in a season, so the pouch records its owner's year. Unlined, 3.5 oz, painted edges: it should feel made, not finished.

Technique and why: Blind deboss with a brass die, 0.4 mm, no foil and no ink, because a shrimp in gold foil becomes a joke and a shrimp pressed into leather becomes a crest. The line weight is single and even so the die cuts clean; an illustrator redraws the SVG for the die. Inside, a 6 pt blind stamp with the care instruction ("Full grain. Made in Utah. Carry it daily.") and the maker's name.

What a local would say: "Is that a brine shrimp? Sea monkeys. That's the state crustacean, right?" Then it becomes the thing they explain to visitors, which is the whole job.

Risk: The shrimp has to be drawn with real confidence or it reads as a bug. The die art should go to an illustrator who draws natural history, not a logo designer, and it should be checked at the 2.75 in size on an actual deboss sample before the die is cut. Secondary risk: an exterior card slot adds a seam on the back; sample it so the card does not bow the leather.

01 Paintbrush Yoke Shirt

File: 01-paintbrush-yoke-shirt.svg

IN references: 03-ginew-western-snap-chambray.jpg (block, cuff hardware, contrast stitching), 11-fort-lonesome-chainstitch-machine.jpg (technique), 06-fort-lonesome-texas-suit.jpg (botanical chain stitch, taken to the quiet end), 23-albion-basin-indian-paintbrush.jpg (the motif), 24-wasatch-aspen-grove.jpg (Sage and green-gold stem palette).

Idea: A plain Mountain Navy snap shirt whose only decoration is one small chain-stitched cluster of Indian paintbrush and lupine on the back yoke, the two flowers that actually grow in Albion Basin in July. Inside the left cuff, a hidden chain-stitch script reads "Albion Basin, 9,500 ft", seen only when the sleeve is rolled.

Garment and fabric: A western snap shirt is the lane's native object (Ginew, Fort Lonesome both start here). 7 oz brushed cotton twill in Mountain Navy gives the thread something to sit on and reads as fall and winter in the city; the 5.5 oz chambray alt is the summer version. Rodeo Red twill is the second colorway, with the paintbrush shifting to Sego Lily and Aspen Gold so the flower still pops.

Technique: Chain stitch, five thread colors (Rodeo Red, Terracotta, Sky, Light Sky, Sage), 4.5 x 3.5 in, centered on the back yoke, 1.25 in below the collar seam. Chain stitch because the loop texture is the whole point of the lane (ref 11): it looks hand-run because it is, and it ages better than flat satin embroidery. The cuff script is 5 x 0.6 in in Sego Lily on the cuff facing. Front is completely clean.

What a local would say: "That's paintbrush. You've been up Little Cottonwood in July." Then they roll the cuff and find the elevation.

Risk: Chain stitch on a run of any size means a contract shop with 114w103 machines (Fort Lonesome-type shops are backlogged for months and priced like art). Budget a domestic chain-stitch house or accept a 100-unit first drop at a hero price.

02 · Vecino

File: 02-vecino-fine-line-tee.svg

IN references: 01-bxr-hyna-tee.jpg, 25-mister-cartoon-by-oriol-1994.jpg, 05-koi-this-is-for-our-family-tee.jpg, 13-estevan-oriol-lamag.jpg, 26-cdmx-rotulos-zocalo.jpg.

Idea. One word, set huge across the chest in a single-weight fine-line script, no explanation, the Born x Raised HYNA move with the Mister Cartoon hand. The word is Vecino, neighbor: what the West Side calls itself, what Comunidades Unidas and Centro Civico say in their own copy, and a plain statement a visitor can wear honestly only if they act like one.

Why Vecino and not the others. Paisa is Paisa Boys' brand name and we are already borrowing their logic. Raza carries political weight VSL has no standing to use. Hyna is Born x Raised's. Familia and Barrio are on a thousand tees already. Oeste is the right place but the community says "West Side" in English. Vecino belongs equally to Mexicans, Venezuelans, Salvadorans and Peruvians, nobody owns it, and it means something in the mouth of a tourism office without being a tagline. The women's cut carries VECINA, same drawing, one letter changed by the same hand.

Garment and fabric. 7.5 oz ringspun cotton, boxy, drop shoulder, garment-dyed black and enzyme-washed so the surface is soft enough for a hairline. The BxR tee is black with white ink for a reason: the line needs the contrast.

Technique. One-color white water-based screen print, 13 x 5 in, 230 mesh, one pass. A letterer redraws it: Pinyon Script is a placeholder for a hand like Cartoon's, single-weight 0.5 pt line, script capitals, the tail of the last letter becoming the underline. Ink on vellum, scanned, vectorized. Back neck: 801 in the same hand at 1.25 in. Hem: "Diseñado en Salt Lake County," plain.

What a local would say. "That's it? Yeah, that's it."

Risk. Fine-line script is unforgiving in production: if the mesh or the ink is wrong the line fills and the tee looks cheap. Strike-offs on the actual blank before any run, and keep the letterer in the approval chain.
