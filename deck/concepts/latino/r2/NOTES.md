# Latino lane, concept round 2

All three R1 pieces were marked IN. R2 pushes each toward a finished thing: the jersey is a full kit, Vecino is a drawn script rather than a font, and the lotería cards are illustrated cards shown as the back print and as a card set. The sheets are lookbook pages now: garment large, detail blown up, colorways as named swatches, a plain caption. No header line, no spec strip, no label-value blocks. The spec lives here.

**Who designs this (unchanged, and still the point).** These are a starting brief, not finished artwork. The line is designed by Latino designers from Salt Lake County (West Valley, Rose Park, Glendale first), with a letterer and an illustrator from the community redrawing everything in these files, and a revenue share written into the deal with a community org (Centro Civico Mexicano or Comunidades Unidas; the Kids of Immigrants x CHIRLA model, org on the hangtag, money going back). VSL is licensor and curator, not author. The test for every piece: would the community wear it to a Sunday car show.

Shared label on all three: interior neck label with the small salt-crystal line icon, "Diseñado en Salt Lake County," and the partner-org line. No VSL logo in the artwork. The eight-place hem list from R1 moved off the outside of the jersey (it read as a caption); it is now printed inside the back neck, under the label.

Fonts in the SVGs are stand-ins named by Google font (Pirata One, Anton, Playfair Display, Archivo Narrow). Vecino is no longer a font: the word is drawn as stroked paths so the weight can vary. The sheet background is Cream, not white, so the files sit together as pages.

Cost tiers below use research/merch/05-production-logistics-and-audience.md: lane one (decorated premium blank) lands at roughly $9 to $30 per finished unit at 24 to 72 piece minimums; lane two (cut-and-sew) at $25 to $90+ with MOQs of 50 to 300. A 50-unit test run sits at the expensive end of every range because screens, digitizing and pattern work amortize over 50 pieces, not 500.

## 01 · Club Deportivo Salt Lake jersey
File: `01-club-deportivo-salt-lake-jersey.svg`

**IN references:** 09-paisaboys-club-deportivo-tee.jpg, 10-paisaboys-doce-estrellas-jacket.jpg, 03-bxr-worldwide-mexico.jpg, 07-koi-nike-jersey.jpg, 35-sltrib-la-barra-real-2021.jpg, 42-ksl-latino-world-cup-utah-2026.jpg.

**What changed in R2.** It is a kit now, not a diagram. Front: v-neck collar and cuffs in Forest with a Cream keyline, Forest side panels, bird's-eye mesh drawn as texture, crest on the wearer's left chest, 801 block on the right, four twill stars down each sleeve. Back: SALT LAKE arched in blackletter with a Cream keyline over 801 in two-color tackle twill at full number height. The crest is blown up as the embroidered badge it will be: merrowed Cream edge, satin-stitch direction on the Forest field and the ridge, the brine shrimp redrawn segmented with a fringe of legs, stalked eyes, antennae and a forked tail, the lake as a Salt Pink band under the ridge. The sleeve star is shown at size as Forest twill with a Cream running stitch. The match-day alt is drawn small: Rodeo Red body, Mountain Navy trim, same crest, Cream number.

**Blank or maker.** Cut-and-sew, Los Angeles (Bomme, Lefty or Indie Source tier), from a tech pack. There is no stock blank that gives a drop-shoulder v-neck mesh jersey in Salt Pink with Forest panels; the sublimated retail tier can use a stock sublimation jersey blank if the budget forces it, but the colors then come from the printer, not the dye house.

**Fabric.** 160 gsm recycled polyester bird's-eye mesh body, 220 gsm flat-knit polyester rib for collar and cuffs in Forest, Cream keyline knitted in. Sized up and boxy, worn loose over a tee.

**Technique.** Crest: 3.5 in diameter embroidered patch, roughly 18,000 stitches, merrowed edge, sewn on (not heat-sealed) so it can also be sold alone at $14 to $18 retail. Stars: 1 in tackle twill appliqué, Forest twill with a Cream running stitch, four per sleeve. Back name and number: 2-color tackle twill (Forest on Cream), SALT LAKE at 1.75 in cap height on a 10 in arc, 801 at 10 in number height. Retail tier: the same art sublimated, crest still a sewn patch.

**Placement and size.** Crest centered 3 in below the collar point on the wearer's left, 801 block 1.5 in cap height mirrored on the right. Stars from 1.5 in below the shoulder seam, 1.4 in apart on center, following the sleeve edge. Back arch top at 2.5 in below the collar; number baseline 4 in above hem. Eight place names printed inside the back neck, 0.25 in cap height, plain.

**Colorways.** Home: Salt Pink #F58B88 body, Forest #0D4C26 trim, panels and twill, Cream #F6F1EC keyline. Match-day alt: Rodeo Red #A42F2C body, Mountain Navy #13375D trim, Cream keyline and number, only with La Barra Real co-signing.

**Estimated unit cost, 50-unit test run.** Lane two. Cut-and-sew mesh jersey with custom rib at 50 pieces: $28 to $38 per unit for the garment, plus $9 to $12 for the patch, twill stars and twill number at that quantity, plus $4 to $6 in pattern, grading and sample fees amortized over 50. Call it $45 to $60 landed. Retail $110 to $130 at DTC multiples. The sublimated tier lands at $26 to $34 and retails $70 to $85.

**One thing to verify before sampling.** Clearance on 801 as a mark in apparel (Utah USPTO and state searches), and a sit-down with La Barra Real before the first sample, because the alt colorway lives or dies on their blessing.

**Who finishes it.** A Salt Lake County embroidery digitizer draws the crest for thread (the SVG is the layout, not the stitch file). A community letterer redraws SALT LAKE in a blackletter that is theirs, not Pirata One. The eight-place list and count stay with the community designers; the Salt Lake City-only six-star version is the fallback.

**What a local would say.** "Which club is that?" "Nosotros."

**Risk.** Same as R1 and still real: a supporters' kit for a club that does not exist can read as a knockoff to RSL and the barra. The upside of that risk is a co-signed version for the barra, which is why they are in the room before sample one.

## 02 · Vecino
File: `02-vecino-fine-line-tee.svg`

**IN references:** 01-bxr-hyna-tee.jpg, 25-mister-cartoon-by-oriol-1994.jpg, 05-koi-this-is-for-our-family-tee.jpg, 13-estevan-oriol-lamag.jpg, 26-cdmx-rotulos-zocalo.jpg.

**What changed in R2.** The word is drawn, not typeset. Vecino is a set of stroked paths in one continuous line: hairline on the up strokes, pressure on the down strokes (the V stem, the i, the n stems, the bowl of the o), an entry flourish on the V, a dotted i, and the tail of the o swinging up and over, then back under the whole word as the underline, thinning to nothing at the left. A light displacement filter roughs the edge the way a liner on cotton does. VECINA is on the sheet on the women's cut: same drawing, the o replaced by an a with a heavy stem, same tail. 801 at the back neck is drawn in the same line. The lettering is shown at print size, thirteen inches, on a black field.

**Blank or maker.** Los Angeles Apparel 1801GD (6.5 oz, garment-dyed, made in Los Angeles) for the hero tier; Shaka Wear SHMHSS Max Heavyweight (7.5 oz) if the drop needs to come in cheaper. The women's cut is the same blank in a cropped boxy fit, not a slim "ladies" tee.

**Fabric.** 6.5 to 7.5 oz ringspun cotton, garment-dyed Ink and enzyme-washed so the surface is soft enough for a hairline. The BxR tee is black with white ink because the line needs the contrast.

**Technique.** One-color water-based white screen print, 230 mesh, one pass, no underbase. The letterer draws the final in ink on vellum, scanned at 1200 dpi, vectorized without smoothing so the hand stays. Strike-off on the actual blank, approved by the letterer, before the run. If the line fills, go up a mesh, not down a line weight.

**Placement and size.** Front, 13 x 5 in, centered, top of the V flourish 3 in below the collar seam. Back neck, 801 at 1.25 in wide, 1 in below the collar. Hem: nothing on the outside. "Diseñado en Salt Lake County" and the partner line are on the interior label only.

**Colorways.** Ink #212121 body, white ink. Second run: Forest #0D4C26 body, Cream #F6F1EC ink. Vecina: Mountain Navy #13375D body, Salt Pink #F58B88 ink.

**Estimated unit cost, 50-unit test run.** Lane one. LAA 1801GD at $9 to $12 plus a one-color print at 50 pieces ($4 to $6 with the screen amortized) plus a woven label and relabel ($1.50 to $2): $15 to $20 landed. On Shaka, $10 to $14. Retail $48 to $55. This is the cheapest piece in the lane to test and should go first.

**One thing to verify before sampling.** That the letterer's thinnest stroke survives at 230 mesh on garment-dyed cotton: print the hairline test strip (0.5, 0.75, 1.0 pt) on the actual blank before the word is finalized, and let the result set the minimum weight in the drawing.

**Who finishes it.** A Salt Lake County letterer with a fine-line hand. The paths in the SVG set the composition (flourish height, tail length, where the weight sits); the letterer owns every curve.

**What a local would say.** "That's it? Yeah, that's it."

**Risk.** Fine-line script is unforgiving in production; a filled-in line looks cheap on a $50 shirt. The strike-off and the letterer in the approval chain are the whole mitigation.

## 03 · Lotería Salt Lake
File: `03-loteria-salt-lake-tee.svg`

**IN references:** 47-loteria-boards.jpg, 26-cdmx-rotulos-zocalo.jpg, 15-ksl-hispanic-heritage-utah.jpg, 19-sltrib-lowrider-urban-arts-2019.jpg, 20-ksl-utah-lowrider-community.jpg, 41-deseret-jaripeo-days-of-47-2019.jpg.

**What changed in R2.** The six cards are illustrated cards now, 2:3 in Don Clemente proportions, with a big serif numeral on a plate top left and the name in a lotería serif on the foot rule. Each image is a scene with line art, flat spot fills, hatching for shade, and the fills deliberately pushed 1 to 2 px off the line so the registration is imperfect, the way the real cards are. El Trax is a red TRAX car under its pantograph with the Wasatch behind. La Montaña is the ridge with snow on the peaks, a sun, foothills and the valley floor. El Taco is three al pastor on a plate under the trompo, with lime and radish. La Sal is a halite cluster on a crust with the pink north arm behind it. El Camarón is the brine shrimp drawn like the DWR drawing: segmented body, eleven pairs of leaf-shaped legs, stalked eyes, antennae, forked tail. La Troca is a single cab laid low on chrome wires under a Terracotta ridge. The sheet shows the board on the back of the tee, El Camarón on the chest, the cards as a fanned set, and three of them at size with card-stock grain.

**Blank or maker.** Los Angeles Apparel 1801GD in Ink, XS to 4XL, because car-club sizing runs big. Shaka SHMHSS as the cheaper fallback. Card set: a Salt Lake offset printer on 14 pt uncoated stock, boxed, with a printed game sheet.

**Fabric.** 6.5 oz ringspun cotton, garment-dyed Ink. Black makes the card palette pop the way the papel picado backdrop does on the Viva La Bonita shot.

**Technique.** Back: spot screen print, 11 colors plus a Cream underbase (Light Sky, Salt Pink, Light Pink, Sego Lily, Sage, Meadow, Forest, Aspen Gold, Terracotta, Rodeo Red, Mountain Navy, Ink line), flat, no halftones, so it feels like card stock and not a photo. Front: the single card, same separations. Wordmark under the board in the lotería serif. If the shop caps at eight heads, drop Light Pink, Sage and Forest into their neighbors; the illustrator decides which.

**Placement and size.** Back board 11 in wide x 10.8 in tall, top of board 3 in below the collar; LOTERÍA SALT LAKE at 0.4 in cap height, 0.75 in under the board. Front card 3.4 x 5.1 in on the wearer's left chest, top 3 in below the collar. Cards in the paper line at 2.4 x 3.6 in.

**Colorways.** Ink #212121 body, full brand card palette on a Cream underbase. No off-palette hit needed.

**Estimated unit cost, 50-unit test run.** Lane one, but the expensive end of it. Twelve screens for the back and the same films reused for the chest at $25 to $35 a screen is $300 to $420 amortized over 50, so $6 to $8 per shirt before the print itself; a 12-color back plus a 12-color chest at 50 pieces runs $12 to $16; blank $9 to $12; label $1.50 to $2. $28 to $38 landed. Retail $60 to $70. The card set, 50 boxed decks on a short offset run, lands at $9 to $14 each and retails $24 to $28. The honest note: the tee is cheaper per unit at 150 pieces than at 50, because the screens are the cost; if the test run is only 50, print the chest card as a single-color Cream line version and keep the full palette on the back.

**One thing to verify before sampling.** A wet-on-wet test of the twelve spot colors on the Cream underbase on garment-dyed black: whether Sego Lily and Light Pink hold their value over the underbase or need a second hit, because that decides whether the print feels like card stock or like a photo transfer.

**Who finishes it.** A Salt Lake County illustrator redraws all six in a lotería hand. The drawings in the SVG are blocking: composition, color placement, what is in each frame, the numbers. The misregistration and the hatching are kept on purpose. Second drop: El Lowrider, La Tejana, La Arepa.

**What a local would say.** "Where's El Lowrider?" (Second drop. Tell them.)

**Risk.** Lotería is the most-borrowed Mexican visual there is and "Lotería [City]" tees exist for a dozen cities. The numbers, the specific objects and a community illustrator's hand are what keep it off the airport shelf. If the illustrator is not from here, cut it.
