# Lane 3, Objects. Round 2 notes

What changed from R1: the eyebrows are gone. That means the sheet header, the tracked-caps captions, the coordinate and elevation strings, the spec strip, and the Le Labo fill-in label on the case (cut) and on the LP back ("Recorded by: ____"). Each sheet is now a lookbook page. The object is large, one detail is blown up in an inset, colorways are named swatches, and the captions are plain sentences. The spec lives here. Two pieces were approved (02 LP, 03 Artemia). The 01 goggle case was cut and is replaced by the CD's own example: a rack for skis or a board.

The fonts in the SVGs are stand-ins. Ivar Display and Saans are named first, with Georgia and Inter as fallbacks; swap to the licensed families for production. Hand-drawn character comes from SVG turbulence filters: rough on the jetty and the label spiral, grain on the sleeve and the leather. These filters drop out in some renderers, so judge the sheets in a browser.

Cost tiers are per finished unit at a 50-unit test run, using the $ scale from research/merch/05 (under $2 / $2–5 / $5–10 / over $10). All three objects land in $$$$ because none is a decorated blank. Each line gives a working estimate, marked (est.), to replace with a written quote. Section 05 has no hardgoods price table, so these are trade rule-of-thumb figures, not cited ones.

## 01. Two Arms (replaces the goggle case)
File: `01-two-arms-rack.svg`

**IN references:** 45-storeyourboard-vail-wood-ski-rack (a single oak bar on the wall, the gear hung as the display: the direct source), 50-brine-shrimp-dwr (the shrimp, the lane's one mascot), 06-parksproject-brass-french-press (an honest material object for daily use), 23-loewe-craft-prize-2025-winner-aoki (credit the maker: a Salt Lake woodshop).

**Idea:** A 30-inch solid white oak rail with two felt-topped arms. It holds one snowboard laid across, or one pair of skis standing tails-down, bases to the wall. The only decoration is the brine shrimp burned into the right end of the rail with the same brass die that debosses the Artemia pouch, so the two objects read as one family.

**Why this object and material:** Everyone here who rides has a board leaning in a hallway. The rack is the thing you screw into the wall of your first apartment after your first season, and it shows where you live without saying so. White oak is hard, stable, and takes a brand cleanly. The arms tilt 6 degrees with a lip at the end so nothing rolls off, and the wool felt protects topsheets and edges.

**Spec**
- Maker: a Salt Lake furniture or woodshop (CNC plus hand finish). The alternative is a powder-coat fabricator for the steel version.
- Material: rail in white oak, 30 × 3.5 × 1.25 in. Two arms, 6 × 1.5 × 1.5 in, mortised and glued into the rail, tilted 6°, with a 1 in lip. Hardwax oil finish (Rubio or Osmo).
- Pads: 3 mm wool felt in Forest, glued to the top of each arm.
- Decoration: brand burned in at 2.75 in wide, right end of the rail, centered on its height. Heated brass die shared with the pouch, so it is one tooling cost for two products.
- Mounting: two 5/16 lag bolts into studs at 16 in centers, under oak plugs. Hardware and a paper template come in the box.
- Colorways: White Oak / Forest felt (hero). Mountain Navy powder-coated 3/16 steel, with the shrimp laser-etched through the coat to bare metal. Sego Lily: soaped ash, the lightest and cheapest option.
- Packaging: kraft box with a printed sleeve. No fill-in label.
- **Cost tier at 50 units: $$$$.** Oak version about $45–75 a unit (est.) at a local shop, plus a one-time brass die of $150–300 (est.) that the pouch shares. Steel version about $35–55 a unit (est.), with a one-time $150–400 for the laser file and fixture (est.). Retail target $145–185 for oak.
- **Verify before sampling:** that the burned shrimp holds its eleven pairs of legs at 2.75 in on oak end-adjacent grain. If the legs fill in, enlarge the brand to 3.5 in or simplify the hatching for the wood die only.

**What a local would say:** "That's a sea monkey on my ski rack." Then they explain it to every guest.

**Risk:** A rack is a big, heavy SKU for a visitor center. It needs to sell online and at a few shops with local shipping, not from the airport. The 157 cm board shown takes almost the full width of a studio wall, so the sheet shows real scale on purpose.

## 02. Field Recordings, Great Salt Lake
File: `02-field-recordings-lp.svg`

**IN references:** 54-a24-primetime-vinyl (the sleeve as a real cover, the disc sliding out), 31-spiral-jetty-uibel-aerial (the cover illustration: playa, bank and dirt road, black basalt arm, spiral, pink water), 21-nasa-iss-great-salt-lake (the split pour), 32-great-salt-lake-salt-crystals-imaggeo (the crust pattern and the small hopper-crystal mark on the back), 23-loewe-craft-prize-2025-winner-aoki (the recordist named on every pressing).

**What R2 pushed:**
- The front is now an illustrated aerial of the jetty in the pink north arm, with the title set in Ivar like a record cover, not a spec. The recordist is credited on the front.
- The back is set like an LP back: a title, then large serif A and B side letters, numbered tracks with times, and a short credit block. There is no data table and no fill-in line.
- The label carries the jetty spiral, counter-clockwise as built, and is shown at full size in the inset.
- The disc is a split pour, pink over blue, with a soft irregular seam at the causeway angle.

**Recordist slot:** the sheet shows "[Recordist]" as a named placeholder, not a blank. Candidate approach: commission one Utah field recordist or sound artist (for example, from the U of U or Westminster sound-art programs, or a local ambient label) and give them the front credit. Their name prints. Nobody fills it in by hand.

**Spec**
- Pressing: a 12 in LP, 180 g, 33 1/3, from a US plant that does split color (Furnace MFG, Precision, or Hand Drawn Pressing).
- Disc: split pour, Salt Pink over Sky. Ask the plant for its closest match to PMS 2339 C / 2390 C.
- Label: 100 mm uncoated, one color Ink. Side B is the same spiral at 60%.
- Jacket: 3 mm spine, reverse-board 24 pt, four-color litho (illustrated front) with a matte finish. A poly-lined inner. A 4 × 6 postcard download card.
- Placement: title lower left on the front, recordist lower right, catalog number top right. The credits block is on the back.
- Colorways: one pressing. The pour varies by nature, which is the point.
- **Cost tier at 50 units: $$$$.** Vinyl does not run at 50 economically. Plants quote 100 minimum and are cheapest at 300. At 100 units, a split-color 180 g run with a printed jacket lands around $14–22 a unit plus about $800–1,200 in lacquers, plates and test pressings (est.), which is $25–35 a unit all-in at 100 (est.). At 50 the per-unit figure roughly doubles. The honest test is 100 with 50 held back for gifting and press. Recording and mastering fees are extra. Retail target $40–48.
- **Verify before sampling:** written permission from the Holt/Smithson Foundation for the jetty image on the sleeve and label. Also location-recording releases from Alta (A2) and UTA (A5). If the Foundation declines, the cover becomes the salt-crust and pink-water field without the jetty, the label becomes the hopper crystal, and the piece still stands.

**What a local would say:** "A2 is the Wildcat lift. I'd know that hum anywhere."

**Risk:** The rights described above, and lead time. Plants run 10–16 weeks, so the record has to be booked for the season it describes.

## 03. Artemia
File: `03-artemia-pouch.svg`

**IN references:** 36-ald-leather-keychain-pouch (a small carried leather piece with brass hardware and one quiet mark), 50-brine-shrimp-dwr (the drawing's source: female, stalked eyes, eleven pairs of leaf legs, brood sac, forked tail), 06-parksproject-brass-french-press (brass, honest materials), 23-loewe-craft-prize-2025-winner-aoki (the maker's name stamped inside).

**What R2 pushed:**
- The die art is now a natural-history plate: stalked eyes, antennae, segmented thorax, eleven phyllopodia with gill lobes, veins and setae, far-side legs in a lighter weight for depth, a brood sac with eggs, a segmented abdomen, and a furca with setae.
- The pouch is shown in a hand at scale (1 in = 60 px), so its size reads instantly.
- The back shows the exterior card slot with a transit card in it.
- The R1 caption strip is gone.

**Spec**
- Maker: a Salt Lake leatherworker, credited by name in the inside stamp ("[Leatherworker]" is a placeholder for the real name, not a blank).
- Material: 3.5 oz full-grain veg-tan. Natural (Sego Lily, darkening to honey) is the hero; black veg-tan is the Ink colorway. Unlined, with painted edges.
- Size: 4.5 × 3 in. A #3 brass zip along the top. A brass swivel hook on a leather tab. An exterior card slot on the back.
- Decoration: blind deboss with a brass die, 2.75 in wide, centered on the front, about 0.4 mm deep. No foil and no ink. Inside, a 6 pt blind stamp reads "Full grain. Made in Utah. Carry it daily." with the maker's name.
- Colorways: Natural, Honey (the same hide at a year, shown for the story, not a separate SKU), Ink.
- **Cost tier at 50 units: $$$$.** About $28–45 a unit (est.) from a local maker: leather, YKK brass zip, solid brass hook, and labor. Add a one-time $150–300 for the brass die (est.), shared with the rack. Retail target $85–110.
- **Verify before sampling:** that the die holds the leg hatching at 2.75 in. Strike one test on scrap veg-tan. If the hatching closes up, the illustrator thins it for the leather die while the plate keeps full detail for print and the rack brand.

**What a local would say:** "Is that a brine shrimp? Sea monkeys. That's the state crustacean, right?"

**Risk:** The drawing. The SVG sets the anatomy and composition; a natural-history illustrator still has to redraw it by hand before the die is cut, or it reads as a bug instead of a crest.
