# Performance lane, concept round 1

Lane thesis (CD-approved): "We run on Salt Lake. Salt Lake is performance." Performance as a belief system, material shown close, training alone more than race day. No race crowds, no bibs. Pillar: Both at Once (the city floor and the summit on the same garment).

Shared decisions across the three pieces
- Type: Saans Medium, tracked caps, for every data line and eyebrow (shown in the SVGs as Inter with fallbacks; production uses the brand's Saans files). The one hard-copy line on the tee is set in a condensed grotesque in the 1990 Wieden+Kennedy register (shown as Oswald; production can use Saans Heavy condensed or Futura Extra Bold Condensed, which is what the 1990 ads used).
- One number runs through the lane: 4,219 ft, the valley floor, lifted straight from the brand's eyebrow line "40.7608° N 111.8910° W · ELEV, 4,219 FT". It is the floor you start every run, ride and skin track from.
- Reflective: a single 3M Scotchlite "SALT LAKE" at the back neck, 1.1 x 0.3 in, that only shows up in headlights. Never on the front.
- Label mark: the salt-crystal line icon lives on the woven size tab at the hem or the sweatband, never in the artwork.
- Palette: Mountain Navy, Cream, Rodeo Red, Sage, Ink. No off-palette hit was needed in this lane; reflective silver is a material, not a color.
- Nothing can be bought at the airport: every blank is cut-and-sew for VSL, and the decoration on every piece is a construction technique (laser, bonded plate, knit-in) before it is a print.

## 01 Shoreline
File: `01-shoreline-perf-tee.svg`

IN references: 24-satisfy-mothtech-logo-detail.jpg (laser perforation as the graphic), 20-bst-trail-runner-deseret-2025.jpg (the Bonneville bench above the grid), 17-nasa-lake-effect-slc-2001.jpg (data as image), 49-nike-1990-group-therapy-ad.jpg and 50-nike-1990-stress-management-ad.jpg (the hard copy line), 07-district-vision-lab-tights-film.jpg (how it should be shot: flat cold light, no gloss).

Idea: The three terraces of ice-age Lake Bonneville (Bonneville, Provo, Stansbury levels, the benches the city is built on and the Shoreline Trail runs along) are laser-perforated across the upper back as three dotted contour lines, dense at the terrace edge and thinning below it. There is no printed graphic on the back at all; the holes are the graphic, and they sit exactly where a vest or pack traps heat.

Why this garment and fabric: A training tee is the piece a Salt Lake runner wears 200 days a year, more than any jacket. 118 gsm recycled polyester micro-eyelet jersey because a CO2 laser heat-seals polyester edges as it cuts (cotton frays and needs a resin, which is why Satisfy's MothTech holes are small and the cotton reads "moth-eaten"; ours read "engineered"). Bonded hems and flatlock seams so nothing chafes under a vest.

Technique and why: Vector-driven laser perforation, back 11 x 7 in, chest 7 x 3 in, underarm gussets 2 x 3 in; 1.2 mm holes at 3.5 mm pitch at the terrace edge, opening to 8 mm pitch four rows down. One-color water-based screen print, Cream, left chest 2.4 x 1.3 in, for the hard copy line and its eyebrow. This is a decoration you cannot knock off on a heat press.

Hard copy line (chosen): **4,219 FEET / IS THE BOTTOM.** with the eyebrow "BONNEVILLE SHORELINE · 4,900–5,200 FT". A plain statement about the place: the city sits at 4,219 ft and every run here starts by climbing out of it.

The other seven candidates, same W+K voice (declarative, talks to the runner, no slogan):
1. "The trail starts where the sidewalk stops. Two blocks."
2. "You can see your house from here. Keep going."
3. "Your office is 2,000 feet below you."
4. "903 inches of snow fell last winter. You ran in all of it." (Alta's 2022–23 record, verify before print)
5. "Nobody is watching at 5 a.m. That is the point."
6. "The lake left a bench at 5,200 feet. Run it."
7. "Lunch is a canyon."

What a local would say: "That's the BST. The holes are the benches." Then they would check whether the Provo level is in the right place relative to the Bonneville level (it is: lower and closer together, as on the hillside).

Risk: Laser perforation on a 118 gsm knit needs a tight tolerance on pitch or the back loses strength and ladders at the terrace edge; sample at three pitches before committing. Secondary risk: the pattern is subtle in flat light and the lookbook has to be shot with raking light or on a dark body for the holes to read.

## 02 Gain
File: `02-gain-alpha-pullover.svg`

IN references: 21-polartec-alpha-direct-swatch.jpg (the fabric itself as the graphic), 36-nike-acg-lava-loft-official.jpg (one block of type over an honest, dirty technical garment), 28-nike-acg-fall-2026-trail.jpg (earth tones, trail-race register), 43-blackdiamond-lcc-home-crag.jpg and 54-wasatch-avalanche-danger-kutv.jpg (terrain as data, the vertical that is right there), 03-arcteryx-system-a-drop6-fieldmag.jpg (run-to-the-climb layering).

Idea: A raglan quarter-zip in Polartec Alpha Direct 90, the lofted grid shown exactly as it is, with nothing on it except an elevation data block on the left chest: the brand's coordinates, GRID 4,219 FT, SUMMIT 9,026 FT, GAIN +4,807 FT. The floor you live on, the peak in every office window (Mount Olympus), and the number between them, printed the way a race profile is printed on a bib.

Why this garment and fabric: Alpha Direct is the active-insulation fabric the serious Wasatch crowd already knows by name (Norrøna, Patagonia Nano-Air family, the skimo and dawn-patrol layer). It needs no liner, breathes while you climb, and is the honest "I know gear" fabric per the materials research. A pullover rather than a hoodie keeps it a mid-layer that goes under a shell on the skin track and over a tee on the Shoreline in March. 90 g/m² because that is the weight you can run uphill in.

Technique and why: Alpha Direct loft takes no ink and should not be needled (embroidery opens the grid), so the only decoration is a one-color Ink heat transfer onto a bonded Pertex Quantum Air 20D chest plate, 3.2 x 2.1 in, Cream. That plate is a real construction detail (it is where a bib pins, and it is the one wind-blocking patch on the garment) and it gives the soft garment its only hard edge. Lycra-bound hem and cuffs, YKK Vislon #3 zip with cord pull, 3M Scotchlite at the back neck.

What a local would say: "Olympus from the Pete's Rock side is 4,100, not 4,800" (they measure from the trailhead). The answer printed on the chest is deliberately from the grid, not the trailhead, because the point of this city is that you start downtown. Expect that argument; it is the right argument to have.

Risk: Alpha Direct snags and sheds, and Polartec restricts sale of some weights to vetted brands; VSL buys through a converter (Discovery Fabrics-type) at small MOQ, or partners with a Wasatch brand that already holds the mill relationship (Black Diamond, Kuhl). Also the Dawn red lot must be matched to Rodeo Red #A42F2C; approve a lab dip, do not accept the stock orange-red.

## 03 Valley Floor
File: `03-valley-floor-cap-sock.svg`

IN references: 34-ciele-fstcap-elite.jpg (perforated panels, reflective trim, no hardware), 24-satisfy-mothtech-logo-detail.jpg (perforation as texture), 35-norda-blackdiamond-fkt-poles.jpg (the one-mark, all-function object), 40-tracksmith-amateur-spirit-cover.jpg and 42-tracksmith-falmouth-race-day.jpg (amateur devotion, the kit you wear every day rather than on race day), 52-porcupine-hill-climb-2016-mccutcheon.jpg (the canyon road climb starts from the valley floor).

Idea: A 5-panel running cap and a pair of merino crew socks that carry one quiet mark and nothing else: 4,219, the valley-floor elevation, tonal-embroidered low on the cap's side panel and knitted into the sock cuff. Perforated side panels on the cap and a reflective bound brim edge do the talking; the front panel is blank on purpose.

Why this garment and fabric: Hats are 80 percent of resort merch and Ciele built a brand on one cap; a running cap is the piece a visitor buys and a local actually wears up Millcreek. 85 gsm 4-way stretch recycled ripstop so it packs in a vest pocket and dries on the run; laser-perforated 1.2 mm / 4 mm pitch side panels for the same reason the Ciele FSTCap has them. Socks: 58 percent 18.5-micron merino, 39 nylon, 3 elastane, 200-needle, because merino is the mountain-town fiber, it does not stink after a two-hour skin, and a knit-in numeral is a real knit decision, not a print.

Technique and why: Cap: tonal flat embroidery, 0.55 in high, one color Sand on Cream, side panel only (left-handed is fine; it reads in the mirror and from the side at a trailhead). 3M Scotchlite bound brim edge as the only reflective; Coolmax sweatband; elastic back, no buckle, no eyelets. Sock: jacquard knit-in numeral at the cuff, 0.75 in, with a single 0.4 in Sage arch band; Y-heel gore and hand-linked toe. Salt-crystal line icon on the woven sweatband label only. Sold as a set in a printed glassine sleeve, gear tier.

What a local would say: "It's the elevation on the airport sign." Then: "Where's the front logo?" There isn't one. That absence is the status signal for the person who skis 20 days a year on a lunch break.

Risk: A number alone can read as arbitrary to a visitor who does not know the city; the hang tag has to carry the one-line explanation ("4,219 ft. The valley floor. Everything here is uphill from it."), and the deck should show the cap next to the Gain fleece so the number reads as a system. Secondary: tonal embroidery on perforated ripstop needs a stabilizer backing or the panel puckers; sample it.
