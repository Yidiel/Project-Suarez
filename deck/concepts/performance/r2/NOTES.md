# Performance lane, concept round 2

Lane thesis (unchanged): "We run on Salt Lake. Salt Lake is performance." Training alone more than race day, material shown close, the construction is the decoration.

What changed from R1
- Every eyebrow is gone: no sheet header, no "FRONT / BACK" tracked caps, no spec strip, no coordinate line, no label-value block. The sheets are lookbook pages: garment large, one blown-up inset, swatches with plain names, a caption in sentences. Spec lives here.
- 01 Shoreline (cut) is replaced by 01 Five A.M., a technical long sleeve where one of the approved hard-copy lines is the entire garment.
- 02 Gain loses the elevation readout. The gain is now a graphic: one bonded strip running the full height of the body with 4,807 as the only type, read upward.
- 03 Valley Floor keeps the single 4,219 but sets it like a race number on the cap (big, bib-spaced, satin stitch, brim crops it) and as a jacquard knit-in up the back of the sock calf.
- Type: the condensed display face is shown as Oswald (Google) with Futura Extra Bold Condensed as the production face, which is what the 1990 Wieden+Kennedy inserts used. Sheet captions are Inter (Saans in production). Numerals on 02 and 03 are the same condensed face so the three pieces read as one system.
- Palette: Mountain Navy, Cream, Rodeo Red, Sage, Ink, Sego Lily, with one Forest and one Sky second-run option. Reflective silver is a material, not a colour.

Fonts to load for the SVGs: Oswald 700, Inter 400/600, Source Serif 4 400 (Google Fonts). Fallbacks are declared in every text element.

## 01 Five A.M.
File: `01-five-am-bonded-longsleeve.svg` (replaces 01 Shoreline; new slug, same slot)

IN references: 49-nike-1990-group-therapy-ad.jpg and 50-nike-1990-stress-management-ad.jpg (stacked condensed copy, talks straight to the runner), 48-wasatch-100-cody-priest-brighton-aid.jpg (headlamp, night, the work nobody sees), 32-bandit-coolgrid-singlet-flopack-detail.jpg (bonded hem, engineered mesh zones, one small mark), 07-district-vision-lab-tights-film.jpg (flat cold light for the shoot), 24-satisfy-mothtech-logo-detail.jpg (laser perforation kept, now only where it works: the gussets).

Idea: The approved line "Nobody is watching at 5 a.m. That is the point." is set stacked and huge across the chest of a bonded technical long sleeve, exactly like a 1990 W+K insert. It is printed in 3M Scotchlite reflective transfer, so by day it is a quiet grey on navy and in a headlamp or a car's lights it goes white. The garment literally says it only when nobody is watching.

Why this garment and fabric: A long sleeve is the dawn-patrol layer from October to April (tee under a vest is a summer thing). 118 gsm recycled polyester micro-eyelet jersey, bonded hem and cuffs, flatlock seams, laser-perforated underarm gussets (1.2 mm holes, 4 mm pitch, 2 x 3 in each) because the CO2 laser heat-seals polyester and this is the one place on a long sleeve that needs to dump heat under a pack strap.

Technique and why: 3M Scotchlite 8710 reflective heat-transfer film, one layer, no outline, no colour, centred chest, 9 x 11 in print area, five stacked lines in Futura Extra Bold Condensed caps, ragged-centred. Reflective film is the honest version of "performance": it is a safety material doing a design job. Second "SALT LAKE" Scotchlite hit at the back neck, 1.1 x 0.3 in. No front logo, no sleeve print. Salt-crystal icon on the woven size tab at the hem only.

Placement and size: chest block 9 in wide x 11 in tall, top of the block 2.5 in below the collar seam, centred on CF. Back neck 1.1 x 0.3 in, 1 in below the collar seam.

Colorways: Mountain Navy #13375D body, reflective grey by day / white when lit. Second run: Ink #212121 body; Forest #0D4C26 body. Reflective film does not change between colorways; the body does.

What a local would say: "That is the Shoreline at five in March." Then they will take a phone photo with the flash on to see it light up, which is the whole marketing plan.

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run: tier $$$$, roughly $38 to $55 landed using a stock technical long-sleeve blank (bonded-hem performance blank from a running-blank supplier) with the reflective transfer ($6 to $10 per placement at 50, film is expensive) and laser gussets done by a Utah laser shop. A true cut-and-sew version with our own pattern is not viable at 50 (research/merch/05, sampling dominates); it becomes viable at 250 through Portugal or a technical factory at $30 to $45. Retail $68 to $78 at the gear-adjacent tier.

One thing to verify before sampling: reflective film is stiff; an 11 in solid block of it can board the chest and crack at the fold. Sample at 50 percent halftone of the film (the lines still read white when lit, the hand stays soft) and compare to solid before committing.

Risk: A big grey block on navy is low contrast in daylight product photos. Shoot it twice, flat daylight and lit, and show both on the product page; the day shot alone undersells it.

## 02 Gain
File: `02-gain-alpha-pullover.svg`

IN references: 21-polartec-alpha-direct-swatch.jpg (the loft is the garment), 36-nike-acg-lava-loft-official.jpg (one hard element on an honest soft garment), 43-blackdiamond-lcc-home-crag.jpg and 54-wasatch-avalanche-danger-kutv.jpg (the vertical is right there), 52-porcupine-hill-climb-2016-mccutcheon.jpg (the number on the climb), 03-arcteryx-system-a-drop6-fieldmag.jpg (run-to-the-climb layering).

Idea: A raglan quarter-zip in Polartec Alpha Direct 90 with one element on it: a bonded Pertex strip, 2.25 in wide, that runs from the hem to the collar seam beside the zip, and the number 4,807 set 1.6 in tall running up the strip. 4,807 is the gain from the downtown grid (4,219 ft) to the top of Mount Olympus (9,026 ft), the peak in every office window. The strip is the climb; you read the number the way you read it on the way up. No coordinates, no labels, nothing else.

Why this garment and fabric: Alpha Direct is the active-insulation fabric the Wasatch crowd knows by name; it breathes while you climb and needs no liner. A pullover keeps it a mid-layer under a shell on the skin track and over a tee on the Shoreline in March. 90 g/m² is the weight you can run uphill in.

Technique and why: Alpha Direct loft takes no ink and should not be needled, so the strip is the only place a print can live. Pertex Quantum Air 20D (35 gsm), Cream, bonded to the loft with a stitch-free edge, with a one-colour Ink heat transfer of the numerals. The strip is also a real construction detail: it is the one wind-blocking panel on the chest and gives the soft garment its only hard edge. YKK Vislon #3 quarter zip with cord pull, flatlock raglan seams, Lycra-bound hem and cuffs, 3M Scotchlite "SALT LAKE" at the back neck, salt-crystal icon on the woven size tab at the hem.

Placement and size: strip on the wearer's left front, 0.75 in from the zip tape, 2.25 in wide, full body length (about 26 in on a M), bonded. Numerals 1.6 in tall, rotated to read upward, baseline toward the zip, bottom of the numerals 1 in above the hem binding.

Colorways: Rodeo Red #A42F2C loft (Polartec Dawn lot, lab dip to match) with Cream #F6F1EC strip and Ink numerals. Second run: Mountain Navy #13375D loft with Sego Lily #FFDDA3 strip and Ink numerals.

What a local would say: "Olympus from Pete's Rock is 4,100, not 4,800." The number is from the grid, not the trailhead, because the point of this city is that you start downtown. That argument is the right argument to have, and it is the hang-tag copy.

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run: tier $$$$, roughly $75 to $110 landed. Polartec will not sell 50 units' worth of fabric direct (300 to 500 yard per-colour minimums); the route is a converter that stocks Alpha Direct by the yard (Discovery Fabrics type, about 1.5 yd per unit at retail yardage) plus Pertex offcuts, sewn by a small domestic technical shop with a pattern we own, with the $500 to $1,500 development amortised over 50. At 250 units through a Wasatch brand that already holds the Polartec relationship (Black Diamond, Kuhl) this drops to $45 to $65. Retail $160 to $180, gear tier.

One thing to verify before sampling: that the bonding adhesive holds a 26 in Pertex strip to an open-grid loft through 20 washes without the loft pulling through or the strip puckering at the hem binding. Sample a 6 in strip first, wash-test, then cut the full one.

Risk: Alpha Direct snags and sheds; the strip edge is a snag line. Keep the bond edge inside the strip (no raw Pertex edge) and say "it sheds a little" on the product page, the way Norrøna does.

## 03 Valley Floor
File: `03-valley-floor-cap-sock.svg`

IN references: 34-ciele-fstcap-elite.jpg (perforated panels, reflective trim, no hardware), 41-tracksmith-us-trials-emily-maye.jpg (the number pinned to the body), 44-speedgoat-50k-2024-sinclair-irunfar.jpg (bib on the ridge), 40-tracksmith-amateur-spirit-cover.jpg (the kit you wear every day), 35-norda-blackdiamond-fkt-poles.jpg (one mark, all function), 52-porcupine-hill-climb-2016-mccutcheon.jpg (every climb starts on the valley floor).

Idea: A perforated five-panel running cap and a merino crew sock carrying one number, 4,219, the valley-floor elevation on the airport sign. On the cap it is set like a race bib: big, bib-spaced numerals in satin stitch, low on the side panel so the brim edge crops the baseline. On the sock it is a jacquard knit-in running up the back of the calf, facing whoever is behind you.

Why this garment and fabric: Hats are most of what a visitor buys and Ciele built a brand on one cap. 85 gsm four-way stretch recycled ripstop so it packs into a vest pocket; laser-perforated side and back panels (1.2 mm, 4 mm pitch) for the same reason the FSTCap has them. Sock: 58 percent 18.5-micron merino, 39 nylon, 3 elastane, 200-needle, because merino is the mountain-town fibre and a knit-in numeral is a knit decision, not a print.

Technique and why: Cap: satin-stitch embroidery, 1.6 in numerals, Ink on Cream, wearer's left side panel, about 7,000 stitches, over a tear-away stabiliser because the panel is perforated. 3M Scotchlite bound brim edge as the only reflective; Coolmax sweatband; elastic back, no buckle, no eyelets. Sock: jacquard knit-in numerals 1.75 in tall up the back of the calf, Cream on Mountain Navy, single 0.4 in Sage arch band, Y-heel gore, hand-linked toe. Salt-crystal icon on the woven sweatband label only. Sold as a set in a printed glassine sleeve.

Placement and size: cap numerals 1.6 in tall, bib letterspacing, centred on the left side panel, baseline 0.25 in above the brim seam so the brim visually crops it. Sock numerals 1.75 in tall, centre back, starting 0.75 in above the heel gore and reading upward.

Colorways: Cream #F6F1EC cap with Ink #212121 numerals; Mountain Navy #13375D sock with Cream numerals and Sage #8DCEA8 arch band. Second run: Sky #147CB2 cap with Cream numerals; Cream sock with Navy numerals.

What a local would say: "It's the elevation on the airport sign." Then: "Where's the front logo?" There isn't one. That absence is the status signal for the person who skis 20 days a year on a lunch break.

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run: tier $$ for the set, roughly $28 to $40 landed. Cap: an embroidered blank cap at 50 is $14 to $18 (research/merch/05), plus $4 to $8 to step up to a stock laser-perforated ripstop running blank, so $18 to $26. A fully custom cap is 150-plus overseas; not at 50. Sock: a domestic small-batch sock mill with a 50-pair minimum runs $9 to $14 a pair for a two-colour jacquard with a one-time $75 to $150 pattern set-up. Retail $64 for the set ($44 cap, $24 sock sold separately later), accessible tier.

One thing to verify before sampling: that satin stitch at 1.6 in over perforated ripstop does not pucker or tear out at the hole line; sample with tear-away and with a cut-away stabiliser and pick the one that lies flat after a wet-dry cycle. On the sock, confirm the mill can knit a 1.75 in numeral without the float on the reverse snagging a toenail (ask for a floats-tied sample).

Risk: A number alone can read as arbitrary to a visitor; the sleeve carries the one line "4,219 ft. The valley floor. Everything here is uphill from it." and the deck shows the cap next to the Gain fleece so the two numbers read as one system.
