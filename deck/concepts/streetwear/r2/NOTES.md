# Streetwear lane: concept round 2 notes

Lane thesis (unchanged): Salt Lake is for the weird. Band-merch logic: back prints, sleeve hits, xerox texture, one-line phrases, the straight-edge X as hardware, SLC Punk! blue-black-white.

What changed from R1 under the CD's rule: the sheet header, the spec strip and every small tracked-caps line are gone. Sheets are now lookbook pages: the piece name set large, two plain sentences, the garment, the graphic blown up, swatches with names, a short caption. The spec lives here. The only small type left on any garment is the flyer bar on piece 02 ("ALL AGES" / "NO RE-ENTRY" in typewriter inside inverse bars, tilted and pasted up like 31-flickr-flyer-c), which is a flyer device, not a label, and the handwritten "actual size" on its back, which is a marker note with an arrow. If Yid reads either as an eyebrow, both come off without hurting the piece.

Palette: Ink #212121, Cream #F6F1EC, Salt Pink #F58B88. Off-palette hit: Poster Blue #1A3BD4 on piece 03 only. Fonts in the SVGs are stand-ins (Anton for condensed poster type, Permanent Marker for hand lettering, Special Elite for typewriter); all text is live vector text. Each sheet is 1200 x 1500 and self-contained.

## 01  801, COUNTED IN X's  (tally longsleeve, replacement for the cut All Ages piece)
File: 01-801-tally-longsleeve.svg

**IN references:** 04-online-ceramics-slaughterhouse-longsleeve.jpg (XXX stack down a black sleeve, white ink), 19-flickr-straight-edge.jpg (the X'd hand, the scene's hardware), 01-online-ceramics-grateful-60-longsleeve.jpg (a count as the whole graphic), 03-online-ceramics-blink182-dude-ranch-longsleeve.jpg (sleeve print plus a small chest hit).

**Idea:** A black longsleeve whose back is a tally of straight-edge X's that counts to 801, the area code, the way you count on a wall: four X's and a strike, 160 full groups and one X left over. The lone 801st X is the chest hit; the wearer's left sleeve says "801" in marker, the right sleeve is the XXX. No venue list, no band, no words beyond the number.

**Why this over the other two options:** the zine-collage back and the xerox photo tee both need licensed 1990s photographs, and the approved set has none we can clear. The tally needs nothing but a marker and a letterer, and it is the most band-merch object of the three: a sleeve-and-back shirt that only makes sense if you know what an X on a hand means.

**Garment and fabric:** 6.5 to 7 oz heavyweight longsleeve (Shaka Wear or Los Angeles Apparel 1807GD class), Ink body, ribbed cuffs. Longsleeve because the scene wears them to shows and the piece needs two arms.

**Technique:** 1-color plastisol, Cream, from hand-drawn art. The 801 marks are drawn once at full size by a letterer in chisel marker on a 13 x 17 in sheet, photocopied one generation and separated; the SVG jitter is a stand-in for that hand. Sleeve prints are a separate pass on a sleeve platen.

**Placement and size:** full back 13 x 17 in, 3 in below the collar seam; left chest X 3 x 3 in; wearer-left sleeve "801" 2.5 x 9 in reading down from the shoulder; wearer-right sleeve XXX 2.5 x 9 in.

**Colorways:** Ink body / Cream ink (main). Alt: Salt Pink pigment-dyed body / Ink ink (the pink flyer paper from R1, kept as a second run if the first sells).

**Unit cost at a 50-unit test run:** decorated-premium-blank lane, mid tier. Heavyweight longsleeve blank $9 to $12, three print locations with two setups on a 50 run roughly $9 to $12 per piece, woven label and hangtag under $1. Landed about $20 to $25, retails $60 to $70 DTC.

**The one thing to verify before sampling:** that the printer can hold a 13 x 17 in back on the chosen blank's body width in size S (some heavyweight longsleeves run narrow; the wall may need to drop to 12 x 16 in, which changes the row count, not the count).

**What a local says:** "Someone counted." Then they count a row to check.

**Risk:** 801 also covers Provo and Ogden; the shirt claims the whole Wasatch Front, not just the county. That is fine for a punk and may not be fine for a county DMO. If it matters, the count can run to 84101, the downtown ZIP, as 16,820 groups, which is a different and funnier object (a shirt that is solid cream from a distance) and would need a halftone separation instead of a hand drawing.

## 02  IT CAME FROM THE GREAT SALT LAKE  (creature-feature tee, the hero)
File: 02-it-came-from-the-great-salt-lake-tee.svg

**IN references:** 05-brain-dead-sunn-o-tee.jpg (one grainy square on a plain body is enough), 31-flickr-flyer-c.jpg (xerox photo under heavy type, the inverse flyer bars), 30-flickr-flyer-b.jpg (cut-and-paste collage, one ink), 34-flickr-zine-a.jpg (zine black and white), 19-flickr-straight-edge.jpg (the X'd hands in the crowd), plus the brine shrimp entry in the symbol bank.

**Idea:** Artemia franciscana, the half-inch animal that is the lake's whole economy, blown up on a copier until it is a 1950s creature-feature monster rearing out of the pink north arm toward an all-ages crowd with its X'd hands up. Three scales on one shirt: the monster at fourteen inches, the crowd at an inch, and the same animal at life size on the upper back with "actual size" written beside it in marker, so the joke lands as you walk away.

**What R2 pushed:** the drawing. The shrimp now has a tapered abdomen of overlapping plates, a wide thorax, feathered phyllopods with setae reaching down for the crowd, stalked compound eyes with a halftone catchlight, mandibles, and the male's clasper antennae drawn as pincers. The sky is a stepped halftone (seven dot sizes, 22 degree screen) with copier streaks, the mountains are a flat ink silhouette with Antelope Island's humps, the water is a Salt Pink flood with ink wave lines and a splash where the tail breaks the surface, and the whole art has a rough-line displacement and 650 xerox specks. Title is a hand-lettered opener ("IT CAME FROM", slanted) over two lines of condensed poster type with a Salt Pink misregistration under both, like a two-color flyer printed a hair off. The crowd is two rows of silhouettes, front row with hair spikes, hands up, about half with a cream X knocked out of the back of the hand and the rest throwing two fingers.

**Garment and fabric:** 7 oz heavyweight boxy tee (Shaka Wear Max Heavyweight or LA Apparel 1801GD class), Cream, not optic white, so the print looks copied onto paper that has lived in a backpack.

**Technique:** 2-color screen print, water-based Ink and Salt Pink, pink down first, ink on top with a deliberate 1/16 in offset. Separations come off a real photocopy of the paste-up so every speck survives; the halftone sky is kept as a stochastic separation, not cleaned up. Back hit is one color, Ink.

**Placement and size:** front 14 x 15.5 in, 2 in below the collar seam; upper back 4 x 2 in, 1.5 in below the collar, shrimp printed at 0.5 in actual length.

**Colorways:** Cream body / Ink + Salt Pink (main). Alt: Ink body with the art flipped negative (Cream + Salt Pink inks), which turns the halftone sky into stars.

**Unit cost at a 50-unit test run:** decorated-premium-blank lane, mid tier. Heavyweight tee blank $7 to $10, two-color front plus one-color back on a 50 run roughly $8 to $11 per piece including three screens, label and tag under $1. Landed about $17 to $22, retails $50 to $60 DTC.

**The one thing to verify before sampling:** a press proof of the halftone sky at 110 mesh with water-based ink on the garment-dyed cream; if the small dots fill in, the sky moves to a coarser screen or to a hand-stippled version by the illustrator.

**Finish by an illustrator:** redraw the shrimp in brush and ink from this drawing at 2x, keep the segment count and the clasper pose, give the plates real overlap and the legs weight; hand-letter "IT CAME FROM" in a fat marker. Keep the crowd as cut-paper silhouettes, not drawn figures.

**What a local says:** "Sea-Monkey from hell." Then they explain to the visitor that the lake really is pink on the north side and that the shrimp are a $60-million-a-year harvest, which is the hangtag.

**Risk:** the title is a stock horror construction, not a scene phrase, and the CD accepted that in R1. The remaining risk is the Objects lane's scientific shrimp on the pouch; the two can share a round but should not share a table at the visitor center. Alternate flyer bars if VSL wants it closer to straight edge: "DRUG FREE" / "ALL AGES".

## 03  NOTHING TO DO IN SALT LAKE  (three-phrase hoodie drop)
File: 03-nothing-to-do-in-salt-lake-hoodie.svg

**IN references:** 36-slc-punk-poster.jpg (white condensed title on black bars over a flat blue field), 26-slc-punk-still-b.jpg (the blue hair, the color trio), 08-pleasures-freak-tee-glitter.jpg (phrase-first single hit), 07-pleasures-suburbs-longsleeve-lookbook.jpg (heavy body, small wordmark logic).

**Idea:** A 14 oz hoodie with one sentence on it, split across the layout of the SLC Punk! poster: the first half in condensed cream type on a black title bar between two thin rules, then a flat blue flood left empty, with the second half of the sentence tucked along the flood's bottom edge. The empty blue is the nothing. R2 runs the drop as three approved phrases on three bodies, shown together: NOTHING TO DO / IN SALT LAKE on Ink, SALT LAKE IS / FOR THE WEIRD on Salt Pink, DON'T MOVE / TO SALT LAKE on Cream. The hood is lined in the blue on all three so the trio shows with the hood down.

**Garment and fabric:** cross-grain 14 oz cotton fleece pullover hoodie (Los Angeles Apparel HF09 or House of Blanks class), hood lined in Poster Blue fleece, flat cream drawcords with metal tips (ink cords on the cream body).

**Technique:** 2-color plastisol, Cream and Poster Blue, the blue pulled as a flat flood with a dry-brush edge so it never reads as a corporate color block; on the Ink body the bar is the garment and only two colors print, on the Pink and Cream bodies the bar prints as a third color (Ink). The thin rules print in Cream on Ink and Pink bodies, in Ink on the Cream body.

**Placement and size:** front panel 11.5 x 13 in, top edge 3 in below the hood seam, bottom edge 1 in above the kangaroo pocket. No back print.

**Colorways:** Ink body (NOTHING TO DO IN SALT LAKE), Salt Pink body (SALT LAKE IS FOR THE WEIRD), Cream body (DON'T MOVE TO SALT LAKE). Hood lining Poster Blue on all three. Phrases and bodies can be re-paired at the CD's call without any other change to the art.

**Unit cost at a 50-unit test run:** decorated-premium-blank lane, upper tier. 14 oz fleece blank $22 to $28, custom hood lining is a cut-and-sew step (an LA studio can line 50 hoods for roughly $6 to $9 each if the blank maker will ship unlined hoods, otherwise it waits for a 150-unit private-label run), two- or three-color front print on a 50 run roughly $7 to $10, label and tag about $1. Landed about $38 to $48 with the lining, $32 to $38 without, retails $120 to $140 DTC.

**The one thing to verify before sampling:** whether the blank maker will sell the hoodie with the hood lining omitted or in a specified color; if not, the blue hood lining becomes a blue hood interior print or drops, and the piece still works.

**What a local says:** "Yeah, that's what we said at 16, and then we went to Kilby every night." Then they buy DON'T MOVE TO SALT LAKE for the friend who just did.

**Risk:** a tourism office printing "nothing to do" and "don't move here" is the whole joke and the whole risk; the client has to want the irony. DON'T MOVE TO SALT LAKE is the line locals actually say when the city shows up on a best-places list, said with pride, and it sells the place by pretending to keep it. If VSL blinks, any candidate below drops into the same layout with no other change. Per the CD, no product in this drop names any other destination. The SLC Punk! logotype is not reproduced anywhere; the piece uses the bar-and-flood structure and the palette only.
