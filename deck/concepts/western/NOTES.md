# Neo-Western lane: concept round 1 notes

Lane thesis (CD approved): "Rancher at 7,000 feet." Quiet workwear, real craft, real ranch. Designed only from the IN items in MARKS.md for western (03, 05, 06, 08, 10, 11, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24). Loud yokes, chenille, Rockmount, Nudie's, Beyoncé are treated as banned directions.

Shared decisions
- Garment copy is place and plain statement only: "Albion Basin, 9,500 ft", "Antelope Island · Herd · 1893", "Days of '47 · Cattle Drive · Main Street · Salt Lake", the brand eyebrow coordinates and elevation. No taglines, no "Salt Lake City."
- Type: Ivar (Playfair Display stand-in in the SVGs) for titles and the rag headline; Saans (Inter stand-in) for tracked caps. The cuff script is a letterer's job; the font in the file is a placeholder.
- Hardware and craft are the decoration: pearl snaps with copper rims, chain stitch on a Singer 114w103 (ref 11), veg-tan deboss, hand-rolled hems. No VSL logo in any artwork; the salt-crystal line icon lives on the neck label only.
- Palette: Mountain Navy, Forest, Rodeo Red, Terracotta, Aspen Gold, Sego Lily, Sage, Sky, Light Sky, Cream, Ink. No off-palette hit used in this lane.

Reference flag: the file 19-days-of-47-longhorn-drive-slc.jpg in deck/moodboards/web/western/ is not the cattle drive; it is a photo of a congregation in a meetinghouse (the Church News gallery scrape pulled the wrong image). Piece 03 was designed from the caption and the real event, and the illustrator will need the actual drive photo from the Church News gallery URL in captions.md.

## 01 Paintbrush Yoke Shirt
File: 01-paintbrush-yoke-shirt.svg

IN references: 03-ginew-western-snap-chambray.jpg (block, cuff hardware, contrast stitching), 11-fort-lonesome-chainstitch-machine.jpg (technique), 06-fort-lonesome-texas-suit.jpg (botanical chain stitch, taken to the quiet end), 23-albion-basin-indian-paintbrush.jpg (the motif), 24-wasatch-aspen-grove.jpg (Sage and green-gold stem palette).

Idea: A plain Mountain Navy snap shirt whose only decoration is one small chain-stitched cluster of Indian paintbrush and lupine on the back yoke, the two flowers that actually grow in Albion Basin in July. Inside the left cuff, a hidden chain-stitch script reads "Albion Basin, 9,500 ft", seen only when the sleeve is rolled.

Garment and fabric: A western snap shirt is the lane's native object (Ginew, Fort Lonesome both start here). 7 oz brushed cotton twill in Mountain Navy gives the thread something to sit on and reads as fall and winter in the city; the 5.5 oz chambray alt is the summer version. Rodeo Red twill is the second colorway, with the paintbrush shifting to Sego Lily and Aspen Gold so the flower still pops.

Technique: Chain stitch, five thread colors (Rodeo Red, Terracotta, Sky, Light Sky, Sage), 4.5 x 3.5 in, centered on the back yoke, 1.25 in below the collar seam. Chain stitch because the loop texture is the whole point of the lane (ref 11): it looks hand-run because it is, and it ages better than flat satin embroidery. The cuff script is 5 x 0.6 in in Sego Lily on the cuff facing. Front is completely clean.

What a local would say: "That's paintbrush. You've been up Little Cottonwood in July." Then they roll the cuff and find the elevation.

Risk: Chain stitch on a run of any size means a contract shop with 114w103 machines (Fort Lonesome-type shops are backlogged for months and priced like art). Budget a domestic chain-stitch house or accept a 100-unit first drop at a hero price.

## 02 Island Herd Chore Coat
File: 02-island-herd-chore-coat.svg

IN references: 05-bode-homer-quilt-jacket.jpg (triangle quilt logic, Cream, gold, rust, red), 22-antelope-island-roundup-riders-herd.jpg (the bison silhouette, tan grass and dark animal), 21-antelope-island-roundup-rider-glove.jpg (leather and hand detail, the reason the patch is leather), 16-louis-vuitton-fw24-pharrell-finale.jpg (blanket coat proportion, the reframing of who wears western), 08-tecovas-boot-truck-window.jpg (waxed canvas and boot leather as the lane's outer materials).

Idea: Plain waxed Forest canvas chore coat on the outside, a full half-square-triangle quilt in the brand palette on the inside, so the brand colors are the quilt and the reveal is the design. The one outside mark is a 2 x 1.25 in veg-tan leather patch on the chest pocket: a single bison silhouette and "Antelope Island · Herd · 1893".

Garment and fabric: A chore coat is what the roundup riders and the Wasatch Front actually wear over a snap shirt from October to April. 10 oz waxed cotton canvas in Forest ages into the Terracotta and gold of the lining as it creases. The lining is printed cotton sateen, channel-quilted on the diagonal over 4 oz fill, which gives Bode's object without cutting up an antique quilt. Ink corduroy collar, corozo buttons.

Technique: Printed and quilted lining rather than pieced, so the repeat (one block = 3 in) is exact and the cost is a jacket, not a quilt. Leather patch is hot-stamp debossed, no ink, so it darkens with the coat. The year 1893 is when bison were put on the island (research symbol bank, verified). The roundup's own founding year is not settled in our files: Deseret News called 2024 the 36th roundup, which implies 1989, while the commonly cited start is 1987; the patch therefore carries the herd date, not the event date. Mark as verify before production if Yid wants the roundup year instead.

What a local would say: "Roundup coat." Then they open it and see the quilt and understand the Terracotta and gold are the island in October.

Risk: Waxed canvas plus a quilted lining is a warm, heavy, $300-plus garment with a limited selling window and a long lead time (waxed canvas from a UK or Pacific Northwest mill). The quilt print has to be printed in a real sateen or it reads as a motel bedspread; sample it before committing.

## 03 Main Street Drive Wild Rag
File: 03-main-street-drive-wild-rag.svg

IN references: 18-ginew-gently-strikes-wild-rag-portrait.jpg (a bandana as the color hit, worn with a plain jacket), 19-days-of-47-longhorn-drive-slc.jpg (caption: longhorns in front of the SLC skyline, the city and the ranch in one frame; see reference flag above), 20-days-of-47-rodeo-bareback.jpg (the rodeo week context and the arena-side serif type), 10-understated-leather-bolo-fringe.jpg (the companion bolo slide and braided cord).

Idea: A 22 x 22 in wild rag whose single-color print is the Days of '47 longhorn drive down Main Street drawn as one illustration: Wasatch ridgeline, downtown towers with a few windows knocked out, the TRAX rails as a street band, three ranks of longhorns and one rider. Headline "Days of '47" and a plain statement line, "Longhorns down Main Street, the mountains behind the towers. Every July." The same steer is engraved on a brass bolo slide sold with the rag.

Garment and fabric: The wild rag is the smallest, cheapest, most giftable object in the lane and it is the lane's color hit against a navy or Forest piece. 12 mm silk twill for the hero, 4.5 oz cotton voile for the core price. Mountain Navy on Sego Lily is the lead colorway (the gold of the paintbrush field and the state flower together); Rodeo Red on Cream and Sage on Forest for later drops.

Technique: One-color water-based screen print, 20 x 20 in image area, hand-rolled hem. One color because a single-line illustration is what the lane does (ref 11 and 12 logic, moved to print) and because it folds into a 2 in band where the skyline shows in the knot and the herd in the tail. The bolo slide is engraved brass with a white enamel field, 1.5 in, braided leather cord. The illustration in the SVG is massing; an illustrator redraws it as a dry-brush line from the real Church News photo.

What a local would say: "That's the cattle drive. They shut down Main for it." Visitors see a bandana; locals see a specific Thursday in July.

Risk: "Days of '47" is the name of a private nonprofit event (Days of '47 Inc.), so a named-event print needs a license or a change to "July 24" or "Pioneer Day cattle drive". The skyline must stay generic massing; drawing the temple or a branded tower invites a different conversation.
