# Neo-Western lane: concept round 2 notes

All three R1 pieces were marked IN, so there is no replacement slot. Round 2 pushes the drawing on each one and resolves the two R1 flags. Lane thesis stays "Rancher at 7,000 feet": quiet workwear, real craft, the place named plainly.

What changed everywhere
- Sheets are lookbook pages now: a name, one italic line, the garment large, the detail blown up, colorways as named swatches, a caption in sentences. No sheet header, no spec strip, no tracked-caps labels, no coordinates or elevations anywhere. The spec lives here.
- The hidden cuff stitch on 01 is no longer "Albion Basin, 9,500 ft". It is the place name alone, in script: "Albion Basin". The script in the file is Pinyon Script as a placeholder; a letterer draws the real one.
- The bison patch on 02 carries 1893, the year bison were put on Antelope Island (symbol bank, verified). It is the herd date, not the roundup date, and the sheet says so in plain words under the patch. The roundup's founding year is unsettled in our files (1987 vs 1989), so we do not print it.
- The rag on 03 no longer says "Days of '47" anywhere. The headline is "Pioneer Day", the public holiday, and the image carries the event. No license needed. The reference flag from R1 stands: 19-days-of-47-longhorn-drive-slc.jpg in the moodboard folder is a meetinghouse photo, not the drive; the illustrator works from the Church News gallery URL in captions.md.
- Type: Ivar is still the intent for titles and the rag headline (Playfair Display stands in). Inter stands in for Saans on swatch names and the small sentences. Nothing is set in tracked caps.
- Palette by hex from BRAND-DIGEST.md only. No off-palette hit in this lane. Veg-tan leather and brass are materials, not colors.

Fonts named in the SVGs: Playfair Display, Inter, Pinyon Script (Google Fonts), with Georgia / Helvetica / cursive fallbacks.

## 01 Paintbrush Yoke Shirt
File: 01-paintbrush-yoke-shirt.svg

What is on the sheet: the shirt from the back with the yoke embroidery in place; the embroidery at thread scale in an inset; the left cuff turned back to show the script; two colorways with their thread sets; the plain front, small.

The drawing, R2: the botanical is now built as chain stitch, not as line art. Every stroke is a row of linked loops that follows the direction a 114w103 would run it: bracts of the paintbrush are three parallel rows from base to tip with the split tips as two short diverging rows, the lower bracts go to Sage the way the real plant does at the base; the lupine florets are small looped rings stacked in pairs up the spike, Sky low and Light Sky high, with a Cream keel stitch in the top florets; the lupine leaf is seven leaflets radiating from one point; stems are two rows side by side (Sage and Meadow) so they read as a stalk, not a wire; grass and a loose ground row in Sage. Three paintbrush stalks at three heights, two lupine spikes, a fifth paintbrush low and small. What a chain-stitch artisan finishes: the real loop count (the file is a stand-in at about 6 px per loop), the fill density in the bract heads, and the thread shading between Rodeo Red and Terracotta.

Blank or maker: a plain western snap shirt in 7 oz brushed cotton twill, pointed back yoke, pearl snaps with copper rims, cuff facing in self fabric. Sourcing route for the 50-unit test: a stock blank western shirt from a workwear maker, re-labeled, rather than cut-and-sew (see cost). Summer alt: 5.5 oz chambray.

Technique: hand-guided chain stitch (Singer 114w103 or Cornely) for the hero run; six thread colors (Rodeo Red, Terracotta, Sky, Light Sky, Sage, Meadow). Cuff script in Sego Lily chain stitch, 5 x 0.7 in, on the inside cuff facing of the left sleeve so it shows only when rolled.

Placement and size: yoke motif 4.5 x 3.5 in, centered on the back, top of the tallest bract 1.25 in below the collar seam. Front is empty. Neck label: woven, "Salt Lake" in Ivar italic, the small salt-crystal line icon beside it; no VSL logo.

Colorways: Mountain Navy twill (paintbrush Rodeo Red and Terracotta, lupine Sky and Light Sky, Sage and Meadow stems, Sego Lily cuff script). Rodeo Red twill (paintbrush Sego Lily and Aspen Gold, lupine Sky and Cream, same stems, Cream cuff script).

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run (est., from research/merch/05): a plain blank western snap shirt sourced from a workwear maker runs $28 to $45 wholesale; machine chain stitch (Cornely multi-head) at this size is $8 to $15 per placement at 50 pieces, two placements; labels and relabel $1 to $2; landed roughly $48 to $75 per unit, which is the $$$$ decoration tier. Hand-guided artisan chain stitch (Fort Lonesome type) is $25 to $150 per piece and makes this a numbered launch piece at $150 to $350 retail, which is the brand's chain-stitch price band. Custom cut-and-sew is not viable at 50 (development $500 to $1,500 before the first shirt).

One thing to verify before sampling: that a domestic multi-head chain-stitch house can hold six thread changes in a 4.5 in motif at 50 pieces without the loops closing up; ask for a sew-out on the actual twill, not on a swatch of broadcloth.

What a local would say: "That's paintbrush. You've been up Little Cottonwood in July." Then they roll the cuff.

Risk: chain stitch at any scale means a specialist shop and a lead time measured in months. If the test run has to be fast, run a flat-embroidery version of the same drawing and keep the chain stitch for the hero 24.

## 02 Island Herd Chore Coat
File: 02-island-herd-chore-coat.svg

What is on the sheet: the coat opened flat so the whole lining shows, fronts folded out; the leather patch at hand scale in an inset with a plain sentence under it giving the date's meaning; the coat closed, small, with the patch on the chest pocket; two shell colorways; one quilt block at repeat size with a sentence on how it is made.

The drawing, R2: the garment is shown open, as asked. The lining is a half-square-triangle quilt built in pinwheel blocks, one block three inches, in Cream, Sego Lily, Aspen Gold, Terracotta and Rodeo Red, with the piecing seams drawn as thin lines and the channel quilting as a dashed diagonal, one direction, the way it will actually be stitched. The bison on the patch is a proper silhouette now (hump, low head, beard, horns, four legs) instead of massing. The patch reads "Antelope Island" in Ivar italic along the top, the bison across the middle, and "1893" large in Ivar bold at the bottom left: the year is the graphic, not a caption. A leather grain filter sits on the patch in the file; the hot-stamp die is cut from the vector.

Blank or maker: cut-and-sew, domestic (LA or Texas studio) for the hero; for the 50-unit test, a stock waxed-canvas chore coat blank from a workwear maker, re-lined. 10 oz waxed cotton canvas shell, Ink corduroy collar, corozo buttons, two hip pockets and one chest pocket, hanging loop.

Technique: lining is printed cotton sateen (the quilt is a print, not pieced) channel-quilted on the diagonal over 4 oz fill. Patch is veg-tan leather, 2 x 1.25 in, hot-stamp debossed, no ink, sewn to the chest pocket on all four sides. Neck label as on 01.

Placement and size: patch centered on the chest pocket, 0.5 in below the pocket's top edge. Lining covers body and sleeves. No other marks outside.

Colorways: Forest waxed canvas with the Cream, Sego Lily, Aspen Gold, Terracotta, Rodeo Red quilt. Mountain Navy waxed canvas with the same quilt (navy is the lake on the drive out to the island).

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run (est., from research/merch/05): a fully custom waxed-canvas coat with printed quilted lining is lane-two cut-and-sew at the very bottom of its MOQ; landed $120 to $180 per unit plus $1,500 to $3,000 of development (pattern, two samples, lining print strike-off), and waxed canvas from a UK or Pacific Northwest mill carries a yardage minimum that 50 coats may not reach. That is a $320 to $380 retail garment and not a test-run product. The honest 50-unit test is the patch and the lining print on a stock waxed chore coat blank ($55 to $85 wholesale), re-lined by a local sewing shop at $25 to $40 per coat, patch $2 to $4 each at 50, landed roughly $85 to $130, which is the $$$$ tier. Price it as a hero at $240 to $280 and treat the first run as the sample order.

One thing to verify before sampling: the lining print on sateen. Print one yard, quilt it, and look at it under shop light; if it reads as a motel bedspread the whole piece fails. Also confirm the leather patch supplier can deboss a 58 pt numeral cleanly at 2 in.

What a local would say: "Roundup coat." Then they open it.

Risk: weight, warmth and price limit the selling window to October through March, and a re-lined blank has a label that is not ours unless the maker allows relabeling; confirm before ordering blanks.

## 03 Main Street Drive Wild Rag
File: 03-main-street-drive-wild-rag.svg

What is on the sheet: the rag flat at 22 x 22 in with the finished one-color drawing; the rag tied at the throat over a Forest chore coat collar, with the skyline in the fold and the herd in the tails; the brass bolo slide with the same steer; three colorways with small flats of the second and third.

The drawing, R2: one picture in one color. Two Wasatch ridgelines with hatched slopes, a downtown massing of thirteen towers with windows knocked out of the ink and hatched shadow sides, the street with the rails as two long lines and ties, the curb below, three ranks of longhorns walking right (ten in the far rank, eight, then six near), three riders (one on point ahead of the near rank, one at the back, one in the middle distance), dust and hoof marks on the street. Headline "Pioneer Day" in Ivar bold with a rule under it, corner horn marks, double-rule hem, the statement in Ivar italic: "Longhorns down Main Street, the mountains behind the towers. Every July." and "Salt Lake" at the foot. A turbulence displacement filter roughens the ink for the sheet; the illustrator redraws the steers and riders as dry-brush line from the Church News photo and keeps the layout. Skyline stays generic massing: no temple, no branded tower.

Blank or maker: 22 x 22 in 12 mm silk twill with hand-rolled hem for the hero; 4.5 oz cotton voile with a machine-rolled hem for the core price. Bolo slide: engraved brass, 1.5 in, white enamel field, braided leather cord with brass tips.

Technique: one-color water-based screen print, 20 x 20 in image area, one screen. Printed before hemming. Bolo slide engraved from the same vector steer.

Placement and size: print centered on the square; headline cap height 2.5 in; herd band 4 in tall across the full width; the rag folds on the diagonal into a 2 in band so the skyline lands in the knot and the herd in the tails (shown on the sheet).

Colorways: Mountain Navy on Sego Lily (lead), Rodeo Red on Cream (second drop), Sage on Forest (third drop; printed in Sage on a Forest-dyed blank).

Estimated unit cost at a 50-unit test run (est., from research/merch/05): a blank 22 in silk twill square with hand-rolled hem from a scarf maker is $9 to $16 at 50; one-color water-based screen print $3 to $6 per piece at this quantity with $40 to $60 of screen setup amortized; label $0.50; landed silk roughly $14 to $24 per unit ($ to $$ decoration tier). Cotton voile blank $2.50 to $4.50, landed $7 to $12. Bolo slide, engraved brass with enamel at an MOQ of 50 to 100: $7 to $14 each, sold as a set with the silk at $85 to $95 or alone at $28. The rag is the lane's volume product and the only one that clears the brand's margin ladder at 50 units.

One thing to verify before sampling: water-based ink on silk twill. Some shops will only print silk with discharge or pigment; ask for a strike-off on the actual silk and wash it twice before approving the hand.

What a local would say: "That's the cattle drive. They shut down Main for it."

Risk: the illustrator's redraw has to stay one color and keep the knockout windows legible at 20 in; if the drawing gets busier than this file it stops folding well. Hold the line count.
