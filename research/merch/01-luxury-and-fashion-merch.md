# How luxury houses and fashion-led brands think about "merch" and objects

Research slice for the Visit Salt Lake merch strategy deck. Thesis the deck is testing: destination merch should carry the brand's ethos on the garment, not just a logo on a tee.

Research notes: compiled 2026-10-26 from web search results. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment and the search budget ran out mid-pass, so some items are marked **[background, verify]** where I am drawing on general knowledge rather than a fetched source. Everything with a URL next to it came back in search results. Prices move constantly; treat them as snapshots.

---

## 1. The philosophy in one paragraph

The brands people actually collect do not think of merch as a logo applied to a blank. They think of it as the cheapest, fastest, most portable way to put their worldview on a body. Hermès does it by refusing the word merch entirely: the scarf, the Rodeo charm, the $19,300 ski and the petit h offcut object all exist to prove the house's craft obsession at every price point, from $300 to $400,000. Streetwear houses do it by treating scarcity, cadence and in-jokes as the product: Supreme's Thursday drop, Corteiz's password site and jacket-swap stunts, Palace's deadpan copy. The "world-builders" (Aimé Leon Dore, Jacquemus, Kith, Dover Street Market, Rapha) do it by building a place, a café, a car, a club, and letting the garment act as the souvenir of having been inside the world. The 2024 to 2026 wave of restaurant, hotel, grocery and museum merch borrows all three moves: a Highsnobiety essay literally titled "Everything Is Merch" argues anything can become merch if it carries enough symbolic value, and Vogue Business's 2026 summit framed the product as "the souvenir of an experience rather than the experience itself." The lesson for a destination: the city already has the world. The garment just has to be a credible artifact of it, and credibility comes from specific references, materials that match the claim, restraint with the logo, and a release rhythm that feels like a scene rather than a gift shop.

## 2. Brand-by-brand notes

### Hermès

**petit h (the offcut objects program).** Started 2010 to 2011 by Pascale Mussard, sixth-generation family member nicknamed since childhood "don't throw that out, it might be useful." The method is "creation in reverse": artisans start with leftover leather, silk, crystal and porcelain that failed Hermès QC and ask what can be invented, instead of starting with an idea and sourcing material. Motto: "We don't throw anything away!" Mussard framed it as "care" rather than sustainability, and as craft tradition rather than a green initiative. Current creative director Godefroy de Virieu: "ideas are materials." At launch the range ran roughly $70 to $70,000; pieces are one-of-one and are sold through traveling exhibitions that move city to city, plus a limited number in boutiques and online. A permanent petit h store opened in 2021. Resale example: a petit h bag charm at $264 on a reseller.
Sources: https://anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/3204/pascale-mussard-creative-director-of-hermes-petit-h, https://nuvomagazine.com/magazine/issue-107-winter-2025/hermes-reimagines-the-leftovers-of-luxury-with-petit-h, https://musemagazine.it/articles/hermes-petit-h-en/, https://www.port-magazine.com/?p=37898, https://fashionunited.es/noticias/retail/hermes-da-alas-a-su-linea-upcycling-y-abre-la-primera-tienda-permanente-de-petit-h/2021091436258, https://www.luxurydaily.com/hermes-spotlights-upcycled-crafts-at-madison-avenue-flagship/print

Why it matters for us: the "waste as treasure" story turns a cost center into the most talked-about product line in the house. It also gives Hermès a reason to tour, which is exactly what a destination brand needs.

**The carré as merch logic.** First silk scarf "Jeu des Omnibus et Dames Blanches," 1937, for the centenary, conceived by Robert Dumas, drawn by Hugo Grygkar, based on a parlour game and framed by horse-drawn omnibuses (equestrian heritage baked into the first one). Over 1,000 to 1,500 designs since. Standard format 90 x 90 cm silk twill with a hand-rolled roulotté hem. The scarf is Hermès's entry ticket: an illustrated, collectible, story-per-design object, roughly $300 to $400 resale average, $70 for a Twilly up to $1,800 for rare editions. **[background, verify]**: current US retail for a 90 cm carré sits around $550 to $600.
Sources: https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/hermes-scarf-history, https://fashionmagazine.com/?p=56817, https://www.jolicloset.com/en/designers-mujer/hermes/womens-accessories/panuelos-seda

Why it matters: every scarf is a new illustrated story on a fixed canvas. That is a merch program disguised as an accessory. A destination could run the same thing with bandanas or ski buffs: one format, one illustrator per edition, a story per print.

**Rodeo charms and playful objects.** The Rodeo Pégase PM (a winged horse in Milo lambskin and Swift calfskin) retails about $770 to $820 US, roughly 600 to 630 euros, CN¥12,000 in China; the MM runs higher. A 2026 "Grigri Rodeo Pégase Touch" with matte alligator wings resells at $1,575. A set of five PM charms from 2021 to 2022 sold at Sotheby's for $5,000. These are the "fun" tier of a house that is otherwise austere: a horse mascot, a joke in leather, something you can actually buy without a waitlist.
Sources: https://ginzaxiaoma.com/blog/hermes-charm, https://sothebys.com/en/buy/_hermes-set-of-5-rodeo-pegase-pm-charms-2021-2022, https://baguseek.com/hermes/product/us/en/rodeo-pegase-pm-charm-H083010CADC, https://majestixcollection.com/product/2026-new-hermes-grigri-rodeo-pegase-touch-bag-charm-noir-pm/

**Ski and sport ("Games and Sports").** October 2025: "Off-Piste" skis, sycamore wood over an ash core from the Vercors, 158 cm, $19,300 (All Mountain) and $18,600 (Alpine); telescopic poles $2,425; Off Piste ski backpack about CZK 182,000. Other objects in the category: street skateboard CA$7,750, long skateboard HKD 68,000, "En Selle" cycling helmet £2,530, Odyssee Eau paddle board HKD 444,300, foosball table $192,900, a $372,000 pool table, carbon riding helmet £1,730 / CA$2,700. The pattern: pick the sport the house's clientele actually does, build the hero object in the house's real materials (wood, leather, saddle stitching), price it as sculpture, and let the editorial around it sell the $800 charm.
Sources: https://www.tetongravity.com/hermes-19000-off-piste-skis-luxury-gear-2025/, https://www.hermes.com/cz/en/home/games-and-sports/, https://hermes.cn/cn/en/product/off-piste-pair-of-alpine-skis-H0009037v00, https://www.hermes.com/ca/en/home/games-and-sports/, https://www.hermes.com/uk/en/home/games-and-sports/, https://www.hermes.com/us/en/home/games/, https://www.bosshunting.com.au/lifestyle/design/hermes-pool-table

Why it matters for Salt Lake: Hermès put skis in the catalogue because mountains are part of its customer's life. A ski town's DMO can own the same category with far more legitimacy.

### Loewe (Jonathan Anderson era, then McCollough and Hernandez)

Anderson's 11 years turned Loewe into the house of the craft object and the meme object at once. The animal bag line (elephant, bunny with knotted ears, frog clutch) went "beyond motifs to embody the animals themselves." The tomato clutch began as a 2024 Instagram joke, captioned "Loewe meme to reality," then shipped as nappa lambskin molded over a metal frame with a gold calyx clasp. Loewe Foundation Craft Prize (annual since 2016, **[background, verify]**) anchors the brand's claim to craft. In March 2025 Anderson left for Dior; Jack McCollough and Lazaro Hernandez debuted SS26 in Paris at the Cité Internationale Universitaire with American sportswear built on Spanish heritage, Ellsworth Kelly color, "jeans" made of orange paillettes, and a reimagined Amazona bag. Reviews split between "flawless debut" and "bland."
Sources: https://www.tatlerasia.com/style/fashion/meme-to-reality-loewe-tomato-clutch, https://thefashionography.com/fashion/accessories/loewe-turns-a-tomato-into-its-new-clutch/, https://blog.fashionphile.com/loewe-a-jonathan-anderson-retrospective/, https://www.meer.com/en/81944-jonathan-andersons-transformative-impact-on-loewe, https://www.thezoereport.com/fashion/loewe-spring-summer-2026-show-review, https://www.purseblog.com/loewe/loewe-amazona-bag-for-spring-2026, https://www.anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/17540/jack-mccollough-lazaro-hernandez-loewe-review-spring-summer-2027-ss27

Signal: an internet joke executed at atelier quality becomes a real product. Craft is what makes the joke land.

### Jacquemus

The brand is a single place (the south of France) rendered everywhere: "the brand color palette, the campaign locations, the product names, and the runway settings all reference the same place." Le Chiquito micro bag "was designed to be photographed, not carried." Accessories are an estimated 65 to 70 percent of revenue (single source). The giant Le Bambino "buses" driving through Paris (2023) were CGI by artist Ian Padgham, over 2 million views, and followed a Galeries Lafayette pop-up with oversized bucket hats, Jacquemus toasters and branded washing machines.
Sources: https://femfounded.org/case-studies/jacquemus, https://culted.com/fashion/culted-community-talks-is-jacquemus-a-marketing-genius/, https://retailboss.co/giant-jacquemus-handbags-in-paris-3d, https://malaymail.com/news/life/2022/12/15/designer-jacquemus-from-micro-bag-to-massive-success/45325

Signal: a brand can be a region. Objects (toaster, washing machine, bucket hat) are props that keep the region in frame.

### Bottega Veneta

Matthieu Blazy (2021 to 2025) made "quiet luxury" that was not bland: no logo, intrecciato weave as the signature, and material jokes like nubuck leather dyed to look like flannel, denim and cotton tees (SS24). "There doesn't need to be a logo to guess where the orange intrecciato gloves come from." Louise Trotter's SS26 debut (Milan, September 2025) shrank the intrecciato strips to 3 mm for softer drape and more shine, showed a 4,000-hour leather cape, recycled-glass pieces, and new bags including a leather intrecciato newspaper bag. **[background, verify]**: the brand also made leather "paper bags" and leather "cardboard boxes" under Blazy, the clearest example of merch-as-trompe-l'oeil.
Sources: https://www.savoirflair.com/article/fashion-week/bottega-veneta-spring-summer-2024-runway-review/c280db5a-2d8e-4690-8e48-5224009b7a67, https://www.fzine.com/fashion/matthieu-blazy-new-bottega-veneta-silent-luxury, https://www.laconceria.it/en/luxury/blazy-bottega-veneta-i-explain-you-quiet-luxury/, https://culted.com/show-reviews/bottega-veneta-ss26-louise-trotter/, https://highxtar.com/en/?p=535922, https://www.10magazine.com.au/articles/bottega-veneta-ss26

Signal: technique can be the logo. If your make is recognizable, you don't need the wordmark.

### Prada Linea Rossa

Born 1997 when Patrizio Bertelli set up the Luna Rossa America's Cup campaign; the red stripe is the Prada logo's red line. Early product "recovered shapes, materials and details from the technical uniforms of yachters," chiefly nylon. Relaunched FW18 with "an even stronger focus on performance and innovation" and a high-impact palette; fabrics include Tela Tecnica Pro and heat-sealed seamless garments. Prada later bought naming rights to the challenger series (the Prada Cup).
Sources: https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/22420/la-storia-di-prada-linea-rossa, https://twinfactory.co.uk/?p=42487, https://trussarchive.com/editorials/an-exploration-of-prada-sport, https://ae.fashionnetwork.com/news/Prada-s-Linea-Rossa-sportswear-line-is-back,1010966.html

Signal: a sport sub-brand with its own mark (a stripe, not a logo) built from a real team's kit. The authenticity came from actually racing.

### Miu Miu

The fastest-growing luxury brand of 2024 to 2025: retail sales up 35 percent for FY2025, up 49 percent in H1 2025, now almost a third of Prada Group revenue while the Prada brand fell 1 percent. The spend goes into "long-running cultural programmes" rather than ads: the Miu Miu Literary Club at Milan Design Week (2024 "Writing Life" on Sibilla Aleramo and Alba de Céspedes; 2025 "A Woman's Education" on Simone de Beauvoir and Fumiko Enchi at the Circolo Filologico, April 9 to 10), Summer Reads pop-ups (2024 across fashion capitals; 2025 park "libraries" in Beijing, Hong Kong, Milan, Paris, Osaka) where visitors were gifted special-edition books in city-specific colours with ex-libris stamps. **[background, verify]**: Miu Miu Upcycled (reworked vintage denim and tees) is a recurring capsule.
Sources: https://www.theindustry.fashion/miu-miu-powers-prada-group-growth-with-35-sales-surge/, https://www.laconceria.it/en/luxury/miu-miu-continues-to-drive-prada-group-7-6-while-zegna-drops-2/, https://fashionlawjournal.com/miu-miu-marketing-strategy-gen-z/, https://thefashionography.com/fashion/culture/miu-miu-summer-reads-2025-turns-green-spaces-into-libraries/, https://www.nssmag.com/it/fashion/40227/miu-miu-literary-club-design-week-2025, https://theimpression.com/read-me-wear-me-miu-mius-summer-reads-cultural-escape/

Signal: the "merch" here is a book in branded packaging, city-coded. The product is a point of view, not a logo.

### Balenciaga (Demna's ironic merch)

AW17: the "Balenciaga 2017" logo paraphrased Bernie Sanders' campaign mark (extra underline, off-centre year); critics noted a luxury house upscaling campaign merch that sold for under $25. The same season's Arena tote, blue glazed leather with a zip, was read as the IKEA Frakta; press price £1,705 or $2,145 versus 40p / 99 cents. IKEA's agency had a response ad ready within two hours ("extremely flattered"). Products also carried the Kering corporate logo, a satire of the parent company. By FW18 commentators said Demna had "outgrown irony" with a World Food Programme charity collab. FW24 included an "Erewhon" tee that resells at about 506 euros.
Sources: https://graziadaily.co.uk/fashion/news/balenciaga-made-pfw-great-bernie-themed-collection, https://www.hollywoodreporter.com//news/balenciagas-bernie-sanders-inspired-merch-965478, https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/balenciaga-speed-hunters, https://www.creativereview.co.uk/work/work-ikea-responds-balenciaga-bag-homage/, https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1075001, https://gocirculaire.com/product/29747730

Signal: irony works when the reference is universally recognized and the execution is sincere (real leather, real construction). It also ages fast.

### Gucci x The North Face (2021)

Two drops. First (Jan 2021) '70s-inspired, entry £135. Second (Dec 2021): 130 pieces mixing ready-to-wear, soft accessories, luggage and shoes, "retro-inspired designs drawing on The North Face's '90s clothing and Gucci's archive," $230 to $3,400 (£145 socks to £2,700 silk dress). Trail print sweatshirt retail $1,600, resale $481 to $1,475. Alessandro Michele: "I don't differentiate between beauty and functionality"; the press release framed fashion "as a powerful instrument of freedom."
Sources: https://fashionista.com/2021/12/gucci-north-face-second-collaboration-collection, https://www.theindustry.fashion/gucci-reunites-with-the-north-face-for-second-drop/, https://marieclaire.co.uk/news/fashion-news/north-face-gucci-724931, https://www.thezoereport.com/fashion/the-north-face-x-guccis-collaboration-includes-the-winter-coat-youll-live-in-all-winter-long-52317138, https://stockx.com/gucci-x-the-north-face-sweatshirt-trail-print

Signal: outdoor heritage plus maximalist print. The outdoors brand lent credibility, the fashion house lent desire.

### Louis Vuitton x Supreme (2017)

Unveiled at LV's FW17 menswear show in Paris. Kim Jones: hard to discuss New York menswear without Supreme; the two graphics "worked together as Pop Art." The idea reportedly came from LV CEO Michael Burke. Red leather duffles expected at 1,500 to 3,000 euros; sold only through LV stores and temporary locations, not Supreme's. A plain white LV x Supreme tee went from $485 in store to over $2,500 resale. Notable because LV had long refused to alter the Monogram, and because Supreme's 2000 bootleg LV monogram deck had been pulled after legal action.
Sources: https://www.thefashionlaw.com/the-louis-vuitton-x-supreme-collaboration-is-here/, https://hero-magazine.com/article/84313/its-official-louis-vuitton-has-collaborated-with-supreme, https://stockx.com/news/fr-ca/supreme-x-louis-vuitton-kim-jones/, https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/james-jebbia-bof-interview/

### Dior x Air Jordan 1 (2020)

Kim Jones again. AJ1 High limited to 8,500 pairs, Low to 4,700, retail about $2,000, sold by lottery (opened March 23, winners March 26, then delayed by the pandemic) with access to a pop-up. Resale opened at $13,050 on GOAT, hit about $20,000 on StockX. Sotheby's estimated a size 6 Low at £4,500 to £6,000 later.
Sources: https://hypebae.com/2020/1/dior-nike-collaboration-air-jordan-1-low-high-og-limited-edition-sneakers-kim-jones-release, https://www.nssmag.com/it/fashion/20044/dior-x-jordan, https://sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2021/life-is-beautiful-london/nike-air-jordan-1-low-dior-size-6, https://www.bosshunting.com.au/life-style/limited-edition-dior-air-jordan-1/

Signal: the exact number (8,500) is part of the product. Publish the edition size.

### Supreme

Founded 1994 by James Jebbia as a community space for NYC skaters, not a fashion company. The box logo came from a Barbara Kruger book lent to a friend; the mark is Futura Heavy Oblique in a red box. Thursday drops at physical stores with supply "nowhere near enough to keep up with demand," which is why lines form around the block. Collab roster spans Jeff Koons, Damien Hirst, Miles Davis, The North Face and Louis Vuitton. EssilorLuxottica bought the brand from VF in October 2024 for about $1.5 billion. **[background, verify]**: Jebbia's often-quoted line is roughly "if we can sell 600, we make 400"; weekly drops run Thursday 11am ET / UK, with a seasonal lookbook preview and a Monday week-ahead preview.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_(brand), https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/james-jebbia-bof-interview/, https://www.highsnobiety.com/2015/10/25/james-jebbia-interview-2002/

Philosophy of merch: every tee is a bootleg of something (a logo, an album cover, an ad) rendered on an ordinary 6 oz tee; the value is scarcity plus reference, not fabric.

### Kith

Ronnie Fieg's Brooklyn store (2015, with Snarkitecture) hung 750 cast white Air Jordan 2s from the ceiling and opened Kith Treats, NYC's first cereal bar, built on a childhood craving. Treats expanded to other flagships and a standalone in Shibuya; from 2017 it sold its own apparel capsules and did a Cap'n Crunch collab. Rotating specials come from "friends and family of the brand." **[background, verify]**: Kith runs a near-weekly "Monday Program" drop and an annual "Kithmas" collab calendar, plus Kith Kids and Kith Women lines.
Sources: https://www.complex.com/sneakers/a/brendan-dunne/kith-brooklyn-cereal-bar, https://ca.kith.com/pages/about-treats, https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/zagat/2015/09/28/zagat--satisfy-your-sweet-tooth-at-kith-treats--nyc-s-first-cereal-bar-, https://www.highsnobiety.com/2015/04/28/ronnie-fieg-interview

Signal: a food counter inside the store gives people a reason to come without buying, and the counter then gets its own merch.

### Aimé Leon Dore

Built on "authentic storytelling, a highly defined aesthetic, defining collaborations, and an immersive retail experience" with Café Leon Dore inside the Mulberry Street store; LVMH took a minority stake in 2022. Teddy Santis restored a 1989 Porsche 911 Carrera 4 (964) with Porsche after visiting the museum and the Stuttgart line; it showed at the ALD NYFW show and at Jeffrey Deitch gallery through February 9, 2020, and the car's interior materials carried into the FW20 clothing. Long New Balance partnership (550, T500, etc.); Santis later became New Balance's Made in USA creative director, with a 2026 running capsule of performance apparel, technical outerwear and two performance shoes.
Sources: https://thefashionography.com/fashion/menswear/the-world-of-aime-leon-dore-nostalgia-exclusivity-and-the-new-luxury, https://newsroom.porsche.com/pdf/77e524ef-6f0b-451e-acca-a0d5ac583321, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_Leon_Dore, https://ae.fashionnetwork.com/news/NY-based-fashion-brand-Aime-Leon-Dore-teases-upcoming-collaboration-with-Porsche,1171530.html

Signal: world-building means the café, the car, the sneaker and the hoodie all tell the same Queens-kid-in-a-Loro-Piana-world story. The merch is a souvenir of a mood.

### Palace

London, 2009, Lev Tanju and Gareth Skewis; Tri-Ferg logo by Fergus "Fergadelic" Purcell. Seasonal weekly drops (e.g. Summer 2026 Drop 7 on June 19 at 12:00 BST in UK/EU/US, June 20 in Asia-Pacific), small quantities, "unpredictable product mix" of apparel, decks and accessories; fans value "irreverent humor and authentic skate heritage." Corteiz's Clint reissued a 2002 Brazil tracksuit on the same day as Palace's and made a Tri-Ferg parody tee. **[background, verify]**: Palace's product descriptions are famously nonsense one-liners written in-house (Tanju and team), which is part of why the drops get screenshotted.
Sources: https://www.nssmag.com/it/fashion/18156/guida-completa-a-palace-skateboards, https://hypebeast.com/it/2026/6/palace-skateboards-summer-2026-drop-7-release-info, https://www.nss-sports.com/en/lifestyle/38505/palace-corteiz-tracksuit-brazil-2002

### Corteiz

Clint419's London brand. Password-protected website, surprise pop-ups announced hours ahead, "RULESTHEWORLD" as slogan. The "Great Bolo Exchange": fans swapped their own branded jackets for one of 50 Corteiz Bolo puffers; every jacket collected was donated to the homeless; a Paris repeat drew hundreds. Nike sued in 2021 over the name's similarity to Cortez, then in January 2023 projected both logos on the Oxford Circus flagship and released the Air Max 95 in Gutta Green, Pink Beam and Aegean Storm, exclusive to London, New York and Paris.
Sources: https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/corteiz-nike-air-max-95, https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corteiz, https://thehoxtontrend.com/about-us/release-news-and-blogs/clint419-explains-the-rise-of-his-corteiz-brand-in-rare-interview-with-90s-baby-show/, https://sneakernews.com/?p=1159667

Signal: make the acquisition itself an event with rules. The exchange turned a product launch into a charity stunt and a loyalty test.

### Stüssy

The "industry's favourite collaborator": Dries Van Noten, Martine Rose (car-themed capsule), Levi's, Tekla, Dover Street Market, Nike, New Balance, The North Face, Comme des Garçons, Dior, a quiet PSG kit for the Paris flagship opening in 2022, and a Stüssy x Our Legacy Co-Op zip hoodie with mixed-fabric paneling. Releases land simultaneously in "chapter" stores and online (Summer 2025 on May 9). Design Scene notes the brand "avoids over-styling and sidesteps high-gloss fashion conventions." Estimated revenue near $93 million (unverified).
Sources: https://blog.laced.com/how-stussy-became-the-industrys-favourite-collaborator/, https://www.measureprotocol.com/insights/stussy-four-decades-streetwear-credibility, https://www.designscene.net/2025/05/stussy-summer-2025.html, https://nssmag.com/en/sports/37348/stussy-football-jersey

Signal: a hand-drawn signature as logo, forty years old, never over-explained, and placed on a 8 oz pigment-dyed tee that costs $50 to $60 **[background, verify]**.

### Carhartt WIP

Work In Progress was founded in 1994 by Edwin Faeh **[background, verify on name]** to distribute Carhartt in Europe, then became its own fashion-facing line. The US Detroit Jacket (1954, originally denim, called the "Zipper Jacket" until 1998) is 12 oz ringspun pre-washed cotton duck, blanket-lined, about $110, cut short to clear a toolbelt; "the fabric and color were intentionally developed to get that worn-in look with age." WIP's Detroit uses 12 oz Dearborn canvas, often organic and chalk-washed for a sun-faded effect, corduroy collar, unlined, oversized fit, $248 to $268. Collabs with A.P.C., Vans, Vetements.
Sources: https://www.thrifted.com/blogs/the-edit/the-history-of-the-carhartt-detroit-jacket, https://www.gearpatrol.com/style/a697638/carhartt-detroit-jacket-explainer/, https://www.coggles.com/blog/fashion/carhartt-brand-history/, https://alexkwa.com/carhartt-wip-detroit-jacket-review/, https://www.urbanindustry.co.uk/products/carhartt-wip-og-detroit-jacket-dark-navy-black-stone-canvas

Signal: same silhouette, different wash and fit, double the price. The fabric story (duck canvas that patinas) is the product.

### Noah

Brendon Babenzien (Supreme creative director 1996 to 2004). "This business is radical so I don't need the clothes to be." Highsnobiety: "less about ceaseless hype and high resale prices than perennial style and low stress." Noah's blog breaks down garment cost; he prefers polyester over nylon for shedding reasons, works with ethical mills, uses Italian wools, Japanese selvedge and fine cottons. Monocle: "a sophisticated retail model based on community, transparency and some colourful clothes."
Sources: https://www.mrporter.com/en-at/journal/fashion/introducing-noah-your-new-favourite-brand-1152742, https://monocle.com/magazine/issues/127/crest-of-a-wave, https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/noah-clothing-guide/

Signal: transparency as a brand value. Printing the cost breakdown is merch too.

### Online Ceramics

Alix Ross and Elijah Funk, LA, started as bootleg Grateful Dead lot tees in tie-dye; John Mayer wore one, Dead & Company made it official. Aesthetic: Dead imagery plus Eastern spirituality, counter-culture icons, punk. Funk: "the attention, that's not what we're in it for. We just wanted to make weird stuff." Their A24 horror merch looks like "the back of a VHS tape in 1987." The 2024 book Tricker's Cabin (MACK) describes the shift from a homespun tee business into "a larger, more philosophical art practice." Ross left in 2024; Funk continues.
Sources: https://www.insidehook.com/?p=347222, https://system-magazine.com/issues/issue-22/online-ceramics, https://www.fastcompany.com/91163999/a24-and-online-ceramics-cult-movie-merch, https://us.mackbooks.co.uk/products/trickers-cabin-the-oral-visual-history-of-online-ceramics

Signal: the lot-tee / bootleg format is the most honest merch format there is: hand-drawn, slightly wrong, made by fans. Garment-dyed tees with front-and-back prints are the template.

### Brain Dead

Kyle Ng (LA) and Ed Davis (Australia) met online; founded around 2014. Ng: "a strong graphic tee should carry significance, since it reflects something about the wearer." ### Stone Island

Massimo Osti, 1982, Ravarino. First product was Tela Stella, a heavy resin-coated military canvas; the compass badge went on the first Tela Stella jacket. Carlo Rivetti: "continuous research and experimentation" is the core of the company; the badge ties to the sea and to research. Over 60,000 garment-dye recipes. ## 3. Principles we can steal for a destination brand

1. **The Hermès rule: no product is "merch."** Every object, from the $770 charm to the $19,300 ski, gets the same materials and the same story discipline. For VSL: one standard of make across the $35 cap and the $300 jacket, or don't make the cap.

2. **Run a petit h.** Take the offcuts of the destination (retired trail signs, old ski lift pads, Delta Center seat fabric, Sundance banners) and have local makers turn them into one-of-one objects. "Creation in reverse." Tour it to Park City, the airport, the convention floor. The story writes itself and it photographs.

3. **The carré model: one format, many editions.** A fixed-size bandana or ski buff, one illustrator per edition, one Salt Lake story per print (the inversion, the Great Salt Lake, the grid, the canyons). Collectible, cheap to produce, infinitely extendable, and it's an illustrated story, not a logo.

4. **Publish the edition size.** Dior did 8,500 and 4,700; Corteiz did 50. Numbers are content. "Edition of 801" (area code) on the hem tag.

5. **Technique can be the logo (Bottega).** A recognisable construction detail (a specific chain-stitch, a contrast bar tack in a set colour, a recurring woven label shape) does more than a wordmark. Keep the wordmark small; make the detail unmistakable.

6. **Sport sub-brand with a mark, not a logo (Prada Linea Rossa).** A ski/trail line with its own stripe or badge, built from real local kit (ski patrol, avalanche center, trail crews). Authenticity comes from who actually wears it on the mountain.

7. **Give the merch a room (Kith Treats, Café Leon Dore, Rapha clubhouse).** A counter, a coffee, a repair bench or a wax station in the visitor center is a reason to come without buying, and then the counter gets its own merch. Rapha's warning applies: it has to be a real community thing or it reads as "a retail store in disguise."

8. **Scarcity with rules (Supreme, Corteiz, Palace).** A calendar (Thursday 11am), a place (in the room only, like Bar Pitti and the hotels), or a ritual (bring an old ski jacket, swap it for ours, we donate yours). Make acquisition an event with a story.

9. **Bootleg honesty (Online Ceramics, Supreme).** Lot-tee energy: hand-drawn, front-and-back print, garment-dyed, slightly off. Reference real local culture (the Jazz '97 lineup, the "This Is The Place" sign, KRCL) rather than official tourism iconography. Fans make the best merch; let local artists make ours.

10. **A consistent visual language beats a logo (Cav Empt, Stüssy).** Decide a type system, a palette and a drawing style and hold it for years. Nobody reads a Stüssy tee; they recognise it.

11. **Memes to reality, executed properly (Loewe tomato, Balenciaga IKEA).** If a Salt Lake in-joke (the inversion, the "Utah Jazz in a desert" thing, fry sauce, the liquor-law "Zion curtain") is universally known locally, make the object, but make it well. Irony with cheap construction is just a gift shop.

12. **Curate like DSM and MoMA.** Everything in the shop is vetted by someone with taste; no brand concessions, no filler. If MoMA's curators sign off on a mug, the mug is credible. VSL's edit should be signed by a named local creative, chef or athlete.

13. **Program culture, not ads (Miu Miu).** The Literary Club and Summer Reads are merch (a book in city-coded packaging) that doubles as programming. VSL version: a reading/record/trail-map edition per season, gifted at events, city-stamped.

14. **Transparency as product (Noah).** Print the make story on the hang tag: mill, weight, where it was sewn, what the fabric does. It's cheap and nobody else in destination marketing does it.

15. **Keep the sale in the room where possible.** Bar Pitti caps, Chateau robes, Rapha club kit: merch that can only be bought at the place is proof of presence. Sell the core online, keep one or two items visitor-center only.

## 4. Materials and construction glossary observed

Fabric weights are ounces per square yard for knits; "oz" on canvas and denim is the same unit.

- **Ringspun cotton** (e.g. 6.5 oz Bella+Canvas heavyweight, Comfort Colors): yarn spun from twisted, thinned fibers; softer and stronger than open-end cotton. Standard for mid-tier tees. https://www.customink.com/products/t-shirts/long-sleeve-t-shirts/bella-+-canvas-heavyweight-garment-dyed-long-sleeve-t-shirt/2400200
- **Loopwheel jersey**: knitted slowly on vintage Japanese circular machines with no tension, giving a "flowy, bouncy, stretchy" fabric; Iron Heart runs 6.5 oz, Samurai 7.5 oz; one-washed, runs small, cold wash and hang dry. https://www.witheredfig.com/products/iht-1610l-gry-loopwheel-crew-neck-long-tee-grey, https://theshade.witheredfig.com/2022/08/worn-iron-heart-iht-1610l-6-5oz-loopwheel-tee-shrinkage-sizing-and-wear-review/
- **Heavyweight tee tiers [background, verify]**: 5 to 5.5 oz is standard promo blank (Gildan 5000); 6 to 6.5 oz is "heavyweight" at the blank level (Comfort Colors 1717 is 6.1 oz, Shaka Wear 7.5 oz); 8 to 10 oz is the streetwear-brand tier (Camber 8 oz, many "10 oz" Japanese and LA heavyweights); above 10 oz is a sweatshirt.
- **Garment dye**: sew the garment in greige, then dye the whole thing; yields softer hand, tonal seams, a faded look, slight shade variation; Stone Island claims 60,000 recipes. Comfort Colors and Online Ceramics' tees are the mass version. https://www.stoneisland.com/en-be/about-us.html
- **Pigment dye / chalk wash / enzyme wash**: pigment sits on the surface and fades at seams; chalk wash gives a sun-faded effect (Carhartt WIP Dearborn canvas); enzyme wash uses cellulase to soften and reduce pilling. https://www.urbanindustry.co.uk/products/carhartt-wip-og-detroit-jacket-dark-navy-black-stone-canvas
- **12 oz cotton duck canvas**: Carhartt's Detroit Jacket fabric, built to patina; WIP's version is Dearborn canvas, organic, water-repellent finish. https://www.gearpatrol.com/style/a697638/carhartt-detroit-jacket-explainer/
- **Tela Stella**: Stone Island's original heavy resin-coated military canvas (1982). https://www.stoneisland.com/en-be/about-us.html
- **Tela Tecnica / Tela Tecnica Pro**: Prada Linea Rossa's technical nylon; heat-sealed seamless garments. https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/22420/la-storia-di-prada-linea-rossa
- **Intrecciato**: Bottega's woven leather; Trotter reduced strips to 3 mm. https://www.10magazine.com.au/articles/bottega-veneta-ss26
- **Nappa lambskin over a metal frame**: the Loewe tomato clutch construction. https://thefashionography.com/fashion/accessories/loewe-turns-a-tomato-into-its-new-clutch/
- **Silk twill, 90 x 90 cm, hand-rolled roulotté hem**: the Hermès carré spec. https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/hermes-scarf-history
- **Milo lambskin and Swift calfskin**: Rodeo charm leathers. https://baguseek.com/hermes/product/us/en/rodeo-pegase-pm-charm-H083010CADC
- **Sycamore over ash core**: Hermès Off-Piste ski construction. https://hermes.cn/cn/en/product/off-piste-pair-of-alpine-skis-H0009037v00
- **Ventile**: densely woven long-staple cotton; fibers swell when wet to close the weave; breathable, quiet, not a true waterproof, slow to dry. **[background]** Originally a British wartime fabric; used by Nigel Cabourn, Private White V.C., Norse Projects.
- **Gore-Tex**: licensed ePTFE laminate; the most waterproof and breathable of the three; "noisy and sweaty" per critics; performance depends on the licensee's construction (hence Supreme x TNF, Stüssy x TNF, Gucci x TNF as credibility borrowed from a fabric).
- **Waxed cotton**: water-repellent via paraffin or hybrid wax; reduces breathability, re-waxable, spark resistant, heritage look (Barbour, Filson). **[background]** British Millerain and Halley Stevensons are the two main UK mills.
Sources for the three above: https://putthison.com/proof-your-choices-for-rain-gear-summer-showers/, https://bushcraftuk.com/community/threads/ventile-versus-unlined-waxed-cotton.71006, https://bushcraftuk.com/community/threads/gortex-v-wax-cotton.15121/
- **Chenille patches** [background]: tufted yarn patches, the varsity-jacket standard; used by ALD, Kith, Noah on caps and crewnecks for a collegiate read without screen printing.
- **Bonded / heat-sealed seams** [background]: taped or welded seams on technical shells (Prada Linea Rossa, Stone Island, Arc'teryx-style); the signal is "real gear."
- **Cashmere** [background]: the Loro Piana / ALD tier; a cashmere beanie is the quietest possible flex and the cheapest cashmere object to make.
- **Polyester over nylon**: Noah's choice because nylon sheds in the wash. https://www.mrporter.com/en-at/journal/fashion/introducing-noah-your-new-favourite-brand-1152742
