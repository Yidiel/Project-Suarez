# 03. Performance, Outdoor, Gorpcore and Technical Apparel

Research slice for the Visit Salt Lake merchandise strategy deck. Thesis under test: destination merch should carry the brand's ethos on the garment itself, not just a logo. This is the slice closest to SLC's actual identity (ten-plus resorts within an hour, 2034 Winter Olympics, Great Salt Lake, a dense outdoor-industry base in the valley and Park City).

Method note: 43 web searches run on 2026-10-26. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment, so quotes come from search excerpts. Where a claim rests on background knowledge rather than a retrieved source it is marked "(background, verify)". Prices are the most volatile data and should be re-checked before they go on a slide.

---

## 1. Summary

The outdoor-as-fashion cycle that got labeled "gorpcore" in 2017 has not died, it has settled. The loud phase (Arc'teryx shells over suits, neon Salomons, Gore-Tex everything) peaked around 2022 to 2023, got declared dead in 2024 ("RIP Gorpcore"), and by 2026 the trade press describes a quieter, more integrated version: one or two technical anchor pieces worn with ordinary clothes, earth tones and stone instead of safety yellow, trail runners as a permanent streetwear staple. Outside's September 2026 headline sums it up: "Gorpcore Is Dead. Long Live Outdoor Style." Meanwhile the big players are doubling down on outdoor as a standalone business: Nike relaunched ACG as its own brand in February 2026 with a Basecamp store in Beijing; Adidas opened its first European Terrex flagship in Munich in 2026; Arc'teryx built a 2,400 square meter "Museum" in Shanghai; Salomon made MM6's Heikki Salonen its creative director. The second big story is materials. Fabric names (Polartec, Gore-Tex, Pertex, Ventile, Brewed Protein) now function as brand signals that consumers read on a hang tag, and the PFAS phase-out means every serious shell is being re-engineered in 2025 and 2026. The third story is regional authorship: Adidas Spezial's Gary Aspden proved a sub-brand can be built on the idea that "culture can exist anywhere," Snow Peak's Local Wear makes apparel in and for specific Japanese prefectures, and 66°North turned Icelandic fishermen's oilskins into a Regent Street flagship. For Salt Lake, the lesson is that the city sits in the middle of this entire landscape (Kuhl, Black Diamond, Cotopaxi, Petzl America, Backcountry.com and Skullcandy are all within 40 miles) and owns a registered trademark, "The Greatest Snow on Earth," that has been on roughly half the state's license plates since the 1980s. The merch program should behave like a small outdoor brand, not a souvenir stand: real fabrics, a tight product list, hats done properly, and the Utah story told through specifics (the snow, the lake, the canyons, the industry) rather than a skyline.

### Nike ACG

- Origins: All Conditions Gear launched in 1989 as Nike's outdoor division, built around Tinker Hatfield era hybrids like the Air Mowabb (1991) and Air Deschutz sandal, with the 90s look defined by earth tones, purple and teal, big logos and the triangle mark (background, verify details). - Errolson Hugh era (2014 to 2018): Nike brought in the Acronym co-founder to rebuild the line under NikeLab. The palette went black, the construction went three-layer Gore-Tex with taped seams, 2-in-1 jackets with deployable hoods, the Deploy cargo pant, the Alpine jacket and reflective ACG logos. The launch included the Responder backpack and sold through NikeLab stores in New York, London, Paris, Milan and Tokyo. Hugh's fit philosophy: patterns drafted for a body "about to move," not a body standing straight like a suit. END's coverage of AW17 had the line "all conditions means all conditions." His final collection (Holiday 2018) added a down parka and three-way collar. https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/nikelab-acg-all-conditions-gear-relaunch/ , https://hypebeast.com/2018/11/nike-acg-errolson-hugh-final-collection-lookbook , https://hypebeast.com/2015/11/errolson-hugh-interview
- Arizumi era (2019 to 2025): James Arizumi, from Nike SB, took ACG "back out of the city and into color," reviving the 90s palette and the national-parks storytelling (Smith Summit, Lungs pieces, Mountain Fly shoe). Highsnobiety reported Nike wanted "less tech, more retro ACG sportswear." https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/nike-acg-james-arizumi-interview/
- The 2026 reset: In February 2026 Nike relaunched ACG as a standalone outdoor performance brand, independent of NikeLab, organized around trail running, running, hiking and "exploration," with Nike Trail folding into it. Flagship products are the ACG Ultrafly trail racer and ACG Zegama, plus the Radical AirFlow race tee and Lava Loft down jacket. The first standalone store, ACG Basecamp, opened in Beijing's Sanlitun Taikoo Li. FashionNetwork reports safety orange as the new signature color alongside black, off-white and purple. - Why it matters for a DMO: ACG is the clearest case of a sub-brand that alternated between two valid ethos expressions (black technical vs. color-and-place) and found both audiences. The 2026 version is place-based again (Basecamp, athlete department, named mountains). Nike's other sub-lines (SB, NOCTA, Nike Trail) follow the same model: a tight identity system, a single hero fabric story per season, and drops rather than catalogs.

## 3. Principles for Salt Lake

1. Pick one hero fabric per season and name it on the garment. ACG does Gore-Tex, Satisfy does MothTech, Goldwin does Brewed Protein. A Visit Salt Lake Polartec Alpha hoodie or Pertex Quantum vest with the mill's tag sewn in reads as real gear, not a souvenir.
2. Run a Local Wear program. Snow Peak limits each project to a regional producer. SLC can do one drop per year made with a Utah maker (Kuhl sews some samples locally, local embroiderers, a Wasatch-based cut and sew shop) and say so on the label.
3. Use the trademark. "The Greatest Snow on Earth" was registered after a fight with Ringling Bros. and has sat on roughly half of Utah's plates. It is the only ski slogan in America with that level of ambient recognition. License it from the state or Ski Utah and put it on a quality beanie, not a novelty tee. https://www.deseret.com/1996/2/26/19227327/ski-utah-license-plates-may-whoosh-into-history
4. Name things after places, the Spezial way. Blackburn SPZL, Manchester 89 SPZL. Think a "Cottonwood" fleece, a "Little Cottonwood" beanie, an "Antelope Island" tee, a "Bonneville" cap. Culture can exist anywhere.
5. Be quiet. The post-gorpcore palette is stone, navy, black and earth. Reserve one accent (safety orange is now ACG's, so pick something ownable: a Great Salt Lake pink, a snow-white tonal) and keep logos small or tonal. The Beams x Arc'teryx move of making a partner an official colorway name is a cheap, clever way to co-brand.
6. Collab with the neighbors, not with luxury. Prada x AspenX is a $5,400 flex that fits Aspen. SLC's credibility is its industry. A Visit Salt Lake x Cotopaxi Del Día pack, x Black Diamond beanie, x Kuhl fleece or x Petzl headlamp strap tells the real story: the gear industry lives here.
7. Make the hat the hero. Ciele built a brand on one cap. Resort merch is 80 percent hats. Do three: a Yupoong 7005 style 5-panel in cotton with a woven label, a Richardson 112 style trucker with a felt or leather patch for the ski-town register, and a merino cuffed beanie for the 2034 register.
8. Build in upcycling and repair. Jackson Hole x ReFleece turned staff uniforms into merch; Arc'teryx's Museum has a reCARE desk; Patagonia's Worn Wear is the gold standard. A "patched" program using retired Visit Salt Lake uniforms or resort banners is both ethos and content.
9. Give the merch a clubhouse. Tracksmith's Trackhouse, ACG Basecamp, Terrex Mountain Loft, Arc'teryx Museum. A pop-up at the Visitor Center or a rotating resort-base kiosk with an athlete department vibe sells more than a web shop.
10. Price in two tiers only. Montbell wins on "humble, durable, shockingly affordable." Satisfy wins on "no excuses." A DMO program should have an accessible tier ($25 to $45 hats and tees) and a gear tier ($120 to $220 fleece, vest, shell) with nothing in between and nothing above.
11. Sell the amateur spirit. Tracksmith's whole brand is that serious hobbyists deserve serious kit. SLC's audience is the person who skis 20 days a year on a lunch break. Copy and product should speak to them, not to pros.
12. Tell the PFAS story honestly. By 2026, state bans and Gore's ePE shift mean a "made without intentionally added PFAS" line on a shell is table stakes and a trust signal, especially for a Great Salt Lake narrative about water. https://www.gore-tex.com/technology/new-products
13. Treat 2034 as a product calendar, not a logo. Build toward it with a numbered annual drop (2027, 2028...) so the Olympic year is a culmination, and keep official Olympic marks out of it until the rights situation is clear.
14. Make the garment carry the ethos in the construction, not the print. A gusseted pant (Gramicci), a reflective tape (And Wander), a green under-visor (Ebbets), a Coolmax liner (Alta Store cap) are details people notice and talk about. That is the thesis of the deck in one line.

## 4. Materials glossary

Cost tier key: $ (commodity, under about $8/yd or low per-unit uplift), $$ (branded mid, roughly $10 to $25/yd), $$$ (premium branded, $25 to $60/yd), $$$$ (exotic, over $60/yd or limited supply). Tiers are rough and for relative comparison only.

| Material | What it is | Who uses it | What it signals | Cost tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polartec Alpha / Alpha Direct | Lofted knit insulation first built for US Special Operations around 2012; Alpha Direct needs no inner lining, extremely breathable, minimal wind resistance, snags easily | Norrøna Lofoten Alpha Raw, FW Manifest hoodie, ROA, Montane, Patagonia Nano-Air (Alpha family) | Active insulation, "I know gear," the current hype fleece | $$$ |
| Polartec Power Grid | Patented grid-back fleece for compressibility and wicking, placed in high-sweat zones | Patagonia R1, Arc'teryx Delta, Dainese | Technical base and mid layer, quiet competence | $$ |
| Polartec Thermal Pro | High-loft knit fleece, best warmth-to-weight, the "sherpa" and "high pile" looks | Norrøna, FW Root Sherpa, Patagonia Retro-X type pieces | 90s outdoor nostalgia, cozy, the fleece that looks like fleece | $$ |
| Polartec Windbloc | Fleece with a windproof membrane laminated inside | Classic 90s softshells, Marmot, Mountain Hardwear | Old-school ski-patrol seriousness | $$ |
| Gore-Tex Pro | 3-layer ePTFE laminate for the hardest shells, most durable and most expensive | Arc'teryx Alpha SV, ACG Hugh-era jackets | The top of the mountain; also now a PFAS conversation | $$$$ |

## 5. Hats and headwear

- Shapes and registers: the 5-panel camper (Yupoong 7005, 100 percent cotton, soft buckram front that keeps patches flat, wholesale roughly $9.65 at 1,000 units to $17.67 at 24, retail $25 to $27) reads skate and trail. The 6-panel unstructured dad cap reads collegiate and running. The trucker (Richardson 112, 60/40 cotton-poly front, polyester mesh back, "the most-decorated cap in America") reads ski town and rodeo. The cuffed beanie is the ski resort default. A ski cap with a pom is the retro register. Balaclavas are the current fashion-forward winter item (Moncler, Satisfy) but a hard sell for a DMO. Bucket hats are the And Wander and Terrex summer trail item. https://www.blankcaps.com/baseball-hats/reviews , https://www.jonestshirts.com/products/y7005-yupoong-classic-jockey-camper-cap
- Beanie fiber: merino is warmer for its weight, resists odor and does not itch at fine micron counts, but costs 3 to 5 times an acrylic blank; acrylic pills and holds smell but is the industry blank for $3 to $6. A Smartwool or Woolpower merino cuffed beanie is the "gear" tier; an acrylic cuffed beanie with a woven label is the accessible tier. (Background on fiber tradeoffs; the search for comparative sources returned no independent reporting.)
- Decoration: direct flat embroidery is cheapest and fastest (2 to 3 weeks vs 4 to 6 for patches), best for a clean logo. Woven patches reproduce fine text down to about 0.15 inches and up to 12 colors but read flat. Embroidered patches are raised and "more premium." Patch cost runs about $0.30 to $1.50 each plus $0.50 to $1.00 to sew. Chain stitch (the vintage western lettering look) is a separate, hand-guided premium that pairs with the trucker register. Leather and felt patches suit ski-town caps. https://griwolfe.com/blogs/guides/embroidery-vs-patch-hats , https://rushpatch.com/blogs/news/woven-patch-vs-embroidered-patch-whats-the-difference-detailed-elaboration-nbsp
- Premium blanks and makers: Ebbets Field Flannels (Seattle, wool caps with the green satin under-visor, around $49 retail, licensing-friendly), Fairends (New York, waxed cotton and wool camper caps), New Era custom program, Ciele (Montreal, GOCap running hat, the model for a single hero hat), Alta Store's Coolmax-lined cap at $26.95 as a resort reference point. https://www.thealtastore.com/page/page/1761744.htm
