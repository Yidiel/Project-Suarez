# 04. Destination merch benchmarks and the Salt Lake City symbol bank

Research note for the Visit Salt Lake merch deck. Written 2026-10-26.

How this was built: web search ran for the first two rounds (about 30 queries), then the session's shared search budget ran out and direct page fetches (visitsaltlake.com, skiutah.com, wikipedia, ksl.com) were refused by the network proxy. So the benchmark section leans on what the searches returned, and the symbol bank mixes those search results with background knowledge. Every claim that comes from memory rather than a fetched source is tagged **(verify)**. Those are mostly dates and numbers, and they are the kind of thing a 20-minute pass with a browser will settle before the deck ships. Nothing in the deck should quote a number tagged verify without that pass.

## 1. Summary

Destination merch splits cleanly into two worlds. The official world (DMOs, tourism boards, chambers) mostly sells safe logo goods through a visitor center or a Shopify page, and almost none of it is fashion-credible. The exceptions are instructive: Pittsburgh handed its official city merch to a local union screen printer and pulled the icon from an 1800s merchant flag; Tokyo built an actual product program ("Tokyo Tokyo" omiyage, 50-plus products with partner makers and a Haneda airport store); Parks Project turned public land into a profitable giving brand; Only NY licensed the NYC Parks leaf and the MTA to make city infrastructure wearable. The unofficial world is where city identity actually gets cool: Chicago's 1917 flag on everything, Portland Gear's "P", Detroit vs. Everybody, Dirty Coast in New Orleans, Aspen and Telluride crewnecks as class signals, Beams Japan treating prefectures like designers. The lesson for Visit Salt Lake is that the credible move is not a logo tee but a point of view carried in the graphic, the material, and the story on the hangtag, and that VSL should behave more like a licensor and curator (Pittsburgh, NYC Parks, Tokyo Tokyo) than a souvenir shop. Salt Lake also has an unusually deep symbol bank: a pink lake, a brine shrimp economy, an earthwork, salt flats, the lightest snow in the country, a grid with a literal origin point, a seagull miracle, a beehive, the biggest hole humans ever dug, a new sego lily flag, and two Olympic Games. Most of that has never been put on a garment with any taste. Visit Salt Lake's 2026 "America's Mountain City" positioning and the 2034 Winter Games give the whole thing a clock.

## 2. DMO and city merch benchmarks

Verdict scale: **credible** (people who care about clothes would wear it), **functional** (fine, souvenir grade), **not credible** (logo on a blank, nobody outside a conference lanyard wears it).

### Salt Lake and Utah officials

**Visit Salt Lake (visitsaltlake.com).** Searches did not surface an online store. Nothing indexed points to a VSL merch shop, which matches what the brief suspects. Background knowledge says VSL runs a Visitor Information Center at the Salt Palace Convention Center (90 S West Temple) that sells gifts and local goods **(verify, and verify whether anything there is VSL-branded)**. Brand history is well documented:
- June 2021: "West of Conventional" brand launched with agency BVK. Logo is a salt crystal meant to show the city's many facets; the idea is unconventional pairings (startups beside wilderness, etc.). Activations included a ten-mural tour with a branded t-shirt reward, which is the one documented piece of VSL apparel. (KSL, VSL press release, PCMA, Simpleview.)
- July 7, 2026: "America's Mountain City" campaign and title. Pitch: the only US city with a major urban core inside a mountain range, resorts about 40 minutes from downtown, all 2034 Olympic venues within an hour. VSL is registering the slogan with the USPTO. Axios says the title was inspired by a Fodor's piece with similar wording; Deseret says nearly 500 outlets picked it up. (Utah Business, Axios, KSL, Deseret, Recommend, Convention Southwest.)
- Naming: the DMO uses "Salt Lake" (county-wide DMO and CVB for Salt Lake County) rather than "Salt Lake City." That matters for merch. "Salt Lake" reads as a place and a lake at once, and it is the version locals actually say.
- Verdict: **no merch program to judge.** The salt crystal mark from 2021 is usable raw material, and "America's Mountain City" is a phrase that could live on a garment the way "Life Elevated" never did.

**Ski Utah (skiutah.com/store).** The site has a Store item in its navigation. The documented product is a Ski Utah vintage tee made with Wild Tribute (an apparel company that gives a share of sales to parks and public lands), per Ski Utah's 2021 gift guide. "The Greatest Snow on Earth" is a registered trademark of the State of Utah, held since 1975 per Ski Utah, and used with permission. Verdict: **functional.** The Wild Tribute collab is the right instinct (a maker with a mission, vintage cut) but it is one tee, not a program. The trademark means any VSL use of the phrase needs state permission, which is a conversation, not a blocker.

**Utah Office of Tourism, "Life Elevated."** Adopted 2006, replacing "Utah! Where Ideas Connect" (2001). The style guide at travel.utah.gov/the-utah-brand describes a 2026 refresh of the Life Elevated logo with more vertical proportions, clearer geometry, and wedge serifs as a nod to the Old West. Partner newsletters in July and August 2026 flag the updated logo. Current campaigns: Parks & Beyond, Ski & Winter, Urban & Outdoors. Utah Pride 2026 even borrowed it ("Pride Elevated"). No evidence of a UOT merch shop. Background knowledge: the Life Elevated identity work is associated with Salt Lake agency Struck and later Love Communications **(verify)**. Verdict: **not a merch player**, but the tagline has 20 years of equity and the state owns it, so VSL should not try to out-tagline it; build under it or beside it.

**Visit Park City.** Searches found only shopping guides. Logo wear in Park City comes from Dugins West (souvenir shop with Park City logo caps), Park City Clothing Company (550 Main), and Park City Mountain's own resort shop. Vail-owned resort merch dominates. Verdict: **functional.** Nothing a Main Street Arc'teryx or Aether customer would buy as fashion, which is exactly the gap a Salt Lake program could fill with a straight face.

**Explore Utah Valley.** Not researched before the budget ran out. Assume no merch of note **(verify)**.

### US benchmark DMOs and official city programs

**I Love NY (New York State).** Milton Glaser sketched the mark in 1976 for a 1977 state tourism campaign and reportedly gave it away. New York State's economic development agency holds the trademark, including for apparel, and runs licensing; there is an official online store **(verify URL)**. The March 20, 2023 "We ❤️ NYC" refresh by designer Graham Clifford for the Partnership for New York City switched to a blocky sans serif and an emoji-style shaded heart. The backlash was immediate: designers mocked the spacing, Adobe's design chief said it lacked anything timeless, memes everywhere, and officials had to clarify it was not replacing the original. Lesson: the original is credible because of restraint and because it was made by a real designer, and the refresh failed because it looked like a committee's idea of friendly. (Surface, Dezeen, Campaign, Creative Bloq, The Fashion Law.) Verdict: original **credible**, refresh **not credible**.

**Pittsburgh, "Printed in Pittsburgh" (2023).** The city's first official merch line, made with CommonWealth Press (founded 2002 by Dan and Shannon Rugh in a South Side basement). Union-printed, officially licensed, co-branded with the city, shirts from $30, launched at Point State Park on July 4, 2023, with a share of revenue going to the city's general fund. The main icon was pulled from the city's 1800s merchant flag flown on river vessels; the Rughs got access to the city sign shop and archives. Official items carry a tag or decal to distinguish them from the sea of unofficial Pittsburgh goods. (Pittsburgh Magazine, WPXI, Print and Promo Marketing, ASI.) Verdict: **credible and the closest model for VSL.** A public body licensing a trusted local maker, with archival graphics and a visible "official" marker, is the playbook.

**Visit Detroit.** No DMO merch surfaced. The city's merch identity belongs to Detroit vs. Everybody, started 2012 by graphic designer Tommey Walker during the Kilpatrick scandal and bankruptcy years, first a Greektown boutique, then Eastern Market, now a flagship in The District Detroit, plus a 2025 "313 vs. Everybody" history line with rugs featuring Faygo and Tiger Stadium that debuted at the Detroit Historical Museum. (Bridge Detroit, Metro Times, Blac Media.) Verdict: unofficial brand **credible**; the museum pop-up is a model for how an institution can borrow a street brand's credibility without owning it.

**Chicago (the flag economy).** The 1917 flag (two blue stripes, four red six-pointed stars) is on everything: WBEZ reported it in 2011 and attributed the spread to younger professionals and students who moved in from the 1990s on and adopted the flag as a marker of belonging, plus ex-Chicagoans using it to signal roots. Retail runs from Dick's to the Chicago Architecture Center shop (distressed flag youth tee) to indie printers like Transit Tees **(verify)**. Nobody licenses it, which is the point: a public-domain, well-designed symbol becomes a commons. Verdict: **credible by default** because the design is good and the city does not police it. Salt Lake's 2020 sego lily flag is the obvious analog and it is still mostly unworn.

**Portland.** Travel Portland sells nothing notable. Portland Gear was founded Black Friday 2014 by Marcus Harvey after he lost a screen printing job, built off the @Portland Instagram handle; first product was the "P" tee with "Portland, Oregon" on the back; sold 200 shirts to four countries on launch night; now bags and a retail footprint, with a memoir, Product of the People. (Wikipedia, UO Alumni, KOIN.) Verdict: **credible.** The "P" is a monogram that stands in for a city, which is what a strong "SL" or "801" mark could do.

**Austin, Denver, Boise, Bozeman, Nashville, New Orleans.** Searches for these were cut off by the budget. From background knowledge **(verify each)**: "Keep Austin Weird" was coined by Red Wassenich in 2000 and is trademarked by Outhouse Designs, which is why it is on a thousand tees and none are official. Denver's merch identity rides on the Colorado state flag "C" (Aksels and others), not on Visit Denver. Nashville's credible local label is Project 615. New Orleans has Dirty Coast (since 2005, "Be a New Orleanian Wherever You Are") and Fleurty Girl, both locals-first brands that tourists buy because locals wear them. Boise and Bozeman mostly run on state-name merch ("Idaho", "Montana") sold by local shops rather than DMOs. Verdict pattern: the credible stuff is always unofficial and locals-first.

**Jackson Hole.** The bucking horse and rider is Wyoming's state trademark (on plates since 1936). Jackson Hole Mountain Resort sells a woven bronco patch marketed as "local pride." MADE, a locally owned shop group, sells bronco glassware and small goods. (JHMR retail, MADE, WyoHistory.) Verdict: **functional to credible.** A state-owned symbol everybody is allowed to love is doing the same job the Chicago flag does.

### International benchmarks

**Tokyo, "Tokyo Tokyo Old meets New."** Logo and slogan decided April 2017 after public input. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government then ran the Tokyo Omiyage Project: more than 50 souvenirs across crafts, stationery, and food, made with partner businesses (fifth collection July 2020 with YAGI & CO. and Tokyo Chainsaws), and opened the Tokyo Tokyo Official Souvenir Shop at Haneda's international terminal on February 22, 2020, selling daruma, maneki-neko, Tokyo Tower models, tees, mugs. (TMG, Japan Today, Business Wire, Kyodo PR.) Verdict: **credible as a program**, uneven as product. The structure (a city as curator and co-publisher of local makers, with an airport retail anchor) is a direct model for VSL and the new SLC airport.

**Beams Japan.** A Tokyo retailer (Beams, flagship Shinjuku, five floors) that treats Japanese regions like brands: rotating regional focus (Okinawa, Hokkaido), a Beppu City collaboration where Beams buyers curated 16 local businesses into souvenirs sold in Shinjuku, a JTB travel tie-up (JTBeams) for a 2018 Hokkaido campaign, a MAFF agriculture project, pop-ups in Paris and at Fred Segal LA. (Time Out, Openers, Inside Retail, MR Magazine.) Verdict: **credible.** This is what "curator" looks like: a taste-making retailer puts its name on a region's goods and the region gets instant credibility.

**Sou Sou (Kyoto).** Background knowledge **(verify)**: founded 2002, textile designer Katsuji Wakisaka (ex Marimekko), modern tabi and split-toe shoes, numerals pattern; sells Kyoto to the world without a single landmark on the product. Verdict: **credible.** Place carried by craft, not by icon.

**I amsterdam.** The letters came down from Museumplein on December 3, 2018 because they drew crowds and, per the GroenLinks councillor, sent the wrong message; they now tour events and sit at Schiphol. (Dezeen, DutchReview.) Verdict: the brand is **functional**, and the lesson is that a city mark can succeed so hard it becomes an overtourism symbol. Salt Lake is nowhere near that problem, but the Great Salt Lake story has a related tension: you can sell a dying lake only if you are also seen to be helping it.

**Visit Oslo, Copenhagen, Visit Iceland, Tourism Australia.** Searches returned only retail guides (Way Nor tax-free souvenir chain in Oslo, Royal Copenhagen porcelain, the Icelandic Handcraft & Wool shop). None of these boards runs a merch shop worth naming **(verify)**. Background knowledge: Visit Oslo's 2024 "Is it even a city?" film was a creative hit without merch; Iceland's de facto national merch is 66°North, a real performance brand; Tourism Australia's 2022 "Come and Say G'day" mascot Ruby the kangaroo has had limited product life **(verify all three)**. Verdict: **not merch players**, which confirms that brand fame and merch credibility are separate things; the brand that sells is the one with real clothing chops.

### The non-official "cool local" model

- **Only NY** (founded 2007 by Micah Belamarich and Julian Goldstein, flagship Lower East Side, store in Greenpoint). Its NYC Parks logo tee (the leaf) has been a bestseller for a decade; a Spring 2019 Parks capsule honored Washington Square, Prospect, Central Park; an ongoing MTA partnership (tees, hoodies, hats, totes, and an MTA lamp that is a top home product); a three-way NYCFC x NYC Parks collection built on Flushing Meadows and the Unisphere; other collabs with Brooklyn Brewery, New Museum, Google. (NYC Tourism "Makers of NYC", onlyny.com, NYCFC.) Verdict: **the most credible city brand in America**, and the reason is that it licenses public infrastructure and treats it like a heritage logo. Salt Lake's equivalents: UTA TRAX, the county parks, Antelope Island, the Olympic Oval.
- **Parks Project** (2014, Keith Eshelman and Sevag Kazanci, ex TOMS). Started as trail cleanups, became a giving brand that donates to 30-plus park conservancies; contributed revenue went from about $1,000 in 2014 to over $2 million by 2021 and nearly $3 million by 2026. The May 2021 Vans x Parks Project capsule (Old Skool, Sk8-Hi, Authentic, Slip-On, UltraRange, each embossed "Leave It Better Than You Found It") gave $250,000 to the National Parks Conservation Association and sold through Nordstrom and Urban Outfitters. (Orange Coast, REI podcast, Fashionista, Field Mag, Sneaker News.) Verdict: **credible.** Public land plus a vintage graphic language plus a real giving number.
- **Pendleton national park blankets.** The first was the Glacier Park blanket in September 1916, commissioned by railroad builder James J. Hill for his park stores; Yellowstone 1923, Grand Canyon 1926 or 1927, Rainier 1928; "points" on the blankets through the mid 1930s indicated size. (Pendleton blog.) Verdict: **credible for a century.** Lesson: a destination object can be a 100-year product if the maker is real and the design is restrained.
- **Los Angeles cluster.** Madhappy (2017, "a community of optimists," Local Optimist magazine, Madhappy Foundation 501(c)(3), Melrose flagship November 2023). Aviator Nation (2006, Paige Mycoskie, garage in Venice, Abbot Kinney store 2009, made in California with a downtown LA factory). Mister Green (2015, Ariel Stark-Benz, online first, then Silver Lake store; "progressive lifestyle" head shop with incense, home goods, and a 2019 made-in-LA "GEAR" line at $20 to $230 built around Stoney Point climbing history). Jungmaven (hemp, cut and sewn in LA). Free & Easy (Venice, "Don't Trip" caps) and the LA Original program (an LA County economic development licensing brand for local makers) could not be verified before the budget ran out **(verify)**. Verdict: these are **credible** because each one sells a mood of the place (optimism, Venice sun, Silver Lake stoner domesticity, hemp) rather than a landmark.
- **Brooklyn Industries, Bred (Baltimore), Pittsburgh Clothing Co.** Not confirmed by search. Background: Brooklyn Industries was founded 1998 by Lexy Funk and Vahap Avsar, began with messenger bags cut from vinyl billboards, used a smokestack-skyline logo **(verify)**. Treat as a cautionary tale: a borough-as-brand label that got big, then generic.

### Ski and mountain towns

Searches on Telluride, Sun Valley, Big Sky, Whistler, Chamonix, Verbier, Niseko, and Hakuba were cut off by the budget. What the Aspen searches showed: the market is flooded with "Aspen" crewnecks at $22 to $100 on Etsy, eBay, PacSun, Tillys, and Abercrombie, including bachelorette and "vintage inspired" versions, with TikTok clips calling an Aspen sweater viral. Celebrity coverage from the 2025-26 season (Bella Hadid, Kate Hudson, Mariah Carey) focuses on fur, puffers, and boots, not the sweatshirt. Footwear Magazine's "brands that rule Aspen" ranked Moncler first, Canada Goose second, Arc'teryx third, Ralph Lauren ninth (Western and Americana). Background knowledge **(verify)**: the "Aspen" collegiate-letter crewneck became a class signal because the town name itself is the luxury brand; Kith has done Aspen capsules; local shops (Pitkin County Dry Goods, Aspen Skiing Co's own shops) sell the "real" version while mall brands sell the fake one. Same mechanics in Telluride (the Telluride Trappings and Toggery type shops), Sun Valley (the sun logo), Jackson Hole (the bronco), Whistler and Chamonix (town-name hoodies sold by every shop on the main drag), Niseko and Hakuba (Australian and local snowboard shops, Rhythm Japan). The split that matters for Salt Lake: resorts and tourism bodies sell resort-logo goods; local shops sell town-name goods; the town-name goods are what people actually wear in the city after the trip. Alta and Snowbird have cult town-name status among skiers (Alta's "Alta is for skiers" slogan, the Snowbird tram graphic), and Park City has polish but less cult. Salt Lake can own the thing nobody else has: the city that contains the ski towns.

## 3. Principles for a destination brand

1. **License, don't manufacture.** Pittsburgh, NYC Parks, the MTA, Tokyo Tokyo, and Beams Japan all work because the public body or curator lends a mark and an archive to a maker who already has taste and a customer. VSL's job is the archive, the story, the official tag, and the partner choice.
2. **Pull graphics from the archive, not from the logo file.** Pittsburgh's icon came from an 1800s merchant flag. Only NY's bestseller is the Parks leaf. Salt Lake has a base-and-meridian marker, a 1913 seagull, a 2002 snowflake, Saltair postcards, a 1899 Salt Palace, Kennecott type, and a new flag. Use those.
3. **The city name beats the slogan.** "Aspen," "Portland," "Detroit," "Chicago" (via flag) do the work. Taglines age (Life Elevated is 20 years old). "Salt Lake" is the asset; "America's Mountain City" is the hangtag copy.
4. **One good mark can become a commons.** The Chicago flag spread because the design is good and nobody polices it. The sego lily flag could do the same if VSL seeds it instead of gatekeeping it.
5. **Locals first, visitors follow.** Dirty Coast, Only NY, Portland Gear, Detroit vs. Everybody all sold to residents first; tourists buy what locals wear. A VSL line should be stocked where locals shop (Sugar House, 9th and 9th, the ski shops) before the airport.
6. **Carry a mood, not a landmark.** Madhappy sells optimism, Mister Green sells Silver Lake, Sou Sou sells Kyoto craft, none use a skyline. Salt Lake's mood is dry air, big light, salt, and early mornings.
7. **Tie a real number to a real place.** Parks Project's $3 million and Vans's $250,000 are why the vintage graphics feel earned. A Great Salt Lake line without a measurable give to the lake is just pink.
8. **Make the official marker visible but quiet.** Pittsburgh's tag or decal, Only NY's license line, Tokyo Tokyo's logo on the omiyage. A woven label that says "Official. Salt Lake." is enough.
9. **Respect the state's marks and build beside them.** "The Greatest Snow on Earth" is state-owned (1975), Olympic marks are untouchable, Life Elevated is UOT's. Salt Lake can reference the snow science and the Games years (2002, 2034) without the trademarked phrases.
10. **Refreshes fail when they get friendlier.** We ❤️ NYC and I amsterdam both lost by being more cheerful and more generic than the original idea. Keep the typography serious.
11. **Objects outlast apparel.** Pendleton's 1916 blanket, the MTA lamp, Tokyo's daruma. Plan one non-garment hero (a salt dish, a blanket, a map) per capsule.
12. **Resort merch is not city merch.** Park City Mountain, Snowbird, and Alta sell resort goods well. Salt Lake's line should never look like a lift ticket; it should look like the city those resorts share.
13. **A program needs a retail anchor.** Tokyo Tokyo's Haneda store, Beams Japan's Shinjuku floor, Pittsburgh's Point State Park launch. For VSL that is the new SLC airport and the Salt Palace, plus one credible local stockist.

## 5. Local makers map

All of the below is background knowledge from before the budget ran out and needs a verification pass (addresses, current status, and whether they take small runs) **(verify)**.

**Screen printers and decorators (Salt Lake County)**
- Spilt Ink, Salt Lake City: long-running local screen printer with a design-forward reputation; the first call for a 200 to 500 unit test run.
- Other local print shops to vet: Hive Screen Printing, Kingsmen Screen Printing, Impact Screen Printing, Ultimate Tees. Confirm which do water-based and discharge inks and which can handle fleece.
- Embroidery and hats: there are several Utah County and Salt Lake promo-grade embroiderers; for a fashion-grade cap it is more realistic to use a national hat maker (see file 05) and have a local shop do finishing.
- Cut and sew: Utah's apparel manufacturing is thin. Kuhl and Cotopaxi produce overseas; local small-run sewing exists for outdoor hardgoods (Voile makes bindings and splitboards in Salt Lake) but not fashion garments at scale. Expect cut and sew to be LA.

**Utah brands worth naming in the deck (credibility halo and possible partners)**
- Kuhl, Salt Lake City: founded 1983 as Alfwear by Kevin Boyle; mountain apparel; big local employer.
- Cotopaxi, Salt Lake City: 2014, Davis Smith; the Del Día color-scrap packs; B Corp; the most fashion-adjacent Utah brand and the obvious collab partner for a bag.
- Black Diamond, Salt Lake City: moved from California in 1991 under Peter Metcalf, Chouinard Equipment lineage; climbing and ski hardgoods plus apparel.
- Petzl America, West Valley City; Backcountry.com and Skullcandy, Park City; Goal Zero, Bluffdale; Rossignol's US headquarters, Park City; Voile, Salt Lake.
- Roam Industry, Salt Lake City: outdoor shop with its own Utah-graphic apparel line; the kind of local stockist that would carry a VSL line.
- Wild Tribute (Ski Utah's tee partner): gives a share to public lands, Utah-connected.

**Artists and designers known for Salt Lake graphics**
- Trent Call (SLC muralist and illustrator), Jann Haworth (Pop artist, co-creator of the Sgt. Pepper cover, long based in Utah, led the SLC Utah Women 2020 mural), Sri Whipple, and the muralists from VSL's own 2021 West of Conventional mural tour are the first list.
- Agencies: Struck (Salt Lake, the historic Life Elevated work and lots of tourism), Modern8 (Randall Smith; the city's blue-chip identity studio), Super Top Secret (Salt Lake, brand and packaging), Fuel (Salt Lake design studio), Think Tank Creative (Utah), Love Communications (UOT's agency of record), BVK (Milwaukee, VSL's 2021 brand agency), and Dakota (Salt Lake brand studio). For merch, a studio that has done apparel graphics matters more than a studio that has done logos.

**Institutions with licensable archives**
- Utah State Historical Society and the University of Utah Marriott Library (Saltair photographs, Salt Palace, rail ephemera); Utah Museum of Fine Arts (Spiral Jetty stewardship with Dia); Utah Division of Wildlife Resources Great Salt Lake Ecosystem Program (brine shrimp science); Utah Olympic Legacy Foundation (2002 venues); Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation; Utah Transit Authority (TRAX and FrontRunner marks, the Only NY x MTA move).
