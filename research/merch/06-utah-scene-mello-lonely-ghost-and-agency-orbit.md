# 06. The Utah scene: Mello, Lonely Ghost, the REDWEST drop, and the agency orbit

Research slice for the Visit Salt Lake merch strategy deck. Thesis under test: destination merch should carry the city's ethos on the garment and be designed at the level of a fashion label. This file maps who in Utah already works that way, who nationally they look up to, which studios make these drops happen, and what the scene is picking up on in late 2025 and 2026.

Method note: all findings come from web search (extended mode) run on 2026-10-26. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment, so anything that only lives on Instagram, TikTok or behind a Shopify storefront is flagged as unverified. Two items in the brief (the identity of "Mello" and a "Lonely Ghost x Red West" drop) could not be confirmed as described; both sections say exactly what was and was not found so the deck does not overstate.

---

## 1. Summary

Utah already has a working model for what Visit Salt Lake wants to do. Lonely Ghost, founded in Provo in 2019 by Indy Blue Severe and Bronson Christensen, turned a text-message phrase ("text me when you get home," "I love you, say it back") into a multi-million dollar DTC label with a grocery-store flagship at Riverwoods, a second shop in Scottsdale, an exclusive national retail deal with Pacsun (August 2025), and a 2025 collab run that included Merrell (first footwear), Dutch Bros, HI-CHEW, Béis and RISE Festival. It was also the presenting sponsor of the REDWEST country festival at the Utah State Fairpark in October 2025 with its own VIP+ Lounge stage. That festival is the "Red West" in the brief: REDWEST SLC, launched October 2024 with AEG as producer, is a homegrown Utah event whose merch ecosystem includes an official "REDWEST x Gigi Pip" trucker hat line from the Salt Lake hat brand and a "REDWEST Merch World" on site. No indexed source confirms a standalone Lonely Ghost x REDWEST apparel drop; the verifiable relationship is sponsorship plus lounge activation. "Mello" as a Utah creative agency could not be located; the only merch-focused Mello is Mello MFG in Austin, Texas, a design-to-fulfillment custom merch studio founded by Zach Mainello whose model (design projects from $3,795, "fashion drop" style retail merch for hotels and restaurants) is still a useful comp. The deeper Utah design credential is Actual Source in Provo (JP Haynie and Davis Ngarupe), which did the identity and Thermal typeface for Virgil Abloh's NikeLab Re-Creation Center in Chicago, an A24 Books imprint identity completed in 2026, Hotel Drugs identity and apparel, and its own apparel including a spring collection for Fresh Service Japan. Around them sit Gigi Pip (Salt Lake hats, roughly $16M est. revenue, a downtown "SLC Speakeasy" hat bar opened October 2025), Fice (Utah's only Tier One Nike account, Utah Jazz collabs, pivoting to community events and art shows), Thrifthood (Provo and now downtown SLC, vintage sportswear), Kilby Court and Kilby Block Party (merch designed by local artists, 25,000 to 30,000 a day in 2026), the Utah Mammoth identity by Doubleday & Cartwright, and the Utah Jazz 2022 rebrand by Mouthwash. Nationally the reference set is Madhappy's Local Optimist world-building, Kids of Immigrants' community-first flagship, Corteiz's rule-breaking drop theater, Denim Tears and Stüssy's narrative capsules, and the studio tier (Porto Rocha, Bureau Borsche, Mouthwash, DIA, Mother Design) that gives sports teams and institutions a fashion-label finish. What the scene is picking up on right now: vernacular lettering over generic vintage, premium trucker caps as the hero accessory, pop-ups and third-space retail as the channel, six-to-eight-week capsule cadence instead of weekly drops, run clubs and workshops as community engines, and a Western revival that Utah can own more credibly than anyone.

## 2. Mello

### What the brief asked for
A Utah or Mountain West design or creative agency, or streetwear label, spelled M-E-L-L-O, known for merch and brand drops.

### What the search actually found
Eleven separate searches with Utah, Provo, Salt Lake, Ogden, St. George, Denver, Boise, Phoenix, Las Vegas and Idaho qualifiers, plus searches pairing "Mello" with Lonely Ghost, REDWEST, Gigi Pip and Swig, did not surface a Utah or Mountain West creative studio or streetwear label named Mello. The entities that do exist under that name:

- **Mello MFG (mellomfg.com), Austin, Texas.** The closest match to the brief's description. A "premium custom merchandise company helping brands design, source, and produce high-quality branded products and apparel." Production HQ at 801 Barton Springs Rd, Austin. Founder is Zach Mainello (Behance lists him as founder; his background is design for CPG clients). Two tracks: Bespoke programs with 100+ minimums, and "Quick Custom" with 50+ minimums for "launches, events, drops, or last-minute needs." Design projects start at $3,795. Client focus is food, beverage, hospitality and lifestyle: Roots Natural Kitchen (ongoing end-to-end merchandising), Lake Roots (merch that turned into a full rebrand), Ruby Hotel (retail merch described on their site as "fashion drops"), Gardencup, Ode to Farm, Oslo Coffee Co., Smash Tennis. Reviews are mixed: 4.5 stars from 53 on Birdeye, but a third-party listing notes complaints about delays and refunds. Social presence found: Facebook (mellomfg) and Pinterest (mellomfg). No Instagram handle confirmed. No press coverage found.
- **Mello Studio (mello.studio), Austin.** Strategy, branding, product and web design. Not a merch studio.
- **Mello Creative (mellocreative.com).** Illustration, animation, creative direction, "merchandising and grassroots street campaigns." Location not stated.
- **Mellō (Facebook: MelloAgency).** Digital product and brand agency, consumer products and fintech. Location not stated.
- **Mello Lifestyle (Amesbury, MA), ClubMello (DeSoto, TX), Mello Clothing (LA), Mello Design (Stow, OH), Mello (Wollongong, Australia motion studio).** None relevant.

### How to use this in the deck
Treat Mello MFG as a comp for the "merch agency as design partner" model rather than as a Utah peer: a studio that sells hospitality brands a drop-style merch program with design fees up front and fulfillment on the back end. If the deck author has a specific Utah "Mello" in mind (an Instagram-only studio, a person's nickname, or a brand that launched in the last few months), it is not indexed and should be sourced directly from Instagram or from the person who mentioned it. Do not put "Mello, Utah agency" on a slide without that confirmation.

## 3. Lonely Ghost

### Origin
- Founded early 2019 in Provo, Utah by influencer Indy Blue Severe (India Blue Severe, known as Indy Blue) and Bronson Christensen. It began as "Indy the Label" and was renamed Lonely Ghost about a year in; Indy wanted a brand not tied to her personal name. Lonely Ghost's own blog still has a post titled "Remember Indy the Label?"
- Mission language from the brand: "created to infuse inclusivity and positivity into the streetwear world" and "to help people feel more connected in a world that often feels disconnected."

### The "text me when you get home" story
- Indy Blue's own essay "my favorite ghost story" (whoisindyblue.com) tells it: a phone call ended abruptly, a follow-up text asked her to "say it back," and that exchange became the slogan for the clothing brand she was starting. "I love you, say it back" is the signature line; "text me when you get home" and "text me when you land" (used on the Béis luggage collab) are the second-tier phrases. The design language is text-first: heavyweight hoodies, tees, sweatpants and beanies with the phrase as the graphic, a ghost mascot, and candy and primary color palettes.
- Early fans named in coverage: Addison Rae, Hannah Meloche, Pyper America, Summer Mckeen. Collections sold out "in minutes" per early press. The community is referred to as the "Ghost Gang."

### Retail
- **Ghost Grocery, Provo.** 4801 N University Ave Suite 650, The Shops at Riverwoods. Started as a grocery-themed pop-up in December 2020 (Forbes called it "Instagrammable retail during a pandemic"), then moved to a larger 2,700 square foot store a few doors down in 2022 with a bakery section, balloon section and grocery carts. Closed Sundays. Ben Biondo, now listed as Creative Director at Lonely Ghost on LinkedIn, says on his portfolio he branded the store, designed all signage, and designed apparel and packaging for more than 10 grocery-style products that fill the shelves.
- **Scottsdale, Arizona.** Lonely Ghost's stores page lists a second shop at Scottsdale Fashion Square, Suite 2125.
- No Salt Lake City or Gateway store found. LinkedIn job posts for an Apparel Production Manager and a Marketing Manager are based in Salt Lake City, so there is an SLC operations presence.

### Scale and structure
- Forbes (Feb 2022) called it a "multi-million dollar brand." The brand has claimed 400 percent growth since year one. No audited revenue figure exists in public sources; Crunchbase shows a $59.3K figure that is not labeled and should be ignored.
- Headcount conflicts: LinkedIn says 2 to 10 (but shows 14 profiles), PitchBook says 12, Crunchbase says 1 to 10. HQ conflicts too: LinkedIn says Los Angeles, PitchBook and Crunchbase say Provo.
- Manufacturing: Forbes 2022 described the brand hand-picking blank pieces "to use as a canvas for creative designs while repurposing for the good of the environment," which points to a decorated-blanks model rather than cut-and-sew. No factory or country of origin is public.
- Ownership: TikTok discourse says Bronson bought Indy out and that Indy "is not a part of Lonely Ghost" anymore; Distractify reports she is no longer affiliated and that 2024 "broke" her, while noting nothing has been officially confirmed. Bronson Christensen is the public face in every 2025 and 2026 press release, quoted as co-founder and creative director. For the deck: refer to Bronson as the operator, Indy as the founder of record.

### Price points
- Hoodies and tees on lonelyghost.co; Halloween drops have run $30 to $90 (Flaunt). HI-CHEW capsule: sweatshirt $75, sweatpants $70, snapback $30. Pacsun "Beautiful Things" women's capsule: 5 pieces, $30 to $65. A trucker hat sits in the current lineup.

### Drop and collab model, 2025 to 2026
- **Pacsun exclusive retail partnership (announced August 21, 2025).** Pacsun became "the first and only fashion retail partner" for the DTC brand, with capsule product in "select Pacsun retail locations nationwide" and online. Launch capsule leaned on the fan-favorite sayings plus hats and totes. Christensen: it lets the brand "meet young people where they are, both online and in real life." Pacsun CMO Richard Cox: Lonely Ghost "has tapped into something real with their audience."
- **RISE Festival 10th anniversary capsule (August 2025).** Tee, tank, tumbler, tote around the phrase "I Told The Sky About You," released through exclusive gifting.
- **Merrell x Lonely Ghost (September 27, 2025).** First-ever footwear partnership, a reworked Moab Speed 2 Vent 2K in two colorways with four interchangeable laces and a charm kit, plus three apparel looks and a trucker hat. Launched with a lace-and-charm bar at Pacsun SoHo (503 Broadway) and two NYC community events. Sold on lonelyghost.co and select Pacsun stores.
- **Dutch Bros x Lonely Ghost (October 1 to 9, 2025).** Free sticker with any drink on October 1, apparel drop on lonelyghost.co on October 6, a custom chai and collectible on October 8. Christensen framed it around "bringing people together."
- **REDWEST 2025 (October 10 to 12, 2025).** Presenting sponsor with a "Lonely Ghost VIP+ Lounge" that hosted its own set list (Marfa, Elizabeth Nichols, Midland, Ian Munsick among the names listed). See section 4.
- **HI-CHEW x Lonely Ghost (November 14, 2025).** Strawberry-flavored holiday capsule, HI-CHEW Strawberry Sticks as gift with purchase. Sold online and in two retail locations.
- **Béis x Lonely Ghost (earlier, roughly spring 2024).** Glossy red luggage with a "text me when you land" print.
- **Pacsun "Beautiful Things" (April 7, 2026).** Women's capsule, 5 pieces, $30 to $65, framed around "slowing down and finding meaning in the everyday."
- Pattern: a drop every four to eight weeks in the back half of 2025, each one tied to a partner with its own audience (coffee, candy, festival, luggage, footwear, mall retail), each one built around a phrase rather than a logo, and each one with an IRL component (lace bar, lounge, sticker day).

### Why Lonely Ghost matters for Visit Salt Lake
- It proves a Utah brand can sell emotion-as-graphic at national scale without a Los Angeles origin story.
- It proves Utah retail can be a destination (people drive to Riverwoods to see the grocery store).
- Its collab list shows which national partners are comfortable with a Utah brand: Pacsun, Merrell, Dutch Bros, HI-CHEW, Béis. Those are plausible partners for a Salt Lake capsule too.

## 4. The Red West drop

### What Red West is
"Red West" is REDWEST SLC (stylized REDWEST, also written Redwest and RedWest), a country music festival at the Utah State Fairpark in Salt Lake City. Facts:
- First edition October 4 to 6, 2024, launched "with seasoned event producer AEG (Stagecoach, Coachella)." Deseret News previewed it in March 2024 with Oliver Anthony as a headliner.
- Owner entity is RedWest LLC (per the site's terms). St. George News credits Kevin Colopy, founder of Utah's Hive Festival, as creator; ABC4 names Tanner Olson as "partner and owner." The Salt Lake Tribune (October 10, 2025) describes it as a festival built by "a group of a dozen friends" from Utah who booked Post Malone, Noah Kahan and Kacey Musgraves.
- 2025 edition ran October 10 to 12 with Post Malone, Noah Kahan, Kacey Musgraves, Koe Wetzel, Treaty Oak Revival, Ole 60 and Cameron Whitcomb. Saturday was evacuated for weather, Post Malone's set was cancelled, one attendee died, and a class action was organized. Noah Kahan closed Sunday. Forbes ran a piece on October 28, 2025 contrasting it with RISE Fest.
- 2026 edition is October 16 to 17 (pre-party October 15), presented by KeyBank, ticketing through Leap, headliners Ella Langley, Shaboozey, Koe Wetzel, Sam Barber, Tucker Wetmore, Wyatt Flores, Ashley Cooke, Cameron Whitcomb. Visit Salt Lake lists it on its own events calendar. The festival is partnering with ALL IN for an accessibility program and has an ambassador program that pays out in tickets and merch.
- On-site merch: the festival info page describes a "REDWEST Merch World" with a general store inside it.

### What the Lonely Ghost relationship actually is
- Lonely Ghost is listed on the festival's sponsor page as the presenting sponsor for 2025, alongside Gigi Pip and Kona Big Aloha.
- The 2025 schedule lists a "Lonely Ghost VIP+ Lounge" with its own daily performances. Forbes describes VIP+ as the top tier with premium food and beverage in a dedicated building. So the activation was a branded hospitality space, the kind of thing Madhappy or Kith would do at a festival.
- No indexed source (PR Newswire, Hypebeast, local press, resale listings, the festival site, the Lonely Ghost site's indexed pages) shows a Lonely Ghost x REDWEST apparel capsule with named pieces, prices or a designer credit. If one exists it is likely Instagram or on-site only. Flag this for the deck as "sponsorship and lounge activation, 2025; merch drop unconfirmed."
- Neither the 2026 sponsor list nor any 2026 announcement names Lonely Ghost. Searches on "REDWEST x" apparel partners for October 2026 returned only unofficial lineup tees on eBay and bootleg sites.

### The verifiable REDWEST merch drop: Gigi Pip
- Gigi Pip, the Salt Lake City hat brand founded in 2015 by Jon and Ginger Parrish, is "the official hat sponsor of the Redwest Music Festival." It sells a "REDWEST x Gigi Pip" collection including a limited-edition "Redwest Mountain Vintage Trucker" and several other Redwest-branded trucker styles. An October 8, 2025 email promoted "NEW RedWest Trucker Hats" alongside a $50-off custom hat offer. Gigi Pip also ran a hat bar activation on site ("Cowboying Up at the Redwest Music Fest" on their events blog) and the "Gigi Pip Redsouth Stage" carried its name.
- This is the closest thing to a designed destination-festival merch drop in Salt Lake right now: a local brand, a vernacular Western object (the trucker), a limited run, a custom-and-personalize experience on site, and a permanent product line afterward.

### How to use this in the deck
REDWEST is a Visit Salt Lake-listed event with a Utah ownership group, AEG production, and two Utah brands already treating it as a merch and hospitality canvas. It is a ready-made partner for a Salt Lake capsule. The honest framing is "Lonely Ghost sponsored and ran the VIP lounge; Gigi Pip made the official hats." If a Lonely Ghost x REDWEST garment drop is confirmed from Instagram, add it, but do not invent pieces or prices.

## 5. The Utah creative and streetwear scene map

### Streetwear and DTC labels born in Utah
- **Lonely Ghost (Provo, 2019).** Covered above. The anchor.
- **Gigi Pip (Salt Lake City, 2015).** Women's hats (Panama straw, Western, felt wide-brim, truckers). Design in SLC, production abroad. Claims 1 million hats sold. Revenue estimates $7.8M (Growjo) to $16.3M (RocketReach, ZoomInfo). Downtown flagship plus the "SLC Speakeasy," a hidden hat bar and private event space opened October 2025. Franchising its "Hat Bar" experience nationally. Collabs: Revolve, REDWEST. Relevant because hats are the single most giftable, most wearable destination object, and a Salt Lake brand already owns the premium trucker locally.
- **Boys Lie.** Not Utah-connected. Founded 2018 (pivot to apparel 2019) by Tori Robinson and Leah O'Malley, Philadelphia-suburb friends, LA-based; $10M-plus annual revenue without paid ads, Urban Outfitters and Nordstrom wholesale from 2024. Useful as a comp for phrase-led emotional streetwear, not as a Utah peer.
- **Cotopaxi (Salt Lake City, 2014).** B Corp, 200 to 1000 employees, 1 percent of revenue to nonprofits. The most fashion-credible Utah outdoor brand because of color blocking and the "Del Día" one-of-one scrap bags. Hint Creative did the "Gear for Good" campaign and Questival branding.
- **Kühl (Salt Lake City, began as Alfwear).** Product-first, branding-light. A cautionary comp: strong product, weak graphic identity.
- **Black Diamond (Salt Lake City).** Hardgoods and apparel, 200 to 1000 employees; logo tees are a de facto SLC uniform.
- **Saga Outerwear (Holladay / Salt Lake).** Started at Windells Camp in 2005, moved to Salt Lake. DTC ski and snowboard outerwear, sponsors the Summer of Death skate event with Milosport and Salty Peaks.
- **Niche Snowboards (Murray, UT).** Eco-forward snowboards.
- **Lamar.** Legacy snowboard brand sold through Salty Peaks; not an active design voice.
- **Jiberish (Denver, 2005).** The Mountain West streetwear reference: ski-culture roots, now everyday apparel plus snow outerwear, runs a ski team, collabs with New Era.
- **Coalatree and Wild Tribute (Salt Lake City).** Mountain-to-city apparel and national-parks-inspired tees respectively; Wild Tribute is a plausible "parks and public lands" graphic partner.
- **Nebu Clothing.** Featured as a homegrown Utah outdoor brand at Outdoor Retailer 2025 in SLC.
- **Albion Fit and Lime Ricki (Salt Lake area).** Swim and fitness. Fashion-credible within the Utah modest-swim niche, not streetwear.
- **Taft (Provo), Nena & Co. (American Fork).** Footwear and artisan bags; part of the Utah County DTC cluster.
- **Swig (dirty soda).** Official merch (tumblers, hoodies, totes, color-changing collectible cups) sold through swig.com; mostly promotional-grade. The viral Utah soda merch moment of 2025 was actually the Salt Lake Bees' "Utah Dirty Sodas" alternate identity (hats, jerseys, a plush) sold through the Bees store, which is a direct precedent for a civic object turned into a drop. Sodalicious has no documented merch program.
- **Kickee Pants, Chums, Hurley.** Not Utah-based; drop from the Utah list.

### Retail and community spaces
- **Fice Gallery / Fice Boutique (downtown SLC, since 2008).** "Utah's only Tier One Nike account," carries Carhartt WIP, Dr. Martens, ASICS, Vans, sells exclusive Utah Jazz merch at the Delta Center, and collections designed by local creatives. Per Salt Lake Sideways, co-owner "Grace" says they are "redirecting our focus to community events and art shows." This is the Salt Lake clubhouse candidate.
- **Thrifthood (Provo, now downtown SLC).** Owner Alan Bostrom. Started in a basement, grew to a Provo storefront, opened a second location in downtown SLC in 2025. Hand-picked '90s and 2000s vintage sportswear. SLUG called it "a third space for excellence in vintage sportswear."
- **Doused (Murray).** Sneaker and resale, stocks Supreme, Bape, ASSC, Gallery Dept, Essentials, Palace, Market.
- **iconoCLAD (SLC).** Vintage, alternative and rave; Best of Utah 2025.
- **Milosport (Orem) and Salty Peaks (SLC).** The two skate and snow shops that anchor the action-sports side; both sponsor Summer of Death with Saga.
- **Kilby Court (SLC, 25 years, longest-running all-ages venue).** Merch designed by local artists: logo tee and hoodie by Chloe Monson, sketch tee and tote by Tom Petersen, a 25th anniversary tee and a dad hat ($30 to $35 tees). Print-on-demand.
- **Kilby Block Party (S&S Presents, Utah State Fairpark, May 15 to 17, 2026; Lorde, The xx, Turnstile).** 25,000 to 30,000 a day. Designer Bailey Rogers' portfolio lists the 2026 project as marketing materials, merch, credentials and signage for S&S Presents. This is the local proof that a festival can treat merch as part of a coherent identity system.
- **Gigi Pip SLC Speakeasy (October 2025).** Hat bar as event space.
- Yelp's 2026 SLC streetwear list adds Curriculum, Icy Society, Top Shelf SLC, Eastside Hype, Modern Monark, Premier Status.

### Design studios and agencies with real merch or identity credentials
- **Actual Source (Provo, 2014 or 2015).** JP Haynie (University of Utah) and Davis Ngarupe (Otis). Started as "Number 04," renamed after their 2015 Utah Museum of Contemporary Art exhibition. Publisher of Shoplifters (Issue 10: 156 typefaces by 114 designers), a type foundry, a bookstore turned studio in 2017, and an apparel line. Work: identity and the Thermal typeface for Virgil Abloh's NikeLab Re-Creation Center Chicago (2019), Hotel Drugs identity and apparel, A24 Books publishing imprint identity (completed 2026), the Past Lives screenplay book (2025), a spring apparel collection for Fresh Service Japan, an IBM Research photo book with Cait Oppermann, and a 2018 apparel-themed solo show at Washer / Dryer Projects in SLC. Spoke at the Walker Art Center's 2025 Insights Lecture Series. This is the Utah studio with the fashion-label-grade typographic voice the deck is asking for.
- **Struck (SLC, 1993).** Utah Office of Tourism agency of record since 2005: The Mighty 5 (2013), Road to Mighty, Find Your Greatest, More Mountain Time, two Mercury Awards. Clients also include Uinta, Probar, McDonald's, University of Utah. Campaign-grade, not merch-grade; the relevant lesson is that Struck gave Utah's parks a brand that still sells tees a decade later.
- **Hint Creative (SLC, 2006).** Branding for CPG, startups and wellness; Cotopaxi "Gear for Good" and Questival, Skullcandy, Nike, Fender, Backcountry listed.
- **modern8 (SLC).** Identity and packaging (Ritual Chocolate, Grow Your Own Way). Top of Clutch's October 2026 Utah branding rankings with Hint.
- **Super Top Secret (SLC, 254 E 100 S).** Digital, brand identity, photography and video; 11 to 50 staff.
- **Fuel Marketing (SLC, 2003).** Advertising, PR and media.
- **Rumor Advertising (North Salt Lake).** Packsize refresh, University of Utah, RootsTech, Venafi.
- **Big Monocle (SLC and San Francisco).** Brand strategy, art direction, production.
- **7D8 (SLC).** Listed with Skullcandy as a recurring client.
- **Think Tank, Zealous, Kaiju, Noble Studios (Reno).** No Utah merch or identity work surfaced under those names; leave off the slide unless sourced.
- **Doubleday & Cartwright (NYC).** Not Utah, but did the Utah Mammoth permanent identity with Jazz senior brand director Ben Barnes after four rounds of fan surveys. That is the current ceiling for Salt Lake sports identity work.
- **Mouthwash (LA / NYC / Barcelona).** Did the Utah Jazz 2022 rebrand motion system, kinetic type, court and jersey colorways, with Sturdy on court renders and Ali Raybould on 3D jersey motion. Also runs its own podcast, journal, magazine and a 13-piece apparel and objects line. Clients include Comme des Garçons, Nike, Thom Browne, Apple, Rimowa.

### Freelance designers and photographers on the Utah scene
- Ben Biondo (Lonely Ghost creative director; Ghost Grocery branding, signage, packaging).
- Bailey Rogers (Kilby Block Party 2026 branding and merch for S&S Presents).
- Chloe Monson and Tom Petersen (Kilby Court merch artists).
- Tim Calkins Creative (Cotopaxi work listed).
- Dribbble lists about a dozen brand designers for hire in Provo as of March 2026.
- Campaign photographers for Lonely Ghost are credited on TikTok but not by name in indexed sources; pull from Instagram tags.

## 6. The national stratosphere and their signature moves

- **Madhappy (LA, 2017).** Signature move: a brand built as a mental-health media company. Local Optimist is a magazine (Issue 04, March 6, 2025), a blog, a podcast and a pop-up tour (LA, NYC, Miami, Aspen or Tokyo depending on source) with panels and workshops. LVMH-backed ($1.8M, 2019), $100M-plus by some estimates. Permanent stores in Meatpacking, Aspen (517 E Hopkins), Miami, Melrose Place, Abbott Kinney, East Hampton seasonal, NoLIta, with Tokyo and Malibu slated for spring 2026. Fall 2026 Classics shot by Roe Ethridge, a second FREECITY collab June 2026. No Park City store or pop-up found; Aspen is the mountain-town template. Peiman Raf on pop-ups: "it's not really about shopping."
- **Corteiz (London, Clint419).** Signature move: drop theater. Pop-up locations announced hours before, the "Bolo exchange," a 2026 lottery whose winner got Clint's 2016 VW Polo. Denim Tears "CORTEARS" capsule December 25, 2025 (varsity, down jackets, denim, sweats), New Era knitwear and velour capsule February 27, 2026, Nike Shox "R419" named after his handle for Holiday 2026 ($180 to $190, three colorways). US tour across LA, DC, NYC, ATL in 2025.
- **Sp5der (Young Thug).** Signature move: puff-print rhinestone maximalism scaled through adidas: Superstar Supermodified with apparel (March 3, 2026), F50 Formotion silver pair worn by Lamine Yamal (June or July 2026, $190), red and green F50 pack August 31, 2026.
- **Denim Tears (Tremaine Emory).** Signature move: a single symbol (the cotton wreath) carried through narrative capsules. FW26 is a tribute to André Leon Talley released in five waves from August 14, 2026, with Stüssy ("Stüssy Tears," orange outerwear, October 16, 2026), Timberland and Billionaire Boys Club.
- **Kids of Immigrants (LA, Daniel Buezo and Weleh Dennis).** Signature move: community as product. First flagship at 4873 Melrose Ave opened July 2026 for the 10th anniversary, designed as a community space and carrying an official Nike selection, which is rare. Nike T90 Mule May 28, 2026 ($120), Nike Tiempo Maestro "Sundial" referencing Honduras and Liberia, revealed October 21, 2026 after Matheus Cunha wore it in training, due November 4 at $260 to $275.
- **Museum of Peace & Quiet.** Signature move: quiet minimalism and mindfulness; collabs with Crocs, Vans Vault, Lusso Cloud. No 2025 or 2026 news surfaced; treat as a tone reference.
- **Stüssy.** Signature move: never chasing Supreme, per Scott Watts' Highsnobiety interview; an ongoing Nike partnership, Martine Rose, Our Legacy hand-reworked capsules across LA and Stockholm, and Denim Tears FW26.
- **Online Ceramics (LA).** Signature move: psychedelic Deadhead illustration applied to institutions. Chapter 7 filing in early 2025, co-founder Alix Ross departed, yet still shipping: Wikipedia 25th anniversary capsule (tees from $72, caps, sweatpants) September 30, 2026 with a Gizmodo story questioning the deal. A warning about running a studio on collabs alone.
- **Brain Dead (LA).** Signature move: a brand with a cinema. Brain Dead Studios, 611 N Fairfax, programs film and events (Fantasy Fest: Cybercon November 10 to 15, 2026); Harajuku store; collabs with Dr Martens, Sound & Fury, Magic: The Gathering.
- **Carrots (Anwar Carrots).** Signature move: taking streetwear to mass. Walmart "Homegrown" in 1,000 stores (January 16, 2025, hoodies, joggers, tees), and a 17-piece "Carrots OG" capsule with Timeless (November 2025).
- **Drew House / Skylrk.** Justin Bieber exited Drew House April 2025 ("don't waste your money"), teased Skylrk; by June 2025 the Skylrk site was password-locked. Lesson: a celebrity brand without the celebrity is just inventory.
- **Golf Wang (Tyler, the Creator, 2011).** Signature move: brand-as-world. GWFC soccer capsule tied to the 2026 World Cup, AriZona Iced Tea green tea collab (2025), FW26 campaign starring Tyler, NYC store since 2022.
- **Fear of God Essentials.** Signature move: licensing a neutral palette to every league. Fall 2026 expands NFL Essentials to all franchises; MLB Essentials Summer 2026 with Francisco Lindor; NBA Essentials. Telfar's "Fear of Job" parody (March 2026) shows how recognizable the system is.
- **Aimé Leon Dore.** Signature move: a New York lifestyle with coffee, a café and a world of collaborators. SS26 New Balance activewear, Technics turntable ($2,100), FW26 with GORE-TEX, The North Face, Last Resort AB, an Olympiacos F.C. capsule and a Knicks cap teasing a first official tie-up. Flagships NYC, LA, London.

## 8. What the scene is picking up on right now (late 2025 to 2026)

1. **Vernacular lettering over generic vintage.** PRINT's 2026 Type Report says designers are leaving "vintage" fonts for real signage, packaging and regional lettering; Rapha's 2026 identity built typefaces from its own race jerseys. For Salt Lake that means motel signs on State Street, ski-area trail markers, Fairpark signage and Deseret-era type, not a distressed "est. 1847" badge.
2. **Bootleg structure, not bootleg property.** Print-on-demand guides warn against copying a team or soda logo closely enough to confuse; the move is borrowing the format (the parking-lot bootleg tee, the souvenir shop layout) with original marks.
3. **Premium truckers as the hero.** Foam fronts are out; cotton twill, corduroy and suede fronts are in. Gigi Pip's REDWEST trucker and Lonely Ghost's Merrell trucker are both local proof. "Viral statement" headwear with slogans is a named 2026 segment.
4. **Capsule cadence of six to eight weeks, three to five pieces.** The 2026 small-label guidance is that a drop every two weeks "turns scarcity into noise," and that fake scarcity with weekly restocks erodes trust. Lonely Ghost's fall 2025 calendar (RISE, Merrell, Dutch Bros, REDWEST, HI-CHEW) is roughly that cadence.
5. **Pop-ups as core channel.** Retail trend reports call pop-ups "a core part of omnichannel strategies," and microbrands use them as warehouse, showroom and acquisition engine. Corteiz announces locations hours ahead; Madhappy runs seasonal outposts; Vuori and Wunderkind both used Park City pop-ups on Main Street before going permanent.
6. **Retail as clubhouse / third space.** Fast Company's "Welcome to the clubhouse" frames the shift "from CRM to community"; Rapha clubhouses, ALD, Kith, Lululemon run clubs, Foot Locker and Nike's Crenshaw Rec, ASICS' ping pong clubhouse. Six in ten Millennials want retail that blends community and shopping (Retail Technology Show 2026). In Salt Lake: Fice pivoting to events and art shows, Gigi Pip's Speakeasy, Thrifthood described as a "third space."
7. **Run clubs and workshops as merch engines.** Another Cotton Lab's Sunday Running Club went from a storefront in Düsseldorf to 2,000 runners at a night run; Bandit was born from NYC run clubs; PYNRS ties merch to Harlem's 1936 Pioneer Club. A Salt Lake run club merch line is low-cost and on-trend.
8. **"Friends and family" and localism.** Not surfaced as a named trend in indexed sources, but visible in the behavior: Kilby Court credits local artists on each tee, Kids of Immigrants builds a flagship as a community room, Lonely Ghost gifts rather than sells its RISE capsule. Treat as a tactic, not a buzzword.
9. **Catalog and film-look photography.** Studio catalog shots on neutral backgrounds are the baseline; film grain and retro grades are the differentiator; heavy retouching now reads as misleading to younger buyers. Madhappy hired Roe Ethridge for Fall 2026, which is the catalog-as-art version.
10. **Western revival, done quiet.** 2026 coverage splits between "Western core is back" (cowboy hats with blazers, square-toe boots, pearl snaps) and "fringe and buckles are out." The winning version is restrained Western detail. Utah has the real thing: REDWEST, Post Malone living in Utah, the Fairpark, the State Fair rodeo. Gigi Pip already sells it.
11. **The Mountain West aesthetic.** Jiberish in Denver, Madhappy in Aspen, Vuori and Wunderkind in Park City, Cotopaxi and Black Diamond in SLC. The gap is a city brand that sits between gorpcore and streetwear; nobody owns "Salt Lake" the way Madhappy owns Aspen.
12. **"Utah is cool" and the post-Mormon moment.** Not covered by fashion press in indexed sources, but the culture is: Secret Lives of Mormon Wives (four of nine cast ex-Mormon), LDS women influencers driving "a new Mormon moment" (Baylor Religion Watch, 2026), Post Malone and Benson Boone as Utah-based stars, Provo's Velour as an alcohol-free all-ages launchpad, Dry Bar Comedy born in Provo. Lonely Ghost and Thrifthood are both Provo-born. The deck can say "Provo is a creative incubator" with evidence; it should not say "post-Mormon aesthetic" as if the press has named it.
13. **TikTok-native brands and the founder exit problem.** Lonely Ghost (Indy Blue), Drew House (Bieber), Sporty & Rich (CEO exit), Online Ceramics (co-founder exit) all show the brand surviving or not depending on whether the system outlived the face. A destination brand has no founder to lose, which is an advantage if the system is strong.
14. **Collab fatigue and institutional merch.** The Wikipedia x Online Ceramics discourse (fans worried Wikipedia got a bad deal) and Rahim's "good on T-shirts for a while" show that institutions doing merch get scrutinized on terms and taste. A DMO drop needs transparent partner terms and a real designer credit.

## 9. How Visit Salt Lake prioritizes this style

1. **Lead with a phrase, not a logo.** Lonely Ghost, Boys Lie and Madhappy all sell a sentence. Salt Lake's sentence should be something residents already say (a weather joke, a canyon direction, a "text me when you're off the pass") rather than a tourism tagline.
2. **Hire one Utah studio with a fashion-label voice as design lead.** Actual Source is the obvious choice: Provo-based, NikeLab and A24 credentials, its own typefaces and apparel. Pair them with a sports or motion specialist (Mouthwash or Doubleday & Cartwright both already know the Utah market) if the program needs a system beyond garments.
3. **Make the trucker the hero object and make it with Gigi Pip.** Local brand, premium blanks, proven REDWEST collab, a hat bar for on-site personalization, and a franchise footprint that can carry a Salt Lake hat out of state.
4. **Anchor the first drop to an event Visit Salt Lake already lists.** REDWEST (October) and Kilby Block Party (May) both have ownership groups, merch infrastructure and audiences of 25,000-plus a day. Sponsor a lounge or a stage like Lonely Ghost did and release the capsule there first.
5. **Run a six-to-eight-week cadence with three to five pieces per capsule.** Four to six capsules a year tied to seasons the city actually has (powder, Sundance, spring runoff, Fairpark fall), not weekly SKUs.
6. **Treat a Salt Lake storefront as a clubhouse, not a gift shop.** Fice is already pivoting to events and art shows and holds the only Tier One Nike account in the state; a Visit Salt Lake corner or rotating shelf there is faster and more credible than a new store. Program it: run club, print workshop, lineup announcement parties.
7. **Credit the artist on every piece.** Kilby Court names Chloe Monson and Tom Petersen on the product page. Lonely Ghost's Ben Biondo is nameable. A DMO should be the most generous crediter in the scene because it has no founder ego to protect.
8. **Use decorated premium blanks for the first year.** Lonely Ghost grew on hand-picked blanks; cut-and-sew can wait until a hit graphic is proven.
9. **Borrow audiences through partners that already like Utah brands.** Pacsun, Merrell, Dutch Bros, HI-CHEW and Béis all said yes to a Provo label. Cotopaxi, Black Diamond, Kühl, Saga and Wild Tribute are local partners with their own channels. The Salt Lake Bees' Utah Dirty Sodas shows a civic object can carry a drop.
10. **Own "quiet Western" before someone from Denver does.** Pearl snaps, a felt hat, a square-toe reference and Fairpark signage type, with none of the fringe. REDWEST, Gigi Pip and the State Fair give Salt Lake a claim nobody else in the Mountain West has.
11. **Shoot it like a catalog with a film grade, in real Salt Lake places.** Riverwoods, the Fairpark, a Trax platform, Kilby Court's alley. Keep retouching light; the audience reads it as honesty.
12. **Say Provo out loud.** The two most-watched Utah streetwear businesses (Lonely Ghost, Thrifthood) are Provo-born, and Actual Source is Provo-based. Visit Salt Lake can position the capsule as a Wasatch Front project and pull Utah County's creative class in rather than ignoring it.
13. **Publish partner terms and give institutions a real cut.** The Wikipedia x Online Ceramics scrutiny is the cautionary tale; a public DMO will be read even harder.
14. **Keep "Mello" and any "Lonely Ghost x REDWEST drop" off the deck until confirmed from primary sources.** The verifiable facts are Lonely Ghost as REDWEST 2025 presenting sponsor with a VIP+ Lounge, and Gigi Pip as official hat sponsor with a named product line.
