# How to Rebrand Yourself in 2025 (and why you should)

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Z3KsU8o2ZM

Length: about 29 min

## The main idea

A yearly personal rebrand where you run your own life like a business. First reset your inputs (workspace, style, brand kit, muses), then write down how you’re seen now and how you want to be seen, then build a marketing plan, an operations plan and a calendar of recurring reviews. He walks through it with a free Notion template and uses his own year as the example.

## Frameworks and steps

- Three levels of self (1:27). Level 1: just you and your job. Level 2: you plus freelance or content. Level 3: you as a business, where marketing, operations and spending are investments in your own potential.
- The three-state lens used in every section (2:58): where I am now, the dream state, the very next step.
- Spaces (2:29): mood board an ideal space for two goals. Inspiration maxing: does the room spark work? Content maxing: does it look good on camera?
- Style and personal aesthetic (4:36), on two levels. What you wear: set a budget, then choose between many cheap looks or fewer lasting investment pieces, not both. Catalog your wardrobe and photograph yourself daily in the same spot. Brand kit: one or two signature colors and fonts, loaded into every app you use (CapCut, Adobe Express) and kept in a note with the color codes.
- Muses (9:47): pick one person per platform you’re active on, list their content pillars, and write down what you’ll take from each.
- Projection (13:04): write how people see you now, then a statement in the form “I will project X.”
- Dream life database (14:05): each item gets cost, benefit type, timeline and next action. Benefit types: health, happiness, wealth, inspiration, signaler, fulfillment. Items with no real benefit get cut.
- Sacrifices and stepping stones (16:32): same table, listing what you’ll give up or work toward to reach the dream items.
- Old me vs new me (18:16): a two-column exercise feeding a don’t-do list and a new-habits list.
- Marketing plan (19:41): budget a percentage of income, as any company would. Have at least one program in each stage: awareness, nurture, convert. Track program, stage, budget, needs and notes.
- Operations plan (22:01): list every recurring activity and tag it by program (day job, side business, content). Rate each one: impossible to outsource, need a provider, source found for later, or already outsourced. Add the cost, then decide whether it’s worth paying for now given your time.
- Action list (25:44): put marketing, ops and personal to-dos into buckets. Break anything big into small first steps.
- Calendar cadence (26:28). Monthly: finance, analytics and marketing reviews at a fixed time. Weekly: a kickoff with each team member early in the week and a work review later. Yearly: major personal and cultural moments (Valentine’s Day, back to school, Black Friday).

## Examples and case studies he names

- His own planning retreat in the Canary Islands: the scale of commitment he gives the exercise.
- Ten 4x8 ft whiteboards from Uline with prints from CVS or Walmart: his best space investment for inspiration.
- Estate sale shelving in a garage-door studio: one secondhand buy that fixed his on-camera backdrop.
- A no-new-purchases year funded only by resale on Depop and Grailed: forces intention when money is tight.
- Bayma (friend): one signature purple on a hat, in story backgrounds and graphics, even a studio painted that Pantone. A color that becomes the person.
- Designed by Brady and Edgy Katrina: places to find font inspiration.
- IM8 (sponsor): packaging that breaks from wellness-category green.
- Muses: Grace Beverley on Instagram (designed journey carousels, her podcast woven in without hard branding), Girlboss Town on TikTok (an annual “31 days of predictions,” ideas over information), Greg Isenberg on X (pure-value text posts).
- His view that ideas now beat information and tactics, since plain news is commoditized.
- Driving Hondas and Toyotas until he could afford the G-Wagon: “be thrift or designer, the middle doesn’t pay.”
- His own ops stack: YouTube and short-form editors, strategy support, an assistant, outsourced accounting and legal. Next up: clipping, DM handling, a design process.
- Cut30 testing newsletter sponsorships as a conversion program.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Cover a wall in large pin-up boards and refresh the prints for each project.
- Get one signature color and use it everywhere: wardrobe, set paint, graphics.
- Keep a phone note with cutout photos of every wearable piece you own.
- Tag every lifestyle want with its real benefit. Honest signalers are allowed.
- Use one muse per platform, and write their pillars down instead of just following them.
- Write a dated “I will project X” line and check your content against it.
- Use screen-time blockers (one sec, Brick) so scrolling only happens at scheduled times.
- Put a dollar figure on each outsourceable task (for example $75 a proposal) and decide based on your current bandwidth.
- Hold finance, analytics and marketing reviews once a month on a fixed date, not daily.
- Map cultural moments in January so drops and campaigns don’t sneak up on you.
