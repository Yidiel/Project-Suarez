# A Creative’s Guide to Great Art Direction

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CNYN0wFkzMo

Length: about 14.5 min

## The main idea

A second-level art direction video built around reference brands. It covers three things: making plain white-cyclorama shoots stop the scroll with action, props and compositing; matching how novel your ideas are to where your customer sits on the adoption curve; and breaking a video vignette into a repeatable formula. Throughout, he works from an ad and organic swipe file (Foreplay, the sponsor) and reviews it regularly.

Note: brand names come from auto-captions, and a few may be misspelled (flagged below with “likely”).

## Frameworks and steps

- White background playbook (1:07): the white studio is the most common, cheapest set, so the art direction has to supply the tension. Levers: lock the camera and composite several frames into one scene; give the model an activity, not a pose; add one absurd or trend-borrowed prop; play with scale so size is ambiguous; shoot on white and composite in AI or real backgrounds.
- Homework from that section (3:26): what could you bring to your next white shoot? Run the Craigslist exercise, meaning a few hundred dollars and a truck for a prop that makes the image.
- Adoption bell curve (6:00): place your customer on the curve before deciding how original to be. Middle-of-curve audiences are fine with formats the industry thinks are tired. Edge brands have to get to trends and fringe talent first.
- Reference monitoring (9:49): pick muse brands and check their running ads on a schedule, since ad styles go in and out of fashion. Add what you find to a try-on-a-shoot list.
- Brand object (10:12): create a physical object for the collection (a book, for example) and put it through many scenarios as the story or announcement device.
- Cognitive dissonance in video (10:40): a voiceover on one subject over visuals telling a different story keeps people watching.
- Model with a job (11:01): have the model teach (a styling lesson, for example) instead of posing. It works even if they can’t act.
- Vignette formula (12:17), from a postcard-style location video, broken into parts: fonts (title and subtitle), video style (look, consistent framing), lifestyle portrayed, people and how they relate to the brand, product integration, narrative (for example sharing a meal), and resources on hand (location, vehicle, market, food).

## Examples and case studies he names

- “Lunge” (likely a fashion label; name uncertain from captions): an ultra-wide, locked-camera composite on white that builds a whole scene.
- Marni (captioned “Marne”): models in action with wires crossing the frame like a cracked screen, so story and curiosity come from one prop.
- His own Valuables shoot: the model reading Catcher in the Rye, with a Bukowski book in another shoot. A simple activity makes the photo more interesting.
- Futura 2000 x Off-White: white space as a canvas, with perspective shifts merged from one camera position.
- Chantelle Paris (captioned “Shantel”): a model in heels on a treadmill, a social trend pulled into the shoot as a prop.
- Balenciaga ads: falling and spinning composites, deliberately ungroomed photos, alongside plain shots.
- Jacquemus: a bag on a desk with no sense of scale. Straight model shots but tricks on product shots. A collection-named book carried through many scenes while the voiceover teaches how to pronounce the brand name.
- Aritzia: late to the TSA-bin video and the 3D fashion animation trend, and both still worked because its customer sits mid-curve.
- A lingerie and swim brand on the edge (captioned “imgia”; name uncertain): an officecore campaign starring a TikTok couple known mainly to very online people, with 20 to 30 content pieces from one shoot.
- Skims: split-screen ads with the model beside the flat outfit, bold before-and-after, minimal styling on white.
- Bandit Running: white-background shots with AI-composited cities and textures behind.
- A menswear brand (captioned “gun”; name uncertain): a model giving a styling lesson to a mannequin in a classroom, plus a “postcards” series set in nostalgic old Italy around a market trip and a shared meal.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Before any white shoot, list one action, one prop and one compositing idea.
- Plan composites with the editor before the shoot, not after.
- Use ambiguous scale for product shots to stop the scroll cheaply.
- Pull a format that’s trending on social into stills (treadmill, TSA bin).
- Decide the customer’s place on the curve up front. If they’re mid-curve, run the proven format and skip the originality guilt.
- If the brand sits at the edge, cast fringe creators before the rest of the market finds them, and shoot many pieces in one go.
- Keep a living swipe file of competitor and aspirational ads sorted by newest and longest-running. Share boards with clients as links.
- Invent a brand object for each collection or campaign.
- Write a vignette spec sheet (fonts, look, lifestyle, people, product, narrative, resources) before scouting.
