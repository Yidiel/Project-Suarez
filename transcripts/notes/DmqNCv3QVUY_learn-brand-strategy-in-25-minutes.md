# Learn brand strategy in 25 minutes

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmqNCv3QVUY

Length: about 22:10

## The main idea

Strategy is what turns a designer into someone who gets paid more, gets promoted and can consult. Oren’s version is fast and content-driven: get inside the customer’s feed, spot recurring themes, package a theme into a brief with examples and how-to tutorials, then run postmortems until you have a personal strategy toolkit. He frames it as a minimum viable process you can repeat weekly instead of a long checklist.

## Frameworks and steps

- Career arc (1:35): designer taking briefs, then creative director once he added strategy, then corporate marketing and sales roles, then paid consulting. Money followed strategy, not design hours.
- The cardinal rule, perspective (3:05): good marketers can stand in the customer’s shoes and admit when they are not the customer.
- Two customer questions (5:10): who buys now, and who do we want buying. Decide whether you market to the current customer, the target one, or both.
- Content is the medium (5:40): paid ads, organic posts, influencer seeding and ads in other channels all run on content, so strategy work is mostly about content.
- Burner account immersion (6:20): separate Instagram, TikTok and X accounts that only follow and engage with the niche, plus scheduled scrolling to bookmark what works.
- Customer avatar sheet (8:00): a named persona with age, where she shops, sites, social habits, income, priorities, backed by links to real people, including low-view non-influencer posts and reviews.
- Perspective into theme (10:05): when the same idea keeps showing up across formats, name it as a theme. Pitch two or three themes per campaign.
- Where themes apply (11:00): new ads, owned content, influencer and employee content, gifting, email automation, website.
- Theme to execution (12:55): attach the theme to a channel with a rule, e.g. 90 days of influencer sends with a travel through line, or half of new ads on theme.
- The toolkit loop in a brief (15:05): theme, examples of the format performing organically, and a tutorial for making it. That completes the handoff.
- Postmortem columns (17:05): did it fail because of communication or follow-through, or because the idea was wrong. Log both without ego.
- Theme brief format (18:30): a hero image, a linked moodboard, brands doing it well, a muse who embodies it, imagery list and what the angle means.
- Management formula (20:30): getting results through people, strategy and impact, and communicating upward and across. Strategy plus one other qualifies you to manage.

## Examples and case studies he names

- A luxury nightclub early in his career (logos, menus, site, flyers): design-only work that paid little, though the owner became a mentor.
- Outdoors brand selling to wealthy hunters: his first lesson that he was not the customer and had to immerse.
- Victoria Paris: if the target audience follows her, study why she works.
- Rhode: searching the brand on TikTok surfaces creators posting about it, a quick route to the real customer.
- Women’s wear accessories consult: luggage drop videos, a travel carousel and scenic collages added up to a travel theme.
- Equinox: long-running text-led campaigns such as “Commit to Something” carried across many executions.
- A coquette-style theme (bows, ornate pieces done minimally) pitched in Notion.
- POV hooks, including his own Valuable series: one hook line that ties loose imagery together. A girls-trip POV could be the copy line across a campaign.
- Upside-down and drop video formats: trends to bundle into briefs with tutorials.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Open a burner account per audience and only feed it that niche. 15 minutes, three times a week, bookmarking with intent.
- Base personas on real accounts, matching influencers with ordinary customers.
- Drop three relevant links into Slack or email when ideas are discussed. Repeated small inputs build your reputation without self-promotion.
- For a promotion pitch, bring 40 influencer links, categorized UGC and competitor ideas sorted into slots.
- In influencer briefs, give a theme, loose rules (use luggage, show movement between places) and freedom to interpret.
- Push for 50 or more creators on one campaign idea so it starts spreading on its own.
- Always attach a tutorial (CapCut or similar) to a trend reference.
- Keep a postmortem doc with a checkbox for “not my fault, but what can I fix.”
- Publish your theme decks or boards; public process brings inbound clients.
- Favor speed: ten quick theme cycles teach more than one long formal process. He sums it up as a “quick and dirty guide to brand strategy.”
