# Mastering Moodboards: Great Creative Work | Beginner to Advanced

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GSBjVedREWE

Length: about 23:45

## The main idea

A moodboard is a working conversation tool, not a pretty collage: you add, cut and reorder references with stakeholders until everyone agrees. Oren walks through which apps to use for discovery vs organization, how to board for color, branding, campaigns, product development and photo shoots, and why the person who keeps organized references keeps getting hired.

## Frameworks and steps

- App roles (0:45): Pinterest for its algorithm and “find more ideas for this board” suggestions; Cosmos for a curated feed, a phone plugin that saves reels and TikToks, and search by hex code; Are.na for the highest-quality references since working creatives use it daily. Milanote, Canva and Figma are for organizing, not discovery.
- Bucket boards (3:05): standing boards for recurring themes in your work (his: avant-garde packaging, brand photography), plus one board per brand or project, so every save is intentional.
- Color boarding (4:05): search hex codes in Cosmos, then sort results dark to light, try variations (more saturated, gradients, cream vs white) and cut what the client rejects until the board becomes the color reference tool.
- Vibe boards vs tactical boards (6:45): a vibe board sets the feeling or archetype; tactical boards decide one thing each (typography, layout, product photo, lifestyle photo, logo direction). He favors several small boards over a few big ones.
- Campaign whiteboard (8:40): one big canvas holding task lists, briefs, merch concepts, color, hero-shot refs, set design, social launch refs, scripts and on-screen graphic styles, so a 10 to 12 person squad can see how pieces connect.
- Brand working board (12:50): vibe at left, colorways at bottom, materials in a corner, current work in the middle, future on the right. Label sections when others use it.
- Product development board (14:15): references, factory sample photos, change requests, examples for laces, padding, logo placement sent to the factory, and colorway trials.
- Photo shoot board, five buckets plus one (17:10): setting, action, angle, lighting, post and edit, then behind-the-scenes as the extra.

## Examples and case studies he names

- Nike: said to keep a dedicated color team of dozens, which is why he finds paid color consults reasonable.
- A rebrand built on heavy blue with cream: the board showed whiter vs creamier versions; black got added as a complement used only with cream.
- Vann (agency where he is creative-in-residence): its creative director runs overall vibe boards plus narrow tactical boards in Figma.
- A two-word wordmark logo project: a pure logo board narrowed down with stakeholders, no vibe content on it.
- A sneaker launch campaign in Milanote with a fictional sports team theme and invented sponsors, steel-wall set, spotlight lighting, group hero shots with implied motion.
- Canva: he is moving team whiteboards there to cut per-seat tool costs as his team grows (he notes a separate paid relationship but calls this unrelated).
- Valuable sneakers: a custom two-tone sole would need molds at about 800 dollars per size, so they went single color; blues and pinks would not match across materials.
- A color-pattern designer he met in London who shows up on most streetwear boards he has seen.
- Jerry Lorenzo working in his studio: action beats posing.
- A white-background studio shoot where one 500-dollar rental item is the single differentiator.
- Nude Project: rated top tier for social brand content; plans BTS shot lists and scripted BTS reels as seriously as the shoot itself.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Replace scroll time with Pinterest or Cosmos time and save into named buckets.
- Use Cosmos hex search to test a palette against real imagery before presenting it.
- Run color reviews live: cut, reorder and add on the board while the client reacts.
- Split boards by decision so sign-off happens one choice at a time.
- For shoots, photograph the actual location and pair it with refs of similar spaces; for unfamiliar spots, scout the light and props like poles or vehicles.
- Show the photographer the lighting look, not the method. Let them spec gear.
- Agree on edit expectations and color correction before the shoot.
- Add BTS to every shot list, including scripted BTS concepts.
- When sending factory changes, show three acceptable examples per detail.
- Book a weekly 30 to 45 minute slot (his is Sunday night) to triage bookmarks and pins: delete, file, move to a project board, or act.
- Use organized boards in pitches to show how you run projects.
