# Exposing my brand strategy playbook

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IBk9xLUdnSo

Length: about 24 min

## The main idea

Filmed in Barcelona while he runs a planning workshop for a global company, this is the five part annual brand planning process Oren uses with clients of every size. It moves from products and the calendar to content, channels, threats, and finally an accountability chart that assigns owners and dates. The thread through all of it is turning wishes into decisions with clear yes or no conditions.

## Frameworks and steps

- Part 1, product (0:51): list three things. What you already have ready, what you need to survive and hit goals (need, not want), and a dream road map of realistic collabs, special editions and licensing for where the brand is right now. Keep it all in a spreadsheet with status.
    - Positioning check (1:34): in retail most categories show a good, better, best trio. Good is the low price, usually not the first mover. Better is the standard pick. Best charges more for a better experience. Also look at competitors’ road maps and the consumer problem you solve.
- Part 2, calendar (5:01): pull last year’s sales by week (52 weeks, a method he credits to Taylor Holiday of Common Thread). Mark the 3 or 4 big peaks and the 2 to 5 smaller ones, judge which are repeatable, then find the lulls. Fill the gaps with:
    - Product releases, often by splitting a big seasonal drop into a smaller drop plus a few mini drops in slow weeks (8:37).
    - Dream road map items like collabs, special editions or licensing (9:04).
    - Cultural moments such as Valentine’s Day, back to school, summer break (9:58).
    - An event you invent, online or in person (10:15).
- Part 3, content (11:30):
    - Postmortem of last year judged on sales, interest, followers and fans, not views. Sort each item into repeat, improve or ignore, by network (12:21).
    - Pillars (13:30): aim for four repeatable formats you run about every one to four weeks, and keep asking if each is rising or fading.
    - Release support (14:38): for every release, list the photo, video, social, seeding and UGC it needs.
    - Tests (15:46): what you want to try, with a gate that decides yes, maybe or no.
    - Success metrics (16:10): kill vanity metrics. Tie social to sales, email signups and interest. For retail brands, regional visibility can be a real metric to show buyers.
- Part 4, channels (17:24): map paid DTC, organic DTC, wholesale, your own stores and partners. For any new channel, write the unlock condition (for example a revenue level), then define success per channel: ROAS target, CAC vs lifetime value, units per store per week.
- Part 5, threats and mitigation (19:20): two column whiteboard, the threat and what you can do now.
- The accountability chart (20:25): list every release, content bucket, event and channel. Mark each as have to, want to, or too much, then name who owns it. Assign dates, break vague goals into smaller dated steps, and set a check-in cadence while keeping meetings small.

## Examples and case studies he names

- His Valuables brand: over 100 products released last year across apparel, footwear, shoe bags, belts and car accessories to find product market fit fast; two winners now in contention. Dream collabs are big car makers, but realistic targets this year are car clubs, shops and accessory brands.
- Best Buy, Fnac, MediaMarkt: shelves show the good, better, best trio in nearly every category.
- A goth menswear brand (hypothetical): make a Valentine’s gift kit for the customer’s partner to fill a January and February lull.
- Lift Foils (electric hydrofoils): summer heavy, so winters were planned around it, including pushing southern hemisphere markets.
- Gel Blaster: did a Halo edition through the Licensing Expo in Las Vegas, where brands pay roughly 10 to 12 percent royalty plus a flat fee for things like heavy metal band iconography. Pudgy Penguins were there pitching their own IP.
- Tarte Cosmetics: Tarte Island influencer trips that play out like a reality show on TikTok, a made-up event that drives sales in a slow window.
- His own content: packaging videos were a pillar every two weeks and are now fading. More shoots plus behind the scenes content directly sells more product, so he aims for a shoot a month.
- Canada retail example: proving a million monthly views in Canada to a retailer like London Drugs.
- A friend who took work back from agencies after realizing he could do it better with the freed time.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Print a big 52 week calendar, mark peaks and lulls, and brainstorm one fix per gap: release, cultural moment, or invented event.
- Split large seasonal collections into a core drop plus mini drops in slow weeks.
- Keep a living dream road map sheet for collabs and licensing, scaled to your current size.
- Run a content postmortem with repeat, improve, ignore, and be comfortable ignoring channels.
- Write gates for every test and new channel: “we do it when X is true.”
- Make threat planning concrete: for a creator-led brand, get more voices on the product and build ads that reach people who don’t know the founder.
- “Who’s going to do it” is the step most teams skip; assign an owner and date to everything.
- Break hard goals like launching TikTok Shop into small dated steps (SKU and compliance info first).
