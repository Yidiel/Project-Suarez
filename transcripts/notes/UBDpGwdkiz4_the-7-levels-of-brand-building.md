# The 7 Levels of Brand Building

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UBDpGwdkiz4

Length: about 19 min

## The main idea

Oren lays out seven stages a brand moves through, from finding the first channel that makes money to staying relevant once it is large. The point is diagnostic: founders who feel stuck have often skipped a stage, and creatives and marketers can see which stage their skills help most. He stresses that some stages take years and that rushing to scale without a foundation is how brands sink.

## Frameworks and steps

- Stage 1, one channel that works (0:31): pick a single organic or paid strategy and get it profitable before anything else. Brand kits and art direction come later. That channel funds the next stage and proves the product. If you have spread out without a profitable channel, pull back.
- Stage 2, foundation (2:54): build the systems that multiply later effort. Landing pages tailored to each campaign, influencer and customer type; email and SMS capture with a clear signup offer; automated education and post-purchase flows so every new customer gets nurtured without extra work.
- Stage 3, multiplier (5:08): push what already works into more places before making anything new. Repurpose organic posts across platforms, move proven Meta ads to TikTok, feed social content into email. Slight edits are fine.
- Stage 4, crossover (9:35): add the missing half, so organic brands add paid and paid brands add organic or marketplaces. Pick channels that lift each other, set the metrics the team reports weekly, and define who owns what. He calls this the stage he has worked in most, and it can last years.
- Stage 5, secret sauce (12:30): the one signature thing that gives escape velocity. It can be a viral series, a near perfect funnel, a breakthrough product, a big collab, or deep expertise in one discipline. Expect to take a few expensive shots.
- Stage 6, scale (14:46): with the signature in place, go full throttle on every channel while keeping culture, metrics and roles intact. Most brands live here for years.
- Stage 7, staying interesting (16:13): at size you compete with leaner challenger brands. You need ongoing methods to keep attention and avoid getting slow.

## Examples and case studies he names

- David protein bars: found their first channel in TikTok Shop through creator commissions, helped by a clear product edge (most protein, fewest calories and sugar). That also gave lift going into retail.
- Bad Ombres (Arizona frozen burritos): pure organic Instagram, telling the founding couple’s story through carousels, video and stories; drops sell out repeatedly.
- Collab data pulled from Particle (sponsor), showing collabs serve different goals:
    - Malbon Golf x Neighborhood: overlapping fan bases, tiny runs (about 100 shirts, 60 golf bags), sold out in days. Awareness for both, not volume.
    - Kith x Giorgio Armani: very few units sold online. Kith paid to sit next to the Armani logo; it was brand building to sell everything else.
    - SKIMS x Roberto Cavalli: the cheapest way into the Cavalli world, thousands of swimsuits sold. Brand lift and real revenue because SKIMS already owns the audience.
    - Khy (Kylie Jenner) with young designers: runs of 100 to 160 units, exposure for both sides. Also a reminder that small production runs are normal.
- Hollow alpaca socks: their secret sauce is Meta ads execution, running every format (podcast clips, AI hooks, UGC, statics) at huge volume.
- Cozy Earth: an influencer event challenge covered heavily on TikTok that went viral and became a repeatable format.
- Represent: the founder’s personal brand on YouTube and Instagram became the signature.
- Ridge Wallet: big giveaways and street interviews, plus bringing a major YouTuber into the company.
- SKIMS (stage 7): product as marketing through attention grabbing concept items, plus a relentless pace of campaigns and collabs with a wide range of faces.
- His own womenswear brand: six plus years, with traction only in the last few.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Before adding a channel, ask whether one channel is actually profitable yet.
- Build separate landing pages per campaign, influencer and audience segment rather than sending everyone to the homepage.
- Decide what the email signup offer is (discount, guide) and test where the capture appears.
- Set up automated welcome and education flows early so they scale for free.
- Audit current assets for reuse on other platforms before briefing new creative.
- When planning a collab, name the goal first: sell out to a shared fan base, borrow prestige, or sell volume at an accessible price.
- Use a sales intelligence tool to check real unit volumes before deciding production runs.
- At scale, use odd concept products and a high cadence of campaigns to stay in the feed.
- For creatives: figure out which stage a client is in and pitch the work that stage needs.
