# How to Make Money as a Creative in 2026 (breakdown and advice)

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kl4DLGKK_VI

Length: about 18.5 min

## The main idea

Marketing has collapsed into content, and the best paid creative role right now is the creative strategist: someone with one strong craft who can also plan, test and measure content across organic and paid. Single-skill creatives are getting squeezed, while people who pair a craft with critical thinking, KPIs and funnel knowledge move up toward creative director. He frames this as the gap between an older leadership generation that doesn’t get social and a younger one that lacks the experience to act on it.

## Frameworks and steps

- Two income models (1:32): a non-creator has salary plus a side hustle that needs traditional marketing. A creator stacks salary, company creator bounties, platform payouts, affiliate (TikTok Shop), brand deals and a services pipeline, so social savvy makes creatives more self-reliant.
- Why creative work is broken (3:08): a generation gap kills on-the-job mentorship, and a decade of avoiding opinions means many people never built and defended taste. Creative quality comes down to whether it sells or moves a goal.
- Three tiers of business content (5:20): companies that barely post (losing), companies that post the basics but not well (most of them), and optimized content across influencers, owned social, paid and culture. The opportunity is moving people from the middle tier to the top.
- Marketing budget flipped into content budget (7:43): events, retail and shoots are now judged by the content they produce, with paid ads as a smaller slice that also runs on content.
- Skill stacking (8:12): one hard-to-replace primary craft, plus easy secondary skills (AI writing tools, Shopify back end) that now take weeks instead of years.
- Strategist to creative director chart, organic side (9:14):
    - Pillar strategy: four content types, each with a set style, tested at once and reviewed monthly as keep, improve or kill.
    - Improvement framework for weak pillars: change format, write better hooks.
    - Shoot execution: research, briefing or scripting, logistics checklist, budget.
    - Creative network: UGC creators, designers and editors at different price points, influencers.
    - Competent at this equals strategist; mastery equals creative director.
- Paid side (10:44): briefing concepts that run as ads, producing volume and style variety. Some brands win on paid alone, and it applies to local lead gen and software too.
- TikTok Shop levels (11:15): recruit affiliates, open applications, stock the store, set bundles, turn the best creator content into ads, test on other networks, go live. At scale a whole team briefs and tracks creators. These jobs barely existed a few years ago.
- AI sorted into two columns (12:49): low value is replacing humans in shoots, AI-written viral scripts, faceless accounts. High value is AI hook variants for ad testing, story ads only AI could make, world building and visual tone, retouching and post, and ideation on angles.
- Four-step route up (15:26): get great at skill A, get somewhere you can test organic then paid live, get busy enough to build a bias for action, build a saved and organized reference toolkit.

## Examples and case studies he names

- Alta: pays employees creator bounties, an example of companies paying staff to be creators.
- Zach and his app Aura: AI used for world building and visual tone, his model of useful AI.
- Cut 30 (his program): his view across hundreds of creators and brands is why he claims a wide read on organic and paid.
- Tatum: sports marketing agency that filled her pipeline by publicly breaking down other people’s strategies.
- Tales: founder went viral on TikTok, stopped posting organic, then moved those learnings into a strong paid content strategy.
- Jasmine: runs a TikTok Shop with founder content, brand content and affiliates, and learns content as a founder skill.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Run four content pillars at a time and do a monthly keep, improve or kill review.
- Attach a KPI to every creative decision so you can argue good or bad with numbers.
- Treat events and retail activations as content shoots first.
- Use AI for hook variations on proven ads, not for replacing talent.
- Build a creative network list with tiered price points for editors, designers and UGC talent.
- Keep a swipe file of ads and organic posts, sorted by what worked, and bring it to pitches and interviews as proof.
- Teach in public: breaking down others’ strategies can act as lead gen (the Tatum model).
- The whole thesis in his words: “Only the critical thinkers survive.”
