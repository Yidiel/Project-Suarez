# How to Name Your Brand (AVOID this deadly mistake for your business)

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=konbTRiJ39I

Length: about 18.5 min

## The main idea

A name has to carry feeling, but in an era when most discovery happens through people talking about you online, it also has to be easy to say, spell and search. The “deadly mistake” is picking a name people can’t pronounce, spell or find on Google, which quietly throws away earned media. He then covers the practical side: search competition, trademark checks, domains and handles, using his own brand Valuable as the worked example.

## Frameworks and steps

- Why names matter (0:25): naming firm Lexicon’s renames show how much a name can change a brand’s ceiling.
- Check 1, feel and connotation (2:30): is it memorable and does it have a vibe? Do an emotion word dump: pick the feeling (professional, counterculture, etc.), list every adjective and sound around it, and borrow whole words or fragments.
- Check 2, say it and spell it (3:08): if someone hears it, can they pronounce it, and if they hear it, can they type it into Google or Amazon? If not, you lose earned media value, the free exposure from people talking about you that would otherwise cost ad money (3:57).
- Check 3, the Google test (5:11): can you actually rank for it? Search the name first. If an established brand with high domain authority already owns it, even in another category, you end up paying for clicks on your own name forever. Tools: Ahrefs, Semrush.
- Category-as-name tradeoff (6:39): naming yourself after the product category can let you own the category search if you are first, at the cost of some later confusion.
- Positioning angles in names (11:09): be the cheapest (value), the most expensive (experience), or the counterculture alternative. Counterculture names are often two blunt words that cut through.
- Check 4, trademark (12:20, he notes he is not a lawyer): search the USPTO’s free database (TESS; the UK has its own). Look at live and dead marks, focus on word marks in your class or a nearby class, and remember that someone selling under the name without registering may still have rights through use in commerce. Bring a short list of conflicts to a lawyer to save billable hours.
- Check 5, domains (14:16): .com first, then .co or .net; other extensions are about equal and weaker. Adding a word is fine, but add it at the end, never the front, so email, URL and search all start with the brand word.
- Check 6, social handles (15:31): keep them unified with the domain and starting with the brand word.

## Examples and case studies he names

- Lexicon renames: Marquis to Impossible (Impossible Burger), Rincon Networks to Sonos, ProMop to Swiffer, an earlier email device name to BlackBerry. Sonos felt minimal and modern next to older audio names; BlackBerry gave a tech product prestige.
- Aesop: commonly mispronounced, which hurts searchability for a new brand even if Aesop itself has been fine.
- Buku (French for plenty), a brand from a past job: fun but nearly impossible for shoppers to spell or search.
- Chlorophyll Water: he spent about six months convincing the founder to build SEO before an influencer seeding blitz, so all the social buzz led to the brand on Google. They reached number two at launch, then number one, and the search data also helped retail pitches.
- Gel Blaster: brand name equals product and category name, same tradeoff.
- Creatine gummies (hypothetical): owning category search becomes leverage when pitching retailers, paired with a dealer locator.
- Hampton (founder community): borrows the luxury of the Hamptons plus a real personal backstory from the founder.
- Goop: playful and a little unserious, the opposite of rigid health media.
- Ten Thousand running gear: he likes spelled-out numbers despite weaker SEO.
- Counterculture names: Death Wish Coffee, Black Rifle Coffee, Liquid Death, Tapout, Kill Crew, Hellstar.
- His brand Valuable: the word implies the item and the wearer have worth, celebrates uncelebrated skill sets, and plays with luxury and resale value. Uses valuablestudios.com plus matching handles, trademark pending.
- Boy Smells, Girl Skateboards, Beats by Dre: names that stand out in crowded spaces or attach a common word to a person to make it ownable.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Before falling for a name, say it out loud to someone and have them type it into Google.
- Run an Ahrefs or Semrush check on who ranks for the name now.
- If you launch with heavy influencer seeding, build SEO first so buzz converts.
- Search the trademark database yourself and bring only the real conflicts to a lawyer.
- Use Namecheap and domain combo tools to check availability; use a handle search tool to check socials in one pass.
- Put any extra word after the brand word in domains and handles.
- Use a hidden personal backstory to strengthen a name that also borrows an obvious association.
- One line test: “don’t underestimate” how little effort a consumer will spend spelling your name.
