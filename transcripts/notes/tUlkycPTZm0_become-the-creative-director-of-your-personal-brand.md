# Become the creative director of your personal brand

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUlkycPTZm0

Length: about 22 min

## The main idea

Creative direction has moved from fashion houses and agencies, through the streetwear-collab era, into a personality era. The brand account matters less than the world of people around it: founder, team, creators, influencers and customers. The same craft that once went into campaigns and retail (sets, design systems, styling, world building, launch arcs) now needs to be applied to personal brands and the people who represent a brand. Filmed right after Cannes Lions, where creators were the main topic.

## Frameworks and steps

- Three eras of creative direction (1:34). 1.0: fashion and agency, where CD was an agency differentiator. 2.0: the Virgil era, when Hypebeast-style culture made CD an aspirational job and big collabs became normal. 3.0: now. The title is diluted, but demand is higher than ever, including PE and VC firms hiring CDs across their portfolios.
- Three roles that get confused (5:05). Creative strategy: weekly concepts for ads or organic, briefs, analytics. Content operations: strategy plus scheduling, vendor management, and sending and receiving briefs. Full creative direction: all of the above plus owning the aesthetics, through lines across campaigns, what the brand stands for, and the world for collaborators, retail and merch.
- The personality era (7:05): the account is no longer the core driver; the world is. Leading ad marketers suggest 30%+ of a Meta ad account run as whitelisted ads from creators’ handles.
- Creative direction as enablement (8:59): design the mailers, budgets and settings that make creators’ content better and more likely to get posted.
- Personal brand 1.0 vs 2.0 (10:42): 1.0 was attention and lifestyle. 2.0 is career growth, money and learning; creators now fill in for missing mentors and teachers.
- Ideation framework (12:25): recurring topics, recurring formats, and stories about events, people and products.
- Biweekly planning meeting (13:33): talk it through out loud, never alone. Steps: (1) niche news, like it or not, opinion or tactic; (2) personal history, meaning the camera roll since the last meeting, then one, three and five years back; (3) outlier videos from a database by topic; (4) ask Meta AI for formats suited to your handle. Then sort the ideas into priority, middle and back burner.
- Distribution as the new retail (16:37): brand CDs had stores, runways and trade booths. Personal brands have every social network plus events and merch.
- The sauce, five levers (17:26): sets, visuals and lighting as recurring story elements; design (colors, fonts, series title lockups, animated bumpers, a brand kit); styling (what people wear and what it signals); world building (collabs, interviews, shared houses and events with other creators); launches run like campaigns (tease, unveil, launch, sustain).
- Breaking in (20:18): have a core craft first (design, editing, copy), add strategy and briefing, then practice CD on personal brands, where the trust bar is lower than at a brand. Paid examples: $1.5K to $5K a month.

## Examples and case studies he names

- His early agency work with Red Bull, Vice and Grey Goose at the Hamptons, the Super Bowl and Sundance: creative direction as a small agency’s edge.
- Travis Scott x McDonald’s, Adidas collabs, Virgil at Louis Vuitton: era 2.0 opened the field up.
- 3DR drone launch film: an in-house CD trained real apes to fly the drone, with 2001: A Space Odyssey references. It went viral for a hardware company.
- His CD role at a beauty PE firm: creative strategy across portfolio brands, including creator mailers whose design raised posting rates.
- A brand gave each of 100 creators about $400 for a luxury hotel room to raise the look of a campaign; renting studios and gear and sending creators on trips work the same way.
- Notion, ChatGPT and Shopify running whitelisted ads from creator accounts.
- Stussy and Gentle Monster: people who became the face of the brand, early on mailers and world building.
- Marty Supreme merch, and Pharrell’s Humanrace with Adidas (a smoothie store): retail-style world building.
- His Cannes villa with Darkroom: orange-branded hats, matches and towels around a 60s/70s murder-mystery theme.
- Monthly collabs (an Italian villa investment creator from Cut30, Bayma Williams, Hans Laurier) and a shared creator house at Cannes.
- His one-week content ops bootcamp for 50 people sold out fast: proof of demand for this skill set.

## Tactics worth stealing

- In your bio or pitch, say which of the three roles you actually do (strategy, ops or full CD). The title alone means little now.
- For any brand, map the world (founder, staff, creators, customers) and art direct their content, not just the brand feed.
- Design creator mailers to be filmable. Posting rate is the KPI.
- Fund upgrades to setting (a hotel room, a studio, a trip) so a whole creator wave shares one elevated look.
- Run a biweekly ideation call with an AI notetaker (he uses Granola), then ask the AI to turn it into a tiered hook and script list.
- Dig through the camera roll at one, three and five years back for origin stories.
- LinkedIn’s desktop news module gives a small algorithm boost to the topics it features.
- Give each recurring series its own title lockup and animation.
- Treat clothing as a deliberate signal: cleaned up, full glam, or purposely candid.
- Give events and trips their own mini brand kit and merch.
