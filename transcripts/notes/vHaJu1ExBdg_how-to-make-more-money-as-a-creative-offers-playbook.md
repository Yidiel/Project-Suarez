# How to make more money as a creative (offers playbook)

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vHaJu1ExBdg

Length: about 18.5 min

## The main idea

Creatives tend to feel guilty about money, partly because agencies and older anti-sellout culture trained them to, yet demand for creative work and the ability to measure its sales impact have never been higher. Oren’s fix is basic service-business structure: a productized offer, a funnel, a video sales letter, offer stacking and a CRM. He closes with a Framer walkthrough (the sponsor) for building the site that sells it.

## Frameworks and steps

- Why creatives undercharge (1:24): companies now see people who understand the internet and acquire customers as some of their most valuable hires, yet those people anchor to their last salary. Big holding company agencies keep creatives in narrow silos priced by billable hours, with profit flowing upward (2:03).
- Creative work and money, historically (2:46): design and writing grew up supporting commerce and banking, artists relied on patrons, and the 70s to 90s treated selling out as a sin. Now Meta ads, UTM tracking and TikTok Shop let creatives prove the dollars their work makes (3:55).
- Three gaps (5:02): learning how business works, actually applying it, and being willing to sell. Willingness comes first. Charging more also funds other creatives: a bigger shoot budget mostly goes to hiring more people and making better work (5:31).
- The offer (6:26): fill in the template I do X for Y at Z: one productized service for a niche at a set price and timeline. Put pricing on the site so buyers don’t have to book a call to find out. You can still sell more once you’re talking.
- Better, faster, cheaper (9:01): stand out on one, ideally two, never all three. Be honest about whether you’re actually the best. If you’re newer, cheaper is a fair way to get reps. Speed is now a strong selling point, see sprint offers.
- The funnel (11:30): a site should move a visitor into your world through calls to book, downloads or waitlists, then sell over time via more pages and email.
- VSL, video sales letter (12:01): some people read, others watch. A video on the offer page covering the buyer’s problem, your background, how you close the gap, and how to buy lifts conversion. Writing these for clients is a sellable skill too.
- Offer stacking (13:20): combine skills into one offer worth more than the parts. Applies at the top end as well, for example a strategist who also buys media.
- CRM (14:50): keep every lead, signup and non-buyer somewhere so you can keep talking to them and sell new things.
- Framer build (15:29): start from a template, swap in your own content and messaging, and ship.

## Examples and case studies he names

- Kevin O’Leary clip: social media went from an intern task to some of the highest value roles in a company.
- Omnicom, Saatchi, Havas: large agencies that silo art directors, copywriters and strategists.
- The book Capslock: on design’s roots in finance and commerce.
- Reel Big Fish, “Sell Out”: stands in for the 90s anti-commercial stance.
- Brand identity offer posted on X: brand in four weeks for $15K, strategy for $5K, with a sample Figma deliverable linked on the page.
- B2B SaaS studio: brand plus website, $10K, three to four weeks, niche only.
- Dmitri’s sprint: $5K for 10 icons, 5 illustrations and 2 to 3 UI screens, fixed scope and timeline.
- His own past side business: corporate websites with a six week process that included messaging, not the best sites but solving the copy pain point at a fair price. Now he only does creative strategy for the biggest brands at a premium.
- His landing page designer (found on Contra): sells one thing, single landing pages in Framer, and won’t take maintenance work. That focus is why Oren keeps coming back.
- His own Framer page: an 11 minute VSL sits in the middle.
- Eddie, his former videographer: stacked shooting, Meta ads and VSL scripting into one lead-gen ad offer for service businesses.
- Videographers turned creators who pitch global brand ads with $50K to $500K budgets because they know what works online.
- Framer templates: Extract (simple dark mode), Artemis (portfolio), Julio (SaaS landing page), a paid Swiss-style portfolio he used.

## Tactics worth stealing

- Write your offer as one line: deliverable, audience, price, timeline.
- Publish prices and a sample deliverable (a clickable Figma file) on the offer page.
- Pick one of better, faster, cheaper to lead with, and say it on the page.
- Package a fixed-scope sprint with set quantities and a set turnaround.
- Record a VSL for your main offer: problem, credentials, approach, how to buy.
- Give every page a next step (book a call, download, waitlist) and capture leads in a CRM.
- Add one adjacent skill (media buying, scripting) and sell the bundle.
- Use what you learn from building your own offers to advise clients on theirs.
- Framing worth keeping: “Never feel bad asking for what you’re worth.”
