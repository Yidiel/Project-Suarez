# What It’s Actually Like Running a World Class Creative Studio

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z08CfQZE6TI

Length: about 32 min

## The main idea

Oren tours the Los Angeles studio of his friend Victor Solomon, the artist who makes the NBA, WNBA and G League trophies along with Nike and brand collaborations. Solomon’s path ran from personal art about basketball, to a league partnership, to a studio built around “aspirational objects.” The lessons: stories baked into every detail, self-funded proof-of-concept projects that show clients what you can do, and owning a narrow niche until the rest falls into place around it.

## Frameworks and steps

- Aspirational objects as a brand world (1:27): collectible fine art, league trophies and luxury product all sit under one thesis, which is everyday sports equipment rebuilt in luxury materials. That idea came from his first pieces.
- Trophy program strategy (4:48): each NBA trophy is named after a historic player, which turns the awards into tributes and gives every design a story to start from.
- Narrative Easter eggs (7:06): every dimension and count maps to the namesake or event, so the object reads as intentional and earned.
- Concept to production pipeline (8:00):
    - Pencil sketches and back-and-forth “scratch sessions” with the client lead, pulling from the namesake’s career milestones.
    - A refined illustration for wider stakeholder buy-in.
    - Full 3D model to spec, sometimes 3D printed to test how it feels in hand.
    - Components split across specialist vendors (bronze foundry, base maker, CNC metal shop).
    - Final assembly in his studio. He personally touches every piece.
- Learning by doing (9:27): he faked his way through the first trophy program, sourcing partners and learning their constraints early so designs suited casting.
- Timeline as a quality stance (10:43): clients often ask for a trophy a week out. He aims for three to six months and leaves rush catalog work to other vendors.
- Naivety as a strength (20:21): not knowing what was “impossible” let him pitch bolder ideas, and Nike’s team pushed him to go further.
- Proof of concept over pitch decks (23:19): nobody funds your first try in a new category, so self-fund a version 1, release it, and let clients react to something real.
- Process transfers (25:12): once you have taken anything from blank page to finished product, the method carries over to watches, fragrance or software.
- Go narrow and deep (30:14): with AI tools and overseas factories open to everyone, picking the niche matters nearly as much as executing it. Niches are bigger than they look.

## Examples and case studies he names

- Stained glass backboard (13:42): a film school dropout’s hobby piece went viral on social, which led to a gallery, Art Basel, a New York show, and NBA staff walking by.
- Michael Jordan NBA MVP trophy (4:10): built with MJ’s team. The form rises from raw to refined, ending in a 23-facet ball. Its height references his number and championships, and five base sides stand for his MVPs. Jordan asked to leave his likeness off so it wouldn’t overshadow the winner.
- NBA, WNBA and G League trophy walls, plus All-Star rings made for the league.
- Nike collaboration (17:46): Nike walked in with sneakers and materials and gave him an open brief on the Air Force 1, Air Max 90 and Foamposite. Results included gold chain laces, Swarovski crystal swooshes, a paisley inlay, and swoosh-shaped chain links in a jewelry line. He jokes the swoosh is a hard logo to design with.
- Michelob Ultra World Cup player-of-the-match trophy (26:26): a 26-degree face for 2026, anodized aluminum as a nod to the brand’s cans, three color layers for Canada, the US and Mexico, 104 facets for the matches, 16 inches tall for the host cities. Michelob had regional celebrities hand out the trophies, so each match became a moment, and fans even made AI images of themselves presenting it.
- Earlier Michelob Ultra championship bottles for the Thunder’s locker room celebration.
- Bose earbuds collaboration, mentioned as another brand partner.
- Orson Welles and Citizen Kane: the model for treating inexperience as freedom.
- In-house side projects: a fragrance (organic, possibly unisex, packaging-led) and a watch, made out of his own curiosity.
- Oren’s own hat: it sold poorly to the public, but several brands asked for that exact item, proof that clients often need to see something before they know they want it.
- Mid-video sponsor segment: Framer AI building new pages from a purchased template’s visual DNA (11:42).

## Tactics worth stealing

- Name a program’s pieces after people or stories so every brief begins with meaning.
- Hide numeric and material references tied to the honoree or event. They pay off in press and fan lore.
- Win buy-in in stages: rough sketch with your main contact, polished illustration for the group, then 3D.
- 3D print prototypes to check feel before committing to production.
- Talk to fabricators early so the design suits their process.
- Set and defend a realistic minimum timeline for premium work.
- Self-fund small releases in categories you want to be hired for, and post the experiments publicly.
- Activation idea from Michelob: local celebrities handing off a recurring award builds anticipation across a tournament.
- Solomon’s core line: “No one’s going to give you your first opportunity with a budget.”
